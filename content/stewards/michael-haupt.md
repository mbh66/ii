Michael Haupt, born in 1966, is the eldest of the three Haupt siblings considering joining Isikova & Ironwood as land stewards [MS]. He has lived in Greyton, in the Valley of Grace, since 2013 [MS]. There he convenes the [Ward 2 Development Forum](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za) [IC] and writes the public sites that record the valley's people, history and rules [MS].

## Building systems on six continents

He started in 1988 as a COBOL programmer at Coopers & Lybrand in Johannesburg, writing the first electronic billing system for the Witwatersrand Regional Services Council [MS]. At Teljoy he led one of the teams that played a crucial role in launching mobile telephony in time for South Africa's first democratic elections in April 1994 [MS]. In 1998 he directed the deployment of Iridium's billing system across Asia Pacific, North and South America, as the first global handheld satellite telephone network went live [MS]. From 1999 to 2004 he led billing and business transformation for telecoms carriers and banking systems from Sydney, Cambridge, Edinburgh, Madrid and Taipei [MS]. He has lived in sixteen cities on six continents [MS].

Michael describes those years as his grounding in thinking about whole systems at a global scale while keeping the local account in view [MS].

## Stepping outside

In July 2004 he left that career and spent two and a half years in Thailand and Malaysia studying neuroscience, monetary history, evolutionary biology, complexity science, and consciousness research [MS]. The question he kept asking was what happens to the systems a worldview has built... **once that worldview reaches its limit** [MS]. He was heavily influenced by Donella Meadows's *[The Limits to Growth](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/sources/meadows-limits-to-growth)* during that time.

He then ran Striata's European operations from London until 2010, when he returned to South Africa [MS]. In 2014 he stepped away from corporate work a second time to raise his daughter on his own [MS]. 

Since 2014 Michael has been developing the idea of the BioConomy, and in 2026 he published the first writing on it, on the [BioConomy wiki](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth) [MS]. Where the Economy measures value by what flows *through* a place, the [BioConomy](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/concepts/economy-versus-bioconomy) measures value by what *remains* in place, and treats the bioregion itself as the thing being managed [MS]. His current work shows rural communities how they can take part in performance-based nature bonds, starting with the [[institutions/greater-cape-town-water-fund|Greater Cape Town Water Fund]] [MS].

## Recording a place

In Greyton he convened the Greyton Business Forum in 2013, which disbanded in 2015 [MS]. On 16 September 2026 he convened the first meeting of the Ward 2 Development Forum in Genadendal, together with Theewaterskloof Municipality's Economic Development department [IC]. His [contributor page on the W2DF register](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/contributors/michael-haupt) covers that work in more detail.

Alongside the Forum he has built a network of public sites, written in plain language for residents and for AI agents [MS]:

- The [BioConomy wiki](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth) sets out the vocabulary, frameworks and templates a BioHub uses to coordinate work across a bioregion [MS].
- The [Ward 2 Development Forum register](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za) lists the ward's initiatives and holds the valley's history and the laws that apply there [MS].
- The [Genadendal Diaries repository](https://github.com/mbh66/genadendal-diaries) holds four of the Genadendal mission diaries as tables and plain text, with new English translations [MS].
- The [BioHubs directory](https://biohubs.bioconomy.earth) and the [High School Decision Tool](https://hs.valleyofgrace.co.za) apply the same method to the valley's BioHub and to families choosing a high school [MS].

Michael treats this body of work as a record of how he captures the essence of a place, from the words it uses to the people working in it [MS]. This site applies the same practice to Isikova & Ironwood [MS].

## Coming to this land

In October 2026 he camped on the farm with his siblings and saw for himself the land burnt in the 2025 fire [MS]. [[stewards/index|The Family's Story]] describes how his journey meets his sister's and brother's.

## What he would bring to the land

Whether the family joins is the company's decision. The family's proposal will be set out under [[proposals/index|Proposals]] once it has been put to the company. If the company agrees, Michael would bring his work on how land can hold its water, and on how that work can be measured and paid for.

### Water Retention Landscapes

A Water Retention Landscape is land shaped and planted so that rain soaks into the soil and reaches streams slowly over weeks, where on bare or compacted soil it would run off within hours and take topsoil with it. The [BioConomy wiki](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/concepts/water-retention-landscapes) describes the idea in full.

In October 2026 Michael and his siblings searched the published research on water-holding earthworks in fynbos and renosterveld, and found no study in the Cape Floristic Region that tests them [MS]. [[research/water-retention-evidence|Evidence on Water and Soil in Fynbos and Renosterveld]] sets out what they did find. Because that evidence is missing, the proposals below start with a record of the land and small trials that are measured from the first day. Michael would bring the design of those trials and the work of keeping their record, as he has kept the record of the Valley of Grace.

### The link to the Cape Water Bond

The [[the-land/cape-water-bond|Cape Water Performance-Based Bond]], listed on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange in April 2026, pays for clearing invasive plants from the catchments that supply Cape Town [IC]. Part of what its investors earn depends on independently verified results [IC]. [[the-land/langeberg-water-source-area|The Langeberg]] is one of South Africa's 22 strategic water source areas [IC]. Nobody knows yet whether a later bond will cover it.

Michael's current work shows rural communities how they can take part in performance-based nature bonds of this kind [MS]. The [BioConomy wiki](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/concepts/performance-based-water-bonds) explains how such a bond ties its return to verified results. A farm that has measured its own water from the start would have evidence to offer if a bond reaches the Langeberg. Michael would bring the methods for that measurement and his working knowledge of how the bonds verify results.

### Proposals written and not yet tabled

Michael and his siblings have written these proposals for the land in October 2026 [MS]. None has yet been put to the company [MS]. Each would go to the company through its Community Management Panel.

- [[proposals/first-five-years-on-the-land|The First Five Years on the Land]]: record the land and run small measured trials of water-holding work while the community settles, with a university as partner.
- [[proposals/whole-farm-water-retention|Water Retention Across the Whole Farm]]: five ways to lay out water-holding work across the farm, with some valleys left untreated for comparison.
- [[proposals/homes-in-a-water-retention-landscape|Homes in a Water Retention Landscape]]: the land around the homes laid out as a Water Retention Landscape, with ponds stepping down from the pond by the cabin.
- [[proposals/lidar-survey-of-the-farm|A LiDAR Survey of the Farm]]: a drone survey of the ground while the fynbos is low after the 2025 fire, to map slope and water flow before anything is built.
- [[the-houses/index|The Houses]]: draft layouts for where the homes and the Main Dwelling could stand.

On 19 September 2024 the Eden Joint Planning Tribunal approved an application to allow six Additional Dwelling Units on the farm, on top of its main dwelling [IC]. Hessequa Municipality gave notice of the decision in a letter from its Director of Development Planning dated 1 October 2024 [IC]. The approval caps the farm at seven homes and sets conditions on how they are planned, sited and built [IC].

The letters in square brackets show where each fact comes from. [IC] means it comes from an official record, in this case the municipality's letter. [[about|About This Site]] explains the tags.

## In short

- The farm may have seven homes in total: one main dwelling and six additional units [IC].
- Each additional unit may have up to 200 m² of floor area and stand up to 6.5 metres high [IC].
- The approval lapses if it is not used within five years, and all the additional units must be built within five years, or before about September 2029 [IC].
- Before building plans are approved, the owner must produce an environmental management plan linked to a stewardship agreement with CapeNature, and obtain the Overberg Water Board's comment on the water-sharing arrangement [IC].
- The units may not be sold off separately by any means, including share block, share title, sectional title or subdivision [IC].

## The decision

The application was for a "consent use", which is permission for a use that the zoning allows only with the municipality's consent [IC]. It was made under section 15(2)(o) of the Hessequa Municipality Land Use Planning By-law, 2015, and approved under section 60 of that by-law, with conditions under section 66 [IC]. The approval covers only the use applied for, and all other requirements of the municipality's zoning scheme still apply [IC].

## Conditions

### Size and timing

- No more than seven units may be built: one main dwelling and six additional units [IC].
- Each additional unit may have a floor area of up to 200 m² and a height of up to 6.5 metres [IC].
- The approval lapses if it is not used within five years of the date of approval [IC].
- Building plans for all the additional units must be submitted, and all the units built, within those five years [IC].

### Site development plan

A site development plan must reach the municipality before or together with the building plans [IC]. It must show the footprint of each unit, its position relative to the existing buildings, and the arrangements for services, access, parking, signs and landscaping [IC]. It must also show [IC]:

- how the clearing and the units will be screened from neighbouring properties, from the R322 and N2, and from the nature reserves within view;
- how light and noise will be kept from neighbours and the wider countryside;
- the buildings on neighbouring properties, the contours and the sightlines, with the units grouped in clusters.

The letter adds that all buildings, homes included, should stand in a small number of clusters, on ground that has already been disturbed near the farm homestead, with landscaping and screening [IC]. Planting around the farmstead should build on what grows there already, such as indigenous vegetation, and use water-wise methods [IC]. Buildings, sheds, netting, tunnels and cleared ground should all suit the area's tourism routes in their size, form and colour [IC].

### Environment and fire

Before building plans are approved, the owner must compile an environmental conservation management plan [IC]. It must include a fire management plan and a plan to control and remove invasive alien plants, address the recommendations of a botanical assessment of the farm dated 8 November 2021, and be linked to a stewardship agreement with CapeNature [IC]. It is drawn up after consulting the municipality's environmental affairs department [IC].

### Access and water

- Before the site development plan is approved, the owner must prove that access servitudes are secured over every farm portion between the site and the provincial road, by servitude or by an agreement noted against the title deeds [IC].
- Before building plans are approved, the owner must obtain comments from the Overberg Water Board supporting the water-sharing arrangement for the new homes [IC].

## Other points the owner must note

- Units may be built only where they will do no harm to the natural environment and have little effect on the farm's agricultural potential [IC].
- The sketch plans in the application give no rights. Full site development plans and building plans must still be submitted under the municipality's rules and the National Building Regulations [IC].
- The property's rates will be adjusted once the development is complete and the property has been revalued [IC].
- The additional units may not be alienated by property share, share block, share title, sectional title, subdivision or any other means [IC].
- Internal roads may be no wider than 4 metres, and environmental authorisation must be obtained if needed [IC].
- Sewage must go to septic tanks installed so that they pose no environmental risk [IC].
- Homes must be painted or finished in natural, earthy colours. Roofs must be a colour that blends with the surroundings, such as dark green, dark brown, grey or black [IC].
- Nothing may be built in highly sensitive indigenous vegetation [IC].
- No native tree may be pruned, covered, felled, removed or disturbed without written approval from the national forestry department, under the National Forests Act of 1998 [IC].
- Every unit must include water and energy saving measures, such as energy-saving lighting, dual-flush toilets, low-flow showerheads and rainwater tanks fed from the roofs [IC].
- The design and siting should follow green-building criteria [IC].
- Waste must be managed by reducing, recycling and re-using it where possible [IC].
- The layout must include sustainable drainage, to keep stormwater clean and reduce flooding downstream [IC].
- The approval does not excuse the owner from any other law. It takes effect only once building plans are approved [IC].

## Why the Tribunal approved it

- The application was based on agricultural use of the land, sheep grazing and permaculture, and on the need to house the people involved in that use while protecting the farm's rural character [IC].
- The application stated that the use is not tourism and that no indigenous plants will be harvested. The provincial environment department's response relied on that statement [IC].
- The disturbed footprint is about 4,507 m², a small fraction of the farm [IC].
- The Western Cape Rural Areas Guideline allows up to five units in addition to a main dwelling on land zoned Agricultural Zone 1. In the absence of a local rural development strategy, the Tribunal allowed six, which it found consistent with the Hessequa zoning scheme by-law [IC].
- The Tribunal found the development fits the character of the area and would strengthen the local economy, provided the conditions are met [IC].
- Two protected areas lie within five kilometres: the Boosmansbos Wilderness Area, declared in 1978 and part of the Cape Floral Region World Heritage Site since 2009, and the Hessequa Hills Private Nature Reserve, declared in 1999 [IC]. In addition, a private individual has established a 615-hectare Critical Biodiversity Area that connects with the Boosmansbos Wilderness Area. This land now forms the core of the Wilderness Nature Reserve, which was officially declared a Provincial Nature Reserve in July 2025, spanning over 643 hectares [IC]. 
- Part of the footprint lies in an area of medium-high botanical sensitivity, which is why the approval requires the environmental management plan and the CapeNature stewardship agreement [IC].
- The Tribunal has no power to judge the validity of water rights. The Overberg Water Board must confirm that water is available before construction starts [IC].
- The application included the agreement of the owners whose land the new access road crosses, with conditions [IC].

## Appeals

The letter allowed 21 days from registration of the notice for an appeal to the municipality's appeal authority [IC].

## What is still open

- This page has not seen the Site Development Plan approved in 2025, so it does not say how the conditions on siting and clustering were met.
- This page does not know whether the environmental management plan, the CapeNature stewardship agreement, the access servitudes or the Overberg Water Board's comments are in place.
- Five years from the Tribunal's decision on 19 September 2024 runs to September 2029. The letter does not say whether the period counts from the decision or from the letter.
- The letter says the additional units may not be alienated by property share or share block, among other means [IC]. Under the [[documents/shareholders-agreement|Shareholders' Agreement]], each shareholder holds the sole right to use one home [MS]. How these fit together is a question for the company and its advisers. This page takes no view on it.
- The letter refers to the municipality's zoning scheme by-law as both the 2018 and the 2015 by-law [IC].

In February 2024 a town planning firm, PlanServ (Pty) Ltd, applied to [[institutions/hessequa-municipality|Hessequa Municipality]] on behalf of the company's directors for consent to build six homes on the farm in addition to its main dwelling [MS]. The application's motivation report, reference 2302, sets out what was asked for and why [MS]. The municipality approved the application in September 2024. [[documents/municipal-approval-2024|Municipal Approval for Additional Homes (2024)]] summarises that decision and its conditions.

This page summarises the motivation report. Everything in it is the applicant's own account, so it carries [MS], even where the report quotes a law or a map. Where an official record confirms a point, the page says so. The report is marked copyright reserved, and its maps and figures are not reproduced here. Its annexures have not been seen. The letters in square brackets are explained on [[about|About This Site]].

## In short

- The application asked for a primary dwelling, six additional dwellings, a renovated barn and three greenhouses [MS].
- The homes are for the directors, their relatives and friends, and none is to be sold on the open market [MS].
- The farm is to run off the grid, with its own power, water, sanitation and waste recycling [MS].
- Outside the building footprint, the farm is set aside for conservation, alien clearing and planting of indigenous trees [MS].
- The report states that the provincial environment department found no listed activity under the National Environmental Management Act in the revised proposal [MS].

The report says the directors' aim is the one they held from the start: off-grid homes in a rural area where permaculture, fynbos rehabilitation and conservation are practised [MS].

### Weather

The report names the mountain Ertjiesvlei-se-berg and gives its average rainfall as about 750 mm a year, falling in both winter and summer [MS]. It describes cut-off lows that bring thunderstorms and lightning, rare southerly rain that can be extreme, as in 1984, cold fronts that bring most of the rain in the cooler months, and strong bergwinds before the fronts [MS].

### Fire

According to the report, the company is a long-standing member of the Southern Cape Fire Protection Association and has a standing agreement with its direct neighbours to help one another fight fires [MS]. Controlled burns have been run every 7 to 11 years [MS]. The southern and eastern boundaries are the main firebreaks against the valley below, from which two strong wind-driven fires have come up and damaged the farm [MS]. A northern firebreak divides the farm in two and doubles as the main road down to the river and to the homes [MS]. The slope makes a firebreak along the northern boundary impossible [MS]. The firebreaks were designed with the association's help [MS].

## What the application asked for

- A primary dwelling and six additional dwellings, for the directors and their relatives and friends: seven families in all [MS].
- The existing barn frame, renovated as an agricultural building for a tractor, wagon, bakkie and trailer [MS].
- Three greenhouses on previously cultivated ground, for food grown for the community and not for sale, mostly from heirloom seed [MS].
- Chickens, cows and sheep kept for the seven families [MS].
- Homes built from natural materials such as adobe, rammed earth, stone and timber, under a building code written by the directors [MS].
- Power from the sun and wind, and rainwater collected and re-used [MS].
- Landscaping with indigenous, water-wise, fire-dampening or resprouting plants, raised in a nursery on the farm that is not run as a business [MS].

The report describes the project as private and non-profit: the homes are not offered on the open market, through estate agents or to third parties [MS].

## Access and water

The farm has a right of way of necessity over Portion 2 of the farm Annex Klip Hoogte No. 84 to the nearest public road, the gravel road DR1316 [MS]. Portion 2 reaches the road in turn over Portion 6 of Annex Klip Hoogte No. 84, the farm Wel van Pas No. 91 and the farm Bontheuwel No. 84 [MS]. The distance to DR1316 is about 1.25 km [MS]. With the neighbours' consent, and on conditions in an annexure not seen for this page, a new access road is to be built across Portion 2 on an agreed route [MS]. The directors are to build and maintain that stretch of road [MS].

The owners of the farm and of Portion 2 have also agreed on sharing water [MS], and on how each will soften the visual effect of the other's buildings [MS]. Portion 2 had its own application in process for additional homes and tourist accommodation [MS]. [[the-land/neighbours|Neighbours and the Conservation Land Around the Farm]] describes the land around the farm.

## Environment

- The north-western part of the farm extends into a mapped terrestrial Critical Biodiversity Area, and no buildings are proposed there [MS].
- The building footprint lies in a mapped Ecological Support Area, where the provincial guideline allows only low-impact uses that respect biodiversity [MS].
- A botanical assessment found at least three plant species of conservation concern in the areas of medium to high sensitivity, and none in the less sensitive areas [MS]. It expected no significant loss of those species from the building footprint [MS].
- In the farm's conservation zone, the only activities are to be removing alien plants and planting suitable indigenous trees for rehabilitation, with a preference for trees that local birds, wildlife and useful insects favour [MS].
- The application describes the [[the-land/hessequa-hills-private-nature-reserve|Hessequa Hills Private Nature Reserve]] as lying about 850 metres south-east, and the building area as overlooking the [[the-land/boosmansbos-wilderness-area|Boosmansbos Wilderness Area]] [MS].

## Heritage

The report says Heritage Western Cape found no records or evidence of historic settlement on the farm [MS]. The only feature of some historic interest it names is an irrigation canal that crosses the farm beside the river [MS]. That finding came from a notification process for building plans. It does not show that nobody lived on or used this land before the title was registered. [[history/overview|The Duiwenhoks Valley: A Historical Overview]] sets out what is known of the valley's earlier peoples.

## The rules the report cited

The report quotes the Hessequa zoning scheme by-law of 2018 on additional dwellings [MS]:

- One additional dwelling is allowed on any farm, and more at one for every 10 hectares, up to a maximum of five [MS]. The report reads this as one plus five, giving six for a farm of 201 hectares [MS]. The Tribunal allowed six, finding that number consistent with the by-law [IC].
- An additional dwelling, with all the buildings that go with it, may have no more than 200 m² of floor space [MS].
- An additional dwelling must be built in a style similar to the main dwelling, unless the municipality permits otherwise [MS].
- A free-standing additional dwelling may be no higher than 6.5 metres to the top of the roof [MS].
- Where an additional dwelling shares a building with another dwelling, the building must look like a single dwelling [MS].
- No additional dwelling may be separated off by subdivision or sectional title [MS].
- Buildings must stand at least 30 metres from every boundary and road [MS].

It also quotes the provincial rural areas guideline of 2019, which recommends one additional dwelling for every 10 hectares to a maximum of five, a floor area of 175 m² including garaging, and a single storey [MS]. The report notes that the municipal by-law takes precedence [MS].

[[the-houses/index|The Houses]] works within these limits.

## What is still open

- The report says on one page that the total floor space of the additional dwellings may not exceed 200 m², and elsewhere that each will be up to 200 m² [MS]. The municipal approval sets 200 m² for each unit [IC].
- Whether a house divided into two self-contained halves counts as one dwelling or two under the by-law. The by-law treats a dwelling inside the same building as another as a separate unit [MS], and the approval caps the farm at seven [IC].
- The report gives the farm as about 8 km from Heidelberg; other pages on this site give about 9 km. Neither has been measured on a common basis.
- The report calls the provincial road both P1316 and DR1316.
- The application's annexures, including the site development plan, the building code, the water-sharing agreement, the access conditions, the botanical assessment and the heritage response, have not been seen for this page.
- The two earlier fires the report mentions are not dated.

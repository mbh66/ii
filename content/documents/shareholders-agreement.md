The shareholders' agreement (SHA) is the legal contract between Isikhova & Ironwood Forest Community (Pty) Ltd and its shareholders [MS]. The company owns the farm [MS], about 200 hectares in the Heidelberg district of the Western Cape [IC]. The agreement sets out how the company makes decisions, who may build a home and where, and how shares change hands. The version summarised here is the final text dated 31 October 2025 [MS]. Daily community life is covered separately by the [[documents/community-constitution|Community Constitution]].

The agreement also sets out how the company is funded, how the cost of building a home is recorded, how shares are valued and how the money from a sale is divided. This site does not publish the financial terms of the company's agreements, so those parts are left out of this summary.

The letters in square brackets show where each fact comes from. [MS] means it comes from the company's own document. [[about|About This Site]] explains the tags.

## In short

- The company plans to have seven equal shareholders, each linked to one home on its own one-hectare Area [MS].
- Each founding shareholder has one vote, whatever the size of their shareholding [MS].
- The biggest decisions need every founding shareholder to agree [MS].
- Nobody can sell or transfer shares without the written consent of all the shareholders [MS].
- A home built by a shareholder belongs in law to the company, because the company owns the land. The shareholder who built it has the sole right to use it [MS].

## How the agreement fits with other documents

- The agreement governs relations between the shareholders and the company. The company's Memorandum of Incorporation (MOI) governs the matters set by company law. Where the two conflict, the MOI wins [MS].
- The shareholders undertook to amend the MOI within 30 days to include the share classes, the way directors are nominated, the voting thresholds, and the rules on exclusive-use Areas and building [MS].
- The [[documents/community-constitution|Community Constitution]] governs daily community life. On legal matters, the agreement and the MOI take precedence [MS].
- The agreement lasts until only one shareholder remains, or until all parties agree in writing to end it [MS]. It binds the parties' estates and heirs [MS].

## Shares and votes

- The company has 100 shares [MS].
- There are three classes of share at present. Class A shares belong to the founders and carry the votes. Class B shares are dormant and carry no vote. Class C shares are held by the company itself and carry no vote [MS].
- Each Class A shareholder has one vote, however many shares they hold [MS].
- Four incoming shareholders are expected to join, each buying 14 shares. Their shares come first from the dormant class and then from part of a founding holding [MS]. These newcomers are called Class D shareholders [MS].
- Once a Class D shareholder holds 14 shares, they have the same rights and obligations as the founders [MS]. When all four have joined, every shareholder except the company has identical rights [MS].
- The end state is seven shareholders with 14 shares each, and two shares kept by the company [MS].
- A shareholding may be owned privately by more than one person, but one person stands as the shareholder for its Area and home in the company's register [MS].
- No new shares can be issued without the written consent of all shareholders [MS].

## The Board and decisions

- Each founding shareholder may nominate one director to the Board [MS].
- **Decisions that need every founding shareholder to agree** (called material decisions) include changing the MOI or the rights of a share class, changing the exclusive-use Areas, issuing shares or taking on any debt, dealing with a shareholder or director or their relatives, giving guarantees, appointing or removing directors, changing insurance cover, winding up the company, approving the annual budget, capital spending, home-building costs above a set limit and large contracts [MS].
- **Smaller contracts** need a simple majority of founding shareholders. The smallest contracts need no Board decision if they are within the approved budget [MS].
- **All other decisions** need a simple majority of founding shareholders [MS].
- **Meetings.** An annual general meeting is held each year, and the Board meets at least twice a year. Shareholders holding at least 10% can ask for a special meeting [MS]. Directors get 14 days' notice of regular meetings and 7 days' notice of special meetings [MS].
- **Quorum.** A Board meeting needs directors who together represent at least 75% of the founding shareholders' votes [MS]. The chair has no casting vote [MS]. A written resolution signed by every director counts as a Board decision [MS].

## Homes and Areas

- The Site Development Plan allows up to seven homes: a Main Dwelling and six Additional Dwellings. Each is allocated to one shareholder and sits within its own one-hectare Area [MS].
- The Main Dwelling is approved for 350 m² under roof. Part A, 200 m², is allocated to one shareholder. Part B, 150 m², is a shared facility. The two parts are connected buildings [MS].
- Each Additional Dwelling may have up to 200 m² under roof [MS]. A double storey needs the approval of all shareholders [MS].
- The design of every home, including its colours and building materials, must be acceptable to all shareholders and follow the Site Development Plan and the constitution [MS].
- Areas stay unfenced, and each home keeps a firebreak of at least 10 metres [MS].
- Each shareholder builds their home at their own cost and maintains it at their own cost [MS].
- The company cedes to each shareholder the sole right to use their home and Area [MS].
- With the consent of all shareholders concerned, an Area can be moved, but only before building has started [MS].
- These rules on the use of the farm can be changed only by the unanimous written agreement of the shareholders [MS].

## Shared facilities and the rest of the farm

- Everything outside the exclusive-use Areas is shared. That includes the workshop, Part B of the Main Dwelling, the bulk water supply and the roads [MS]. Each shareholder has fair access to them under the constitution [MS].
- Shareholders keep shared facilities tidy after use. A shareholder who keeps breaking this rule can lose access, by majority decision of the other shareholders, until they put it right. If the breach goes on for six months or more, the others can withhold access until they are satisfied it will not happen again. A new shareholder who buys from that person gets full access back [MS].
- The company maintains the shared facilities and the farm. Each shareholder maintains their own home [MS].

## Insurance

- The company insures the shared facilities, the homes, other structures and movable property against fire, storm and other risks, and reviews its cover every year [MS]. After damage, it repairs or rebuilds [MS].
- If an insurance payout falls short, shareholders cover the gap on shared assets in proportion to their shareholding. The gap on a home falls to that home's shareholder [MS].
- Each shareholder insures the contents of their own home, at their own risk [MS].

## Selling or passing on shares

- A sale needs the written consent of all the other shareholders, who may refuse without giving a reason [MS].
- A seller gives written notice to the company, its accountants and the other shareholders, naming the buyer and the price, and confirming that the buyer will sign the agreement [MS].
- A sale must cover the seller's whole stake in the company. A partial sale is void [MS].
- The buyer signs the agreement before the shares are registered in their name [MS].
- If the price is disputed, an independent valuer decides it [MS].
- The company handles the marketing of the dormant shares to incoming shareholders [MS].
- A shareholder may move their whole stake into a company or trust owned by or for themselves and their close family, without the full sale process. They give the other shareholders at least 7 business days' notice, with proof and a signed copy of the agreement [MS].
- On a sale, the company arranges for the seller to be released from their obligations as a shareholder or director [MS].

## Removal of a shareholder

The agreement calls this a "Deemed Offer" [MS].

- If the Board decides by special resolution that a shareholder has seriously breached the agreement or the MOI, or caused serious harm that justifies removal, that shareholder must offer their whole stake to the company [MS].
- The stake is valued within 60 days. Within 10 business days after that, the company buys it or names a buyer [MS]. The price follows a formula in the agreement, which this site does not publish.
- From the date of the Board's decision, the shareholder loses their voting and management rights [MS].
- The shareholder, with their family, friends and belongings, must leave the farm within three months of that date [MS].
- Under the constitution, the community's own process of mediation and discipline runs first, and the CMP works with the Board [MS].

## Confidentiality and personal information

- Each party keeps the agreement, the negotiations behind it and information about the company confidential, unless the law requires disclosure or a party needs it to pursue a legal remedy [MS].
- The parties agree the wording and timing of any public announcement about confidential matters [MS].
- A shareholder who wants to sell may give a prospective buyer the information they need, once the buyer has signed a confidentiality letter that the Board accepts [MS].
- The parties commit to handling personal information under the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) [MS].

## Disputes and default

- **Talk first.** A party invites the others in writing to meet, and they try to settle the dispute within 14 days [MS].
- **Arbitration.** If talks fail, the dispute goes to informal arbitration in Cape Town, with the aim of finishing within 21 business days [MS]. The arbitrator is an accountant for accounting questions or a Senior Counsel for legal questions. For anything else, the parties agree on a suitable person. If the parties cannot agree on the arbitrator within three business days, the President of the Law Society of the Cape of Good Hope appoints one [MS]. The arbitrator's decision is final [MS].
- **Courts.** A party may still go to court for urgent relief. The Western Cape High Court has jurisdiction [MS].
- **Default.** A party in breach has 14 days after written notice to put it right. After that, the others can go to court to enforce the agreement and claim damages, and the party in breach pays their legal costs [MS]. No party may cancel the agreement [MS].
- Each party confirms that it took its own legal, regulatory and tax advice [MS].

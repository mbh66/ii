> **This idea is not yet agreed.** The prospective stewards wrote it in October 2026. Nobody else has been asked: not the company, its Community Management Panel (CMP) or its Board, not the residents, and not CapeNature, the Greater Cape Town Water Fund, The Nature Conservancy, any university or the owners of neighbouring land. It will be put to the company through the CMP, after the company has responded to [[the-land/the-lands-story|The Land's Story]].

## The goal

> Spend the first five years learning how water and soil behave on the farm, with measurements good enough to share, while the community settles its homes and its way of working, and only then take the results to neighbours, CapeNature and water funders.

## Why the land needs it

- **Nobody has measured the farm's water.** No rainfall, soil moisture or stream flow record for the farm is known to this site [MS]. The gauging station on the river near the farm recorded flows only from 1972 to 1984 [IC].
- **The question is new.** A literature search found no study that tests water-holding earthworks in fynbos or renosterveld, and no study of how such earthworks behave on land that burns [MS]. The farm is mapped mostly as South Langeberg Sandstone Fynbos [MS], and a fire burned it in August 2025 [MS]. [[research/water-retention-evidence|Evidence on Water and Soil in Fynbos and Renosterveld]] sets out what is and is not known.
- **The search set out what a first study would need.** At least two years of records before any work starts, untreated land kept for comparison, and records over at least three years to catch dry and wet seasons [MS]. The measures it lists include how fast water soaks in, soil moisture at several depths, runoff, sediment, groundwater, stream flow, plant cover and cost [MS].
- **The community has already named this work.** The constitution lists removing alien plants, planting indigenous species, low-tillage methods, hedgerows and swales among its conservation priorities, and its CMP portfolios include Environment and Energy-Water-Waste [MS]. Residents tell the CMP before any major earthworks and get its sign-off [MS].
- **The land already has conservation conditions.** The 2024 approval for additional homes requires an environmental conservation management plan, covering fire and invasive plants and linked stewardship agreement with CapeNature, before building plans are approved [IC]. This wiki makes no claim on whether the plan or the agreement is in place, and says nothing more on them until the company has replied [MS].
- **Durable restoration elsewhere began with records and patience.** A review of sixteen community-led initiatives in sub-Saharan Africa found that the ones that lasted built their formal relationships after years of shared work, and that a community holding its own long record gained standing with its leaders and with researchers [MS]. [[research/restoration-lessons-from-africa|Restoration Lessons from Muonde and Other African Cases]] gives the evidence and its limits.
- **Water funders ask for measured results.** The [[greater-cape-town-water-fund|Cape Water bond]] pays its success payments only on independently verified results [IC]. Its work, and the Greater Cape Town Water Fund's, lies in catchments that supply the Western Cape Water Supply System, and the Duiwenhoks is not among them [MS]. Rand Merchant Bank has described the bond as the first of a series meant to cover the country's strategic water source areas [TBV]. The Langeberg is one of those areas [IC].

## The idea

The idea has four strands. The first two would run for the whole five years. The last two would begin once there is something to show.

### Strand 1: record the land and run small trials (years 1 to 5)

**Years 1 and 2: the record before any work.** Residents and the prospective stewards would set up simple, repeatable measures: rain gauges, soil moisture probes at several depths, infiltration tests, fixed photo points, plant survey plots, erosion pins in any gullies or banks, and a log of every clearing and every fire. The record would include how the land comes back after the August 2025 fire. Part of the farm would be left untreated as a comparison. The protected land beside the farm could also serve as a reference, if its managers agree.

**Years 3 to 5: small trials, each paired with an untreated plot.** Once two years of records exist, the community could try small, hand-built water-holding work on a few plots: contour bunds, brush packing on bare ground, and small check structures in an eroding gully. Each trial would sit beside a plot left alone, so the difference can be measured. Trials would stay out of the area the 2024 decision marks as botanically sensitive, would avoid the river and its banks until any authorisation they need is confirmed, and would be planned with the farm's fire plan. Every trial would go to the CMP for sign-off first, as the constitution asks for earthworks.

**The record would belong to the community.** Data would be kept on the farm, in a form residents can read, with copies held by any research partner under a written agreement.

### Strand 2: settle the community (years 1 to 5)

While the land is being recorded, the prospective stewards would build within the approved Site Development Plan and the constitution, and learn how the community works through its CMP. The measuring itself could become part of community life: shared rain readings, seasonal walks to the photo points, and a yearly look at the results at the annual general meeting. New members would learn the record as part of their induction.

### Strand 3: a research partnership with a South African university (from year 1)

The prospective stewards would invite a South African university to help design the measurements, so that the record meets research standards and the standard a verifier would ask for. Postgraduate students could run parts of the study as theses. Stellenbosch University theses have already studied water repellency in fynbos soils and the hydrology of renosterveld [IC], so its soil science and conservation ecology departments are natural first contacts, alongside universities closer to the Southern Cape. A written research agreement would settle who owns the data, how residents consent to work on their land, and how results are published. Nothing would be published about the community without the CMP's agreement, as the constitution asks for public statements [MS].

### Strand 4: share the results (from about year 4)

Outreach would begin when there is something to show: at least three years of records, the first trial results, and a short report the university partner has reviewed.

- **Neighbours first.** The prospective stewards would offer the results to the owners of neighbouring land and to the [[institutions/grootvadersbosch-conservancy|Grootvadersbosch Conservancy]], whose members already clear invasive plants and plan fire together [TBV]. Field days on the farm could let neighbours see the trials and the records. Any wider role along the river or the coast would come only by invitation.
- **CapeNature.** CapeNature manages the protected land above and beside the farm and runs the stewardship programme [IC]. The farm's records of fynbos recovering after fire, and of trials beside untreated plots, could be offered to its scientists and its stewardship staff. How this proposal relates to the stewardship agreement the 2024 decision requires is for the company to say.
- **The Greater Cape Town Water Fund and The Nature Conservancy.** With measured results in hand, the prospective stewards would share them with the [[institutions/greater-cape-town-water-fund|Fund]] and [[institutions/the-nature-conservancy|The Nature Conservancy]], as evidence from a catchment the Fund does not yet work in. A measured record is what a later bond for the Langeberg would need before it could pay for water [MS].

### The five years at a glance

| Years | Land and water | Community | Research | Outward |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set up measures; record fire recovery | Homes begin within the approved plan; stewards learn the CMP's process | First contact with universities; draft research agreement | None |
| 2 | Full year of records; untreated areas fixed | Shared readings become routine | Study design agreed; first student project | None |
| 3 | First small trials beside untreated plots | Annual review of the record | Trial measurements | None |
| 4 | Trials continue; second wet season | Record part of induction | First reviewed report | Results offered to neighbours and the conservancy |
| 5 | Trials continue | CMP reviews the five years | Second report | Results offered to CapeNature, the Fund and The Nature Conservancy |

## Who would benefit

- **Residents** would know their land's water, soil and fire behaviour from their own records, which the farm's fire and alien plant plans could draw on.
- **People downstream.** The river below the dam supplies Overberg Water's scheme for Heidelberg, Slangrivier and Witsand, and the farmers who share the dam's water [IC]. Whether the scheme's pump station lies below the farm has not been checked [MS]. If trials or clearing were found to add water to the river, the record would show it.
- **Researchers and conservation bodies** would gain the first measured results on water-holding work in fynbos, from land that burned in 2025.
- **Neighbours** would gain results from land like their own, tested before anyone asks them to try anything.

## Who would pay

Nothing has been costed. Most of the first two years would be residents' time and simple instruments. A university partner might bring its own research funds and student bursaries. Grants for research or restoration could be sought through a separate body, so that funders' terms stay apart from the CMP's decisions. No income from water, carbon or bonds is assumed. The company's budget and capital spending are decisions for the shareholders under the shareholders' agreement [MS].

## Who would decide

- **Community matters go to the CMP.** The record, the community's part in it, and each trial's sign-off would go to the CMP, which advises the Board [MS].
- **Legal matters go to the Board.** A research agreement with a university, any agreement with CapeNature or a water fund, and any change to the title deed are contracts or legal matters for the Board under the shareholders' agreement [MS]. Approving the annual budget, capital spending and large contracts are among the decisions that need every founding shareholder to agree [MS].

## What is still open

- Whether the company, its CMP and its Board want to consider this idea. None have been approached yet.
- Whether residents already keep any record of rainfall, fire, clearing or plants on the farm.
- Which parts of the farm would be left untreated, and whether the managers of the protected land next door would allow it to serve as a reference.
- Whether trials near watercourses would need authorisation from the [[institutions/breede-olifants-catchment-management-agency|Breede-Olifants Catchment Management Agency]] or under environmental law, and which trials can go ahead without it.
- How trials would fit the environmental conservation management plan and the stewardship agreement the 2024 decision requires. This concerns the company, and the wiki will say nothing more on it until the company has replied.
- Which university, and which department, would take part, and on what terms.
- Who in the community would keep the record day to day, and how much time it would take.
- What "something to show" means in practice, and who decides that outreach can begin.
- Whether the Greater Cape Town Water Fund or [[the-nature-conservancy|The Nature Conservancy]] has any interest in catchments beyond the Western Cape Water Supply System. Neither has been asked.

## What happens next

1. Share this proposal with the company, through the CMP, after the company has responded to [[the-land/the-lands-story|The Land's Story]].
2. Ask residents what they already know and record about the land, and whether they would take part.
3. Ask the company how the proposal relates to its environmental plan and stewardship agreement.
4. If the company agrees in principle, write a one-page study design for the first two years and take it to two or three university departments.
5. Ask CapeNature and the managers of the neighbouring protected land about a reference site.
6. Bring a costed plan for years 1 and 2 to the CMP and the Board.

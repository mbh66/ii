> **This idea is not yet agreed.** The prospective stewards wrote it in October 2026. They have asked a drone survey firm for a quote and asked the creator of LandScope, the mapping software named below, whether the survey is needed. Nobody else has been asked: not the company, its Community Management Panel (CMP) or its Board, not the residents whose homes the drone would fly over, not the owners of neighbouring land, and not CapeNature. It will be put to the company through the CMP, together with [[proposals/first-five-years-on-the-land|The First Five Years on the Land]], because the other proposals on this site depend on the maps it would produce.

## The goal

> Measure the shape of the farm's ground to within a few centimetres before any home, road, pond or swale is placed, and keep that measurement as the first entry in a ten-year record of how the land changes.

## Why the land needs it

### The site's maps rest on coarse data

- **Every height on this site comes from 10-metre contours.** The proposals for the homes and for water-holding work were drawn from the Western Cape Government's 10-metre contours [IC]. Their heights, slopes and lengths are marked [TBV] for that reason.
- **The contours miss features the family saw on the ground.** A fairly deep indentation runs along the south-western side of the Residential Zone [MS], and the contours are too coarse to show it [TBV]. The model built from them sends the water in the residential area north-west into the west gully, which means the layout of the cascading ponds may be wrong [TBV]. The family also saw a slight indentation in the shelf north of Unit 1 that the contours cannot show [MS].
- **Swales need levels the contours cannot give.** [[proposals/homes-in-a-water-retention-landscape|Homes in a Water Retention Landscape]] places a swale along the 411-metre contour and a return swale falling from about 394 to 389 metres [TBV]. A swale works only if it follows the ground to within a few centimetres along its length. Ten-metre contours place a line somewhere within a 10-metre band of height.
- **LandScope's creator advises caution.** LandScope's creator reviewed the farm's map at the stewards' request. He wrote that the 10-metre contours are useful for a broad sense of the land, and that he would be cautious about using them for detailed siting or water decisions in terrain this complex [MS].

### Decisions are waiting on the ground's shape

- **Where the homes go is still open.** The six Units' positions are provisional, and each plot can move within roughly the Residential Zone; the Main Residence can go almost anywhere [MS]. [[the-houses/index|The Houses]] sets out four draft layouts.
- **The ground limits how homes are built.** It is sandy and very rocky, and a builder advised the family to dig as little as possible [MS]. Slope and rock at each site decide how much a slab, a stepped floor or an earth-sheltered build would cost.
- **The water proposals need exact levels.** Pond 3 near Unit 3 sits on ground of about 20% and may need an earth wall of 2 to 3 metres [TBV]. The gully stuffing, the ponds and the swales all depend on where water gathers and how fast it moves.
- **Research plots must be matched.** [[proposals/first-five-years-on-the-land|The First Five Years on the Land]] pairs every trial with an untreated plot. Two plots can be compared only if their slope, aspect and wetness are alike.
- **Fire planning needs the same map.** The farm burned in August 2025 [MS]. The 2024 approval for additional homes requires an environmental conservation management plan that includes fire management [IC]. Firebreaks and access for fire vehicles follow the ground.

### This season may be the best time to measure

- **LiDAR reads the ground best where plants are low.** The survey firm's quote says accuracy falls where the ground is hidden by dense vegetation, and that such areas are reported and left unfilled [MS].
- **The fynbos is at its lowest after the fire.** Most of the farm maps as South Langeberg Sandstone Fynbos [MS], and it burned in August 2025 [MS]. As the fynbos regrows, a later survey would see less of the ground [TBV].
- **A survey now also records the fire's aftermath.** The same flight that measures the ground measures what is growing on it. That gives the research record its starting point for recovery after the 2025 fire.

## The idea

A drone carrying a LiDAR scanner would fly the whole farm in about a day. The scanner sends pulses of laser light to the ground and times their return, building a cloud of millions of measured points. Software separates the points that hit the ground from the points that hit plants and buildings.

### What the survey would deliver

The quote the stewards hold sets out the following [MS]. LandScope has been asked to confirm that each item meets what its mapping needs.

**Area and scope**

- About 200 hectares of private land, inside the SG boundary for Farm 88/10 used in our Google Earth map: https://earth.google.com/earth/d/1Z2zXb-eL7OAv6sm-yILVHrx4tfyZ1oys?usp=sharing. The work would be re-priced if the final boundary differed materially.
- The aerial survey only. A survey on foot is not included.

**Time on site**

- One day to set out ground control, one day of flying and three days of processing.

**Ground control**

- About eight ground control points, built from materials suited to LiDAR and aerial photography, and made to last the length of the project.
- A list of the points as a CSV file, in the coordinate system and datum the company chooses.
- Ground control accurate to within 1.5 centimetres.

**Products**

- A model of the bare ground, made from the LiDAR.
- Contours at 1-metre intervals.
- An aerial photograph of the whole farm, stitched from the flight.
- A project report.

**Stated accuracy**

- Position and orientation of the scanner in flight: 1 centimetre and 0.002 degrees.
- Density of the point cloud: 20 to 50 points per square metre.
- Separation of ground points from plants and structures: 95% confidence.
- Ground model: within 5 centimetres in height.
- Aerial photograph: better than 5 centimetres per pixel.
- Each figure checked on delivery against independent check points.

**Limits**

- The accuracies hold where the sensor can see the ground.
- Dense canopy, standing water, dense structures and steep shadowed ground give fewer ground returns. The quote says these areas are marked in the report and left unfilled.

**Not listed in the quote**

- The classified point cloud itself.
- A surface model showing the height of plants and buildings.
- File formats, the resolution of the ground model, and the terms on which the company would own and share the data.

### Turning the survey into maps

The ground model would be processed in [LandScope](https://landscope.earth), a web-based terrain mapping service that accepts a drone survey uploaded by its user [TBV]. LandScope produces layers for slope, aspect, landforms, flow accumulation, wetness and the drainage network, with a 3D view, and exports to Google Earth and standard mapping formats [TBV]. Residents and advisers could view the maps, measure on them and leave comments in a shared workspace [TBV].

LandScope's own guidance is to extend the analysis beyond the property line, to see water arriving from upslope [TBV]. The drone would fly only the farm unless neighbours agree to more. Beyond the boundary, coarser public data would fill in.

### What the survey would answer

| Decision | Question | What the maps would show |
|---|---|---|
| Where the homes stand | Which sites are flattest and least rocky? Where does cold air settle on still nights? | Slope, aspect, landforms, low ground where cold air pools |
| How the homes are built | How much cut and fill would each site need? | Slope and cross sections at each site |
| Roads and tracks | Which routes keep gradients workable and stay out of water paths? | Slope, drainage network |
| Ponds and swales | Where can water be held, at what level, and behind how high a wall? | 1-metre contours, flow accumulation, cross sections |
| The gully stuffing | Where does water concentrate, and where would check structures hold? | Flow accumulation, wetness, drainage network |
| Research plots | Which treated and untreated plots are alike enough to compare? | Slope, aspect, wetness |
| Fire planning | Where should firebreaks and access for fire vehicles run? | Slope, aspect, vegetation height from the point cloud |

### A record repeated in years 5 and 10

The ground control points would stay in place, so a second survey in year 5 and a third in year 10 would line up with the first. Comparing the three would show where soil has moved, with gains behind check structures and losses from eroding banks. It would also show how the fynbos has grown back on treated and untreated land. A survey repeated on fixed control points is the kind of measured record [[proposals/first-five-years-on-the-land|The First Five Years on the Land]] says funders and researchers will ask for.

The protected land north of the farm, [[the-land/wilderness-nature-reserve|Wilderness Nature Reserve]], could serve as a reference for the research if its managers agree. A matched strip of it, flown at the same time as the farm, would let the record compare land under the stewards' care with land under protection.

### What the survey would not do

The survey measures the surface. It would show nothing about soil depth, rock below the surface or groundwater. In dense thicket, steep shadowed ground or standing water, the ground model would be weaker, and the report would say where [MS]. Every placement drawn from the maps would still be walked, pegged and checked on the ground before work begins.

## Who would benefit

- **Residents and future residents** would place homes, tracks and water works from a measured map, and would see the maps themselves.
- **The company** would hold an accurate record of its land, useful for the environmental conservation management plan and for any later building or planning application.
- **A research partner** would start with a measured baseline from the first year, and with the means to repeat it.
- **Neighbours, the conservancy and CapeNature** could be offered maps of water leaving the farm toward the Duiwenhoks River and of fynbos recovering after fire, if the company agrees to share them.

## Who would pay

The survey has been quoted, but who would pay has not been discussed with anyone. The quote asks both parties to keep its terms confidential [MS], so the firm and the price (in the region of US$4,000) are held by the stewards and shared with the company directly. A research partner could be asked to share the cost of the surveys in years 5 and 10.

The survey could be paid by the company, by the prospective stewards, or by both. The company's capital spending and large contracts are decisions for the shareholders under the shareholders' agreement [MS]. The drone scanning company is available only from early December.

## Who would decide

- **Legal matters go to the Board.** The contract with the survey firm, the landowner's consent the survey needs, and any agreement to share data with a research partner are matters for the Board under the shareholders' agreement [MS]. Capital spending and large contracts need every founding shareholder to agree [MS].
- **Community matters go to the CMP.** The drone would fly over existing structures and layouts and photograph them in detail. Whether residents agree to that, and who may see the photographs, is a community matter for the CMP, which advises the Board [MS].
- **Existing tracks and firebreaks would be the starting point.** Firebreaks have already been cleared and tracks laid out so that the farm manager can reach most parts of the land, though none have been graded [MS]. Any change the survey suggests would be agreed with the farm manager through the CMP.

## What is still unknown

- Whether the company, its CMP and its Board want to consider this idea. None have been approached.
- Whether prospective residents agree to the land being mapped, and on what terms the aerial photograph would be kept and shown.
- Who would pay, and whether the survey would be paid as a cost of the stewards joining or as an investment by the company.
- Whether the survey firm will supply the full point cloud and a surface model, and on what terms the company would own the data.
- Which coordinate system the data would use, and whether the ground control points can be made permanent.
- Whether the flight should extend beyond the boundary, onto upslope land or onto the reserve, and whether those owners and managers would agree.
- What LandScope would cost for this farm, and for how long the maps would stay available.
- Whether any public LiDAR covers the farm. An extensive search has been conducted and none has been found [MS].
- How long the low vegetation after the fire gives an advantage, and how much a survey in two or three years would lose.

## What happens next

1. Share this proposal with the company, through the CMP, together with [[proposals/first-five-years-on-the-land|The First Five Years on the Land]].
2. Ask the CMP how residents would like to be asked about the flight over their homes.
3. Ask the survey firm to confirm the extra products, data ownership, coordinate system and permanent control points.
4. Ask LandScope's creator for the brief he offered, setting out the design questions the survey should answer, and for the cost of processing.
5. If the company agrees in principle, ask CapeNature about flying a reference strip of the reserve.
6. Bring the costed survey to the Board for consent and a decision on who pays, in time to fly while the vegetation is low.

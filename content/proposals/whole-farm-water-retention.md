> **This idea is not yet agreed.** The prospective stewards wrote it in October 2026 from a desk reading of the province's contours and a weekend on the land. Nobody else has been asked: not the company, its Community Management Panel (CMP) or its Board, not the residents, not the owners of neighbouring land, not CapeNature and not the Breede-Olifants Catchment Management Agency. It will be put to the company through the CMP, together with [[proposals/first-five-years-on-the-land|The First Five Years on the Land]] and [[proposals/homes-in-a-water-retention-landscape|Homes in a Water Retention Landscape]].

## The goal

> Choose, from the shape of the whole farm, where water-holding work would do most for the land and the river, and where land should be left alone so the work can be measured against it.

## Why the land needs it

All heights, areas and lengths on this page come from a model of the ground built from the Western Cape Government's 10-metre contours [IC], and are [TBV] until checked on the ground.

- **Most of the farm is steep.** Of its roughly 200 hectares, about 21 hectares slope at less than 5°, and 13 of those lie on the plateau in the Residential and Production Zones [TBV]. About 83 hectares slope at 5 to 15°, and about 95 hectares are steeper than 15° [TBV].
- **Most of it is set aside for nature.** The working map marks about 164 hectares, everything outside the Residential and Production Zones, as an Ecological Zone earmarked for biodiversity and conservation [MS]. Almost all the steep land lies in it [TBV].
- **Most of the farm drains west to the Duiwenhoks.** The river runs along the farm's western boundary for about 1.3 kilometres [TBV]. About two-thirds of the farm reaches it, most of that through one gully [TBV].
- **The main gully crosses the farm.** It starts at the cabin, at about 400 metres, and runs about 2.4 kilometres north-west to the river, at about 130 metres [TBV]. It drains about 127 hectares, about 100 of them on the farm, and also takes water from the land east of the cabin [TBV]. It falls at 11 to 14% for most of its length and eases to about 6% over its last 500 metres before the river [TBV]. It carries no water today [MS].
- **A west valley lies wholly on the farm.** It runs about 870 metres from the north-western end of the Residential Zone, at about 340 metres, to the river, and drains about 23 hectares [TBV]. A fairly deep valley, almost a gully, runs along the south-western side of the Residential Zone [MS]. The contours are too coarse to show it, and the model sends its water north-west into the west valley [TBV].
- **A boundary valley is shared with a neighbour.** It runs about 675 metres along the farm's south-western boundary to the river and drains about 13 hectares, about 5 of them on the farm [TBV]. Near the river it is 25 to 30 metres deep [TBV].
- **A south valley leaves the farm.** It drains about 16 hectares from the southern edge of the plateau, including part of the Production Zone, and leaves the farm across its southern boundary at about 255 metres [TBV].
- **Some of the land is botanically sensitive.** Most of the farm maps as South Langeberg Sandstone Fynbos, and one point near the north-western boundary maps as Swellendam Silcrete Fynbos, which is listed as endangered [MS]. Part of the approved building footprint lies in an area of medium-high botanical sensitivity [IC].
- **The farm burned in 2025** [MS]. Fire-killed wood lies across much of it [TBV].
- **The evidence is open.** No published study tests water-holding earthworks in fynbos or renosterveld, or on land that burns [MS]. [[research/water-retention-evidence|Evidence on Water and Soil in Fynbos and Renosterveld]] sets out what is and is not known.

## The idea

A [Water Retention Landscape](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/concepts/water-retention-landscapes) is land whose soil, plants and earthworks catch rain, hold it and release it slowly. On this farm the plateau is the only gentle ground, the valleys carry the water, and the river edge is where restored land does most for baseflow. The five layouts below each take one of those places as their starting point. They can be combined.

![[ii-water-retention-whole-farm.png]]

### Layout 1: Sponge on the plateau

Contour swales across the plateau between 395 and 415 metres, up to about 5.7 kilometres in all, with the three cascading ponds set out in [[proposals/homes-in-a-water-retention-landscape|Homes in a Water Retention Landscape]] [TBV]. Rain is caught at the top of every catchment and seeps slowly into the main gully and the south valley below.

- **Fits:** all the digging stays in the Residential and Production Zones, close to the homes, where it is easiest to build, look after and measure.
- **Watch:** the swales cross tracks and the firebreaks around each Area, and need planning with both. Swales on the plateau's southern edge change the head of the south valley.

### Layout 2: The gully, head to river

One design for the whole main gully, in four reaches:

| Reach | Along the gully | Height | Work |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upper | 0 to about 520 m | about 400 to 335 m | The Proposed Gully Stuff reach: brush packing and rock |
| Middle | about 520 to 1,700 m | about 335 to 175 m | Rock check structures at bends and narrows |
| Lower valley floor | about 1,700 to 2,240 m | about 175 to 145 m | A chain of shallow ponds and leaky weirs on the gentlest ground |
| Mouth | the last 200 m | about 145 to 130 m | A small wetland where the gully meets the river |

- **Fits:** the gully carries most of the farm's water, so this layout reaches most of the land. A restored valley floor near the river is the kind of work the BioConomy wiki credits with the greatest effect on baseflow.
- **Watch:** all of it is work in a watercourse, so it needs the most authorisation. The lower reaches lie far from the homes and are harder to reach and look after.

### Layout 3: Paired valleys

Treat the west valley with gully plugs and brush packing, and leave the boundary valley beside it alone. Both face north-west and drain to the river on the same ground. The west valley lies wholly on the farm, so its whole catchment is under the community's care.

- **Fits:** the cleanest experiment of the five, at the smallest scale. The difference between a treated and an untreated valley side by side is the evidence a researcher or a water fund would ask for.
- **Watch:** the boundary valley is half on the neighbour's land, so measuring it fully needs the neighbour's agreement. The deep valley along the south-western side of the Residential Zone feeds the west valley, so homes, tracks and swales there would change it.

### Layout 4: Lines across the slopes

Brush or rock lines laid by hand on the contour, one for every 10 metres of height, on the 5 to 15° slopes of the Ecological Zone: about 4.7 kilometres in all [TBV]. Wood killed in the 2025 fire could supply the brush.

- **Fits:** no digging, so it suits land set aside for conservation, and residents could build it a few lines at a time.
- **Watch:** brush is fuel in fynbos. Rock lines are safer near homes and firebreaks, and brush lines belong in the fire plan.

### Layout 5: The river edge

Clear invasive plants and plant indigenous species in a strip about 60 metres deep along about 1.3 kilometres of the Duiwenhoks, about 14 hectares in all, with the wetland at the mouth of the main gully [TBV].

- **Fits:** mostly clearing and planting, with little digging. Clearing invasive plants is the work the [[the-land/cape-water-bond|Cape Water bond]] pays for in Cape Town's catchments [IC], and volunteers working with CapeNature already clear pines above the Duiwenhoks [IC]. The Grootvadersbosch Conservancy reports that invasive plants dominate the lower river [TBV].
- **Watch:** the river and its banks fall under the water law, and the steep banks are hard to work on. Results on the river are the hardest to measure, because the river also carries water from upstream.

### How the layouts compare

| | 1. Plateau | 2. Gully | 3. Paired valleys | 4. Slopes | 5. River edge |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Where | Residential and Production Zones | Main gully, 2.4 km | West valley and boundary valley | 5 to 15° slopes | River frontage and gully mouth |
| Main work | Swales and ponds | Stuffing, check structures, ponds, wetland | Plugs and brush packing | Brush or rock lines | Clearing and planting |
| Digging | Small machine and hand | Hand, small machine on the valley floor | Hand | None | Hand |
| In the Ecological Zone | No | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Work in a watercourse | No [TBV] | Yes | Yes | No | Yes |
| Distance from the homes | Beside them | 0 to 2.4 km | 0.6 to 1.5 km | Across the farm | 1.5 to 2.5 km |
| Strength as evidence | Moderate | Strong at the river, if a comparison is kept | Strongest | Weak, unless set out as plots | Hard to separate from upstream |

### A suggested combination for the first five years

- **Years 1 and 2:** record all three north-western valleys, the south valley and the river edge, so every later change has a before.
- **Layout 1 with the homes,** starting with Pond 2 and its swale when the Main Dwelling is built.
- **Layout 2 from the top down,** beginning with the upper reach once any authorisation is in place, then the valley floor and the mouth.
- **Keep the west valley and the boundary valley untreated** as the comparison for the main gully. Layout 3 then becomes a measuring task for the first five years, and the treatment of the west valley waits for a second phase.
- **Layout 5 after two years of records,** beginning with clearing.
- **Layout 4 only as a few test plots,** each beside a plot left alone.

## Who would benefit

- **Residents** would gain water held around the homes, records of their own land, and work that can be done by hand over many seasons.
- **People downstream.** The Duiwenhoks below the dam supplies Overberg Water's scheme for Heidelberg, Slangrivier and Witsand, and the farmers who share the dam's water [IC]. If the work added to the river's flow between rains, the record would show it.
- **Neighbours, CapeNature and researchers** would gain a planned and measured Water Retention Landscape in fynbos, on land that burned in 2025, with untreated valleys kept for comparison.

## Who would pay

Nothing has been costed. Layouts 3, 4 and 5 are mostly hand work with materials from the farm. Layout 1 needs a small machine for the ponds. The lower reaches of Layout 2 may need one too, and are the costliest to reach. A university partner might bring research funds and students for the measuring, and grants for restoration could be sought through a separate body, as [[proposals/first-five-years-on-the-land|The First Five Years on the Land]] suggests.

## Who would decide

- **Community matters go to the CMP.** Each earthwork needs the CMP's sign-off under the constitution [MS]. The CMP advises the Board [MS].
- **Legal matters go to the Board.** Any application for a water use or an environmental authorisation, and any agreement with a neighbour, a university or CapeNature, would be made by the company as owner. Approving capital spending and large contracts needs every founding shareholder to agree [MS].
- **The neighbour on the south-western boundary** would need to agree to any measuring on its side of the boundary valley.
- **The Breede-Olifants Catchment Management Agency** would say which of the gully, valley and river work needs authorisation, and which can go ahead under a general authorisation [TBV].
- **CapeNature** could advise on work in the Ecological Zone and on the sensitive vegetation, through the stewardship agreement the 2024 approval requires [IC].

## What permissions might apply

- Storing water, impeding or diverting the flow of water in a watercourse, and altering the bed or banks of a watercourse are each water uses under section 21 of the National Water Act [IC]. Gullies that flow only after heavy rain may still count as watercourses [TBV].
- Clearing indigenous vegetation above a set area, or in sensitive vegetation, can trigger environmental authorisation [TBV]. Hand-built work that disturbs little ground is the least likely to.
- No native tree may be pruned, felled or disturbed without written approval from the national forestry department [IC].

## What is still open

- Whether the company, its CMP and its Board want to consider water-holding work beyond the plateau, given that most of the farm is set aside for nature.
- How deep and how long the valley along the south-western side of the Residential Zone is, and where it joins the west valley.
- Which of the gullies and valleys count as watercourses in law.
- Where the 2021 botanical assessment places sensitive vegetation, and how each layout sits against it.
- Whether the neighbour on the south-western boundary would allow the boundary valley to be measured.
- How the layouts fit the farm's fire plan and its firebreaks.

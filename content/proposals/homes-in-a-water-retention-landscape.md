> **This idea is not yet agreed.** The prospective stewards wrote it in October 2026, after a weekend on the land and a desk reading of the farm's contours, and revised it the same month. Nobody else has been asked: not the company, its Community Management Panel (CMP) or its Board, not the residents who look after the ponds and food forest near the cabin, not the household allocated Unit 3, not Hessequa Municipality and not the Breede-Olifants Catchment Management Agency. It will be put to the company through the CMP, together with [[the-houses/index|The Houses]] and [[proposals/first-five-years-on-the-land|The First Five Years on the Land]].

## The goal

> Show, on the ground around the homes, what a properly planned Water Retention Landscape looks like on this land, with the Main Dwelling and its shared building at its centre, close to the other homes and beside the water.

## The Site Development Plan as a homestead

The approved Site Development Plan works well as a homestead. It places seven homes in one loose cluster near the farm's working heart, as the 2024 approval asks [IC], and Layout D shows that most of them have reasonable ground [TBV]. The [[proposals/first-five-years-on-the-land|First Five Years]] proposal asks a larger question of the same land: what a properly planned Water Retention Landscape could look like here, measured well enough to show neighbours, researchers and water funders. This proposal answers that question for the ground around the homes. It keeps six of the seven homes where the plan puts them. It adds the ponds and swales, and it moves the Main Dwelling, which is not yet allocated [MS], to the middle of them.

## Why the land needs it

- **The cabin already sits at the head of a chain of ponds.** The family's map marks an existing pond just south of the cabin, and two more ponds beside it that a neighbour keeps [MS]. On the contour model they stand at about 416, 412 and 410 metres, falling towards the gully [TBV].
- **A dry gully starts at the cabin.** It runs north-west below the Residential Zone, falling from about 410 metres beside the cabin to below 340 metres north of Unit 1 [TBV]. It carries no water today [MS].
- **The ground north of the homes is a long sloping shelf.** From north of the campsite to Unit 3, the province's contours at 410, 400 and 390 metres lie 50 to 85 metres apart, a slope of about 12 to 20% [TBV]. Below 390 metres the gully side drops at about one in three [TBV]. The family saw a slight indentation in the shelf north of Unit 1 [MS]. It is too small for the 10-metre contours to show.
- **The gully reach the family has marked lies below that shelf.** The family's map marks a reach of about 1.2 hectares as the Proposed Gully Stuff, running from about 388 metres down to about 346 metres [MS]. It can move up or down the gully [MS].
- **Rain on the shelf leaves quickly today.** Without swales or ponds, water running off the north face of the knoll goes straight down the gully side, a few hundred metres from the ponds by the cabin to the stuffed reach [TBV].
- **The Main Dwelling is far from the homes on the approved plan.** Layout D places it beside the cabin, about 440 metres from the nearest home and about 690 metres from the six homes on average [TBV]. The plan's allocation table shows the Main Dwelling as available [MS], and the Main Dwelling may stand almost anywhere [MS]. Its approved 350 m² under roof is in two connected parts: a 200 m² home and a 150 m² shared facility [MS].
- **The community has already named this kind of work.** The constitution lists swales among its conservation priorities, and residents tell the CMP before any major earthworks and get its sign-off [MS].
- **The evidence is open.** No published study tests water-holding earthworks in fynbos or renosterveld, or on land that burns [MS]. [[research/water-retention-evidence|Evidence on Water and Soil in Fynbos and Renosterveld]] sets out what is and is not known.

## The idea

A [Water Retention Landscape](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/concepts/water-retention-landscapes) is land whose soil, plants and earthworks catch rain, hold it and release it slowly. This proposal takes the water that leaves the pond by the cabin and gives it the longest path the ground allows before it reaches the gully. It runs west along the shelf through three ponds, as far as Unit 3. Then it doubles back east along a lower contour to the head of the stuffed reach.

### The water's path

All heights and lengths come from the contour model and are [TBV] until levelled on the ground.

| Step | From and to | Length or size | Height |
|---|---|---|---|
| Existing pond | Beside the cabin | | about 416 m |
| Swale A | Existing pond to Pond 1 | about 200 m | 416 to 413 m |
| Pond 1 | About 100 m due north of the campsite, just outside the Production Zone | about 700 m² | about 412 m |
| Swale B | Along the 411-metre contour, then down to the shelf | about 330 m | 412 to 399 m |
| Pond 2 | On the shelf north of Unit 1, beside the Main Dwelling | about 1,000 m² | about 398 m |
| Channel C | West along the shelf | about 240 m | 398 to 395 m |
| Pond 3 | East of Unit 3, about 17 m outside its Area | about 600 m² | about 395 m |
| Swale D | The return: east along the contour, below Pond 2 and the Main Dwelling | about 450 m | 394 to 389 m |
| Gully stuffing | The head of the Proposed Gully Stuff reach | about 1.2 ha | 388 to 346 m |

1. **Pond 1, north of the campsite.** When the existing pond is full, part of its overflow runs west along Swale A to a new pond on gentle ground just north of the Production Zone. Pond 1 also takes run-off from the ground around the campsite.
2. **Pond 2, at the Main Dwelling.** Swale B follows the 411-metre contour west, catching the rain that runs off the north face of the knoll, then drops to the shelf. Pond 2 is the largest of the three, and the Main Dwelling stands beside it.
3. **Pond 3, near Unit 3.** Channel C carries Pond 2's overflow west along the shelf. Pond 3 sits on steeper ground, about 20%, so it needs an earth wall of perhaps 2 to 3 metres on its downhill side [TBV].
4. **Swale D, the return.** Pond 3's overflow enters a level-sill swale that runs back east along the contour at about 394 metres, falling gently to about 389 metres. It passes below Pond 2 and the Main Dwelling, about 40 metres from the buildings, and soaks water into the lower shelf and the top of the gully side as it goes. What it cannot hold spills down a rock chute into the top of the stuffed reach.
5. **The gully stuffing.** Brush packing, rock check structures and leaky weirs, built by hand, slow each flow, catch sediment and let water soak into the gully's banks and bed. The reach also receives whatever flows down the gully from the ponds by the cabin. Over years, the stored water could seep out between rains and give the gully below a year-round trickle to the river [TBV].

Together the path is about 1.2 kilometres of swale and channel and about 2,300 m² of open water, falling about 27 metres [TBV]. Running straight down the gully, the same water would travel about 400 metres [TBV]. The swales sit across the slope, below every home on the north face of the knoll, so the roofs and tracks of Units 1, 2 and 3 can drain into them.

The Proposed Gully Stuff reach can stay where the family has drawn it. Swale D reaches it at its upper, eastern end, and the overflow of every pond on the shelf ends up there [TBV].

### The Main Dwelling at Pond 2

The home and the shared building stand together on the eastern end of the shelf, at about 400 metres, with Pond 2 to their west and north-west [TBV]. The shared building opens onto the water. From its terrace, people would look across the pond and over the gully to the north, with the winter sun from the north and the evening sun over the water. The knoll behind them to the south breaks the wind from that side.

Both roofs, about 350 m² together, would drain to Pond 2. Each millimetre of rain on that much roof gives about 350 litres. The ponds would also hold water for fighting fire across the north side of the homes. The rain gauge, the pond levels and the record the [[proposals/first-five-years-on-the-land|First Five Years]] proposal asks for would sit in plain view of everyone who uses the shared building.

The Main Dwelling's one-hectare Area is drawn on the shelf, 125 by 80 metres, around the two buildings. It does not overlap any Area of Layout D or the Production Zone [TBV]. The ponds, swales and channel stand outside every Area, on shared land.

### Every home as part of the catchment

Each roof would fill its own tanks first, and the overflow would run to the nearest swale. The tracks between the homes would be shaped to shed water into swales on their downhill side. On the north face of the knoll, those swales lead to Swale B and the ponds. The water each household does not use would go on working on the land.

The homes on the knoll stand about 15 to 20 metres above the shelf, so the walk to the shared building goes down and the walk home comes back up. The model shows the shelf at the Main Dwelling flatter than the contour spacing suggests, because it smooths between lines 10 metres apart. The rise and fall figures there are the least certain in the table.

## What could go wrong

- **Sandy ground may not hold water.** The ground is sandy and very rocky [MS]. Ponds dug in it may drain through their floors. Each pond may need clay, a natural seal or a liner, or it may be designed to fill in winter and soak away through the dry months. A test pit and a percolation test at each pond site would settle this first.
- **The water may not be there.** Nobody has measured how much the existing pond overflows, or what feeds it. If its overflow and the run-off from the knoll are small, the lower ponds may stand dry for much of the year.
- **Swale D runs above a steep slope.** Below it, the gully side falls at about 24 to 37% [TBV]. Water held in a swale above such a slope can make the ground slump. Swale D would need to sit back from the break of slope, with a level sill and rock-armoured spillways, and its eastern end may be better as a few short swales stepping down.
- **Pond 3 needs a wall.** On ground of about 20%, its downhill wall carries the most risk of the three. It could be split into two smaller ponds stepping down the slope.
- **Fire, mosquitoes and safety.** Open water near homes needs gentle edges for children and animals, and planting that does not carry fire to the buildings.

## Who would benefit

- **Residents** would gain a shared building within a few minutes' walk of the homes, three ponds along the north side of the homes for dry months and for fire, and a visible record of how the land holds water.
- **People downstream.** The Duiwenhoks below the dam supplies Overberg Water's scheme for Heidelberg, Slangrivier and Witsand, and the farmers who share the dam's water [IC]. Whether the gully reaches the Duiwenhoks itself, and where, has not been checked [MS]. If a revived stream added to the river, the record would show it.
- **Researchers, neighbours and water funders** would gain a planned Water Retention Landscape in fynbos, on land that burned in 2025, with its records from before the first pond was dug.

## Who would pay

Nothing has been costed. Pond 2 and Swale B could be dug when the Main Dwelling is built, as part of its earthworks. Ponds 1 and 3, Channel C and Swale D could follow in stages, with a small machine and by hand. Gully stuffing is hand work with brush and rock from the farm, and could be built by residents over several seasons. Moving the Main Dwelling would add the cost of amending the Site Development Plan, and of any authorisation the ponds and the gully work need. A university partner or a restoration grant might meet part of the cost of the measuring.

## Who would decide

- **Community matters go to the CMP.** Each pond, swale and stage of the gully work is an earthwork that needs the CMP's sign-off under the constitution [MS]. The CMP advises the Board [MS].
- **Legal matters go to the Board.** Moving the Main Dwelling's Area needs every founding shareholder to agree, and an Area can be moved only before building on it starts [MS]. Any request to amend the Site Development Plan, and any application for a water use or an environmental authorisation, would be made by the company as owner.
- **The neighbour who keeps the ponds by the cabin** would need to agree to any change in how water reaches or leaves them.
- **The household allocated Unit 3** would need to agree to Pond 3 and Channel C near its Area. Unit 3 is allocated [MS].
- **Hessequa Municipality** would decide on an amended Site Development Plan [TBV]. The 2024 approval asks for buildings in a small number of clusters, on ground already disturbed near the farm homestead [IC].
- **The Breede-Olifants Catchment Management Agency** would say whether the ponds and the gully work are water uses that need authorisation, and which can go ahead under a general authorisation [TBV].

## What permissions might apply

- Storing water, impeding or diverting the flow of water in a watercourse, and altering the bed or banks of a watercourse are each water uses under section 21 of the National Water Act [IC]. A gully that flows only after heavy rain may still count as a watercourse [TBV]. The three ponds would store water on the shelf, outside the gully's channel, so only the chute and the stuffing would work in the gully itself [TBV].
- Building with a footprint over 100 m² within 32 metres of a watercourse can trigger environmental authorisation under the 2014 listing notices [TBV]. The two buildings would stand about 75 metres from the top of the gully side and about 175 metres from its floor [TBV].
- Part of the approved footprint lies in an area of medium-high botanical sensitivity [IC]. How the shelf, the swale routes and the pond sites sit against the 2021 botanical assessment has not been seen.

## How it fits the First Five Years

[[proposals/first-five-years-on-the-land|The First Five Years on the Land]] asks for two years of records before any water trial, untreated land kept for comparison, and no work near watercourses until any authorisation is confirmed. This proposal keeps that order and gives the trials one planned design:

- **Now:** fix the places of the three ponds, the swales and the Main Dwelling in the plan.
- **Years 1 and 2:** record the shelf and the gully with erosion pins, photo points, soil moisture probes and rain gauges. Log the existing pond's overflow after each heavy rain. Dig test pits at the three pond sites.
- **With the Main Dwelling:** dig Pond 2 and Swale B, with the CMP's sign-off.
- **From year 3:** add Pond 1, Pond 3, Channel C and Swale D in stages, and stuff the gully reach once any authorisation is in place. Each stage is measured against the record before it, and a stretch further down the gully stays untreated for comparison.

## What is still open

- Whether the company, its CMP and its Board want to consider this idea. None have been approached yet.
- What feeds the existing pond, how much it overflows today, and whether the neighbour who keeps the ponds beside it would welcome part of that water being turned west.
- Whether the sandy ground at each pond site will hold water.
- How flat the shelf really is at the Main Dwelling, where the indentation lies, and whether that ground has been disturbed before.
- Whether the household allocated Unit 3 would welcome a pond beside its Area.
- Whether the gully reaches the Duiwenhoks, and whether it counts as a watercourse in law.
- Whether the municipality would accept a Main Dwelling about 450 metres from the homestead as meeting the clustering condition, and how long an amendment would take against the September 2029 deadline [IC].

## What happens next

1. Ask the neighbour who keeps the ponds by the cabin, and the residents who look after the existing pond, what feeds the ponds and how they behave in heavy rain.
2. Walk the whole path with a dumpy level or a water level, from the existing pond's spillway through the three pond sites to the head of the gully reach, and dig a test pit at each pond site.
3. Redraw the ponds, swales and buildings from those heights, and add the result to [[the-houses/index|The Houses]] as a fifth layout.
4. Ask the household allocated Unit 3 about Pond 3.
5. Ask the Breede-Olifants Catchment Management Agency whether the gully is a watercourse and what the ponds, the chute and the stuffing would need.
6. Ask the municipality whether moving the Main Dwelling needs an amended plan.
7. Bring the idea, the heights and the answers to the CMP.

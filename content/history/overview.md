Isikhova & Ironwood lies on the Duiwenhoks River near Heidelberg [MS]. It is portion 10 of the farm Broken Hill No. 88 [IC], about 700 metres downstream of the [[the-land/duiwenhoks-river-and-dam|Duiwenhoks Dam]] [MS]. This page sets out what the written record says about the river valley around the farm, from the Khoekhoe herders who grazed it before 1652 to the dam that regulates the river today. It covers the district and the river. The history of the farm's own title has yet to be traced, and *What is still open* lists that work first.

The written record for most of this period was made by outsiders: [Dutch East India Company](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/history/institutions/dutch-east-india-company) officials, travellers, surveyors and later colonial administrators. Where the page relies on their accounts, it says so. Descendants of the Khoekhoe who lived here may live in the district today, and their own accounts correct this page. The key to the letters in square brackets is on [[about|About This Site]].

## The river

The Duiwenhoks River rises in the Langeberg, inside what is now the [[the-land/boosmansbos-wilderness-area|Boosmansbos Wilderness Area]], and flows south past Heidelberg to the Indian Ocean [IC]. Heidelberg stands on its banks [IC]. The Duiwenhoks Dam sits on the river upstream of the town and holds about 6.2 million cubic metres at full supply [IC].

## Before the Company

Before 1652 the district lay within the grazing lands of [Khoekhoe](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/history/tribes/khoekhoe) herders [IC]. The [Hessequa](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/history/tribes/hessequa) pastured cattle and sheep along the southern Cape from present-day Swellendam to Albertinia and beyond, a range that takes in the Duiwenhoks valley [IC]. Richard Elphick, as cited in a 2006 heritage assessment for Heidelberg, describes the coastal belt from the mountains behind the Cape to the Keurbooms River as the grazing ground of the Hessequa and the [Chainoqua](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/history/tribes/chainoqua) [TBV]. Sources place the boundary between the two peoples in different places, and none located for this page says which group used the Duiwenhoks valley itself [MS].

Hunter-gatherers, whom Dutch writers later called [Soaqua](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/history/tribes/soaqua), used the Langeberg [TBV]. No source located places a particular group inside what is now Boosmansbos [MS]. The name Boosmansbos means "angry man's forest" and comes from a hermit who lived in the forest in the early nineteenth century [IC]. It tells us nothing about who used the forest before colonial times.

The local municipality today carries the Hessequa name [IC].

## Trade and disease

From the 1660s the Company sent expeditions east across the mountains to [barter cattle](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/history/markets/cattle-trade) from the Hessequa; the first recorded contact was in 1660 [IC]. One account names the Hessequa and the Chainoqua as the main cattle suppliers to the Cape station from 1662 to 1713 [TBV]. In 1689 Ensign Isaq Schrijver led an expedition east to trade for cattle [IC]. Several accounts credit him with naming the Duiwenhoks River on that expedition [TBV].

In 1713 a smallpox epidemic, carried ashore from a passing ship, killed most of the Khoekhoe of the south-western Cape [IC]. Further epidemics followed in 1755 [IC] and 1767 [TBV]. The sources checked for this page do not record how far the 1713 epidemic reached the Hessequa of this district [MS]. The economic historians Alan Dye and Sumner La Croix argue that the fall in Khoekhoe numbers and their weakened ability to resist made it cheaper for the Company to let settlers spread across the interior [TBV].

## Loan farms and title

By 1714 the Company was issuing land beyond the settled districts as [loan farms](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/history/markets/loan-farms-and-quitrent) [IC]. A loan farm holder paid the Company an annual rent for the right to graze and live on the land, and the Company kept the title [IC].

Doornboom was the loan farm on which Heidelberg later grew [IC]. The Receiver of Land Revenue's register records it leased to Andries Gous for grazing in 1730, and records Louis Fourie as holder of its grazing licence by 1733 [IC]. The Doornboom homestead stands on the western bank of the Duiwenhoks [TBV].

In 1813 a proclamation by Governor [Sir John Cradock](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/history/people/john-cradock) let loan farm holders apply to convert their farms to perpetual quitrent, a surveyed grant held in return for a fixed annual rent [IC]. Few did so at first: by 1825 fewer than a quarter of loan farmers had converted, according to the historian Leslie Duly [TBV]. Doornboom converted in 1823, when it was granted in perpetual quitrent to Louis Johannes Fourie, great-grandson of the Louis Fourie who had held its licence, as a farm in the district of Swellendam [IC]. Quitrent payments across the Cape were abolished by the Abolition of Quitrent Act of 1934 [IC].

## A church town on Doornboom

In 1855 a [Dutch Reformed](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/history/institutions/dutch-reformed-church) congregation was founded for the farmers between Swellendam and Riversdale, and the town took the name Heidelberg [IC]. In 1857 Louis Fourie subdivided Doornboom and sold the portion on the western bank of the Doorn River to the church wardens, to lay out the village and to serve as its commonage [IC]. The transfer was registered in April 1858 [IC]. In 1901 the commonage passed to the Municipality of Heidelberg, which divided it into irrigation lots [IC].

The sources checked for this page record no mission station in the Duiwenhoks valley [MS]. The nearest mission recorded in them is Suurbraak, west of the valley in the Langeberg, founded in 1812 by the London Missionary Society at the invitation of Khoekhoe led by Captain Hans Moos [IC]. The Moravian mission at Genadendal lies further west, in the Riviersonderend valley [MS]. The Ward 2 Development Forum register holds the full history of [Genadendal](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/villages/genadendal) and of the [Moravian Church](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/history/institutions/moravian-church).

## Mountain and water in the twentieth century

Boosmansbos was demarcated as a State Forest in 1896 [TBV]. It was proclaimed a Wilderness Area in 1978 and became part of the Cape Floral Region World Heritage Site in 2004 [IC]. It is managed today as part of the [[the-land/grootvadersbosch-nature-reserve|Grootvadersbosch Nature Reserve]] [IC]. Adjoining private mountain land was proclaimed a Mountain Catchment Area in 1981 [TBV].

The Duiwenhoks Dam was built in the early 1960s; one local account gives construction from 1960 to 1963, and another source gives completion in 1965 [TBV]. Farmers below the dam share its water through an [[institutions/duiwenhoks-irrigation-board|irrigation board]], whose water bailiff divides the supply between grazing and vegetable farmers [TBV]. The research file behind this page gives annual allocations of about 3.7 million cubic metres for irrigation, 1.1 million for a rural water scheme and 1 million for Heidelberg [TBV].

Under the [National Water Act](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/rules/national-water-act) of 1998, the national government holds the country's water resources as public trustee [IC].

## What is still open

- The chain of title for Broken Hill No. 88 and its portion 10, back to the first grant of the farm. The Deeds Office and the Surveyor-General in Cape Town hold the diagrams and transfers that answer this.
- What water use the river supports on the farm, which lies below the dam [MS]. This concerns the company, and the page will say nothing on it until the company has replied.
- Which Khoekhoe people used this stretch of the river, and where their descendants went. Mission registers at Suurbraak and Genadendal may hold names and places of origin.
- Whether Schrijver named the Duiwenhoks. His journal of 1689 is published and can settle it.
- The 1896 State Forest and 1981 Mountain Catchment Area dates, which rest on one document.
- Whether any land restitution claim touches the farm. The research file found none on the wilderness area or the dam in the public record it searched; nobody has yet checked the farm itself.
- The accounts of Hessequa descendants in the district, which this page has not yet heard.

A summary of Western Cape floods gathered for this site lists flood events in 13 seasons between 2003 and 2026, with a list of the sources behind it [MS]. Most of the floods were brought by cut-off lows or strong cold fronts [TBV]. In many of them the damage to land came from water running off bare or disturbed ground: topsoil washed from fields, riverbanks torn away, and rockfalls and mudslides on slopes stripped by fire [TBV]. This page sets out that record, what the sources confirm or dispute, and what is known of floods in Hessequa and the Duiwenhoks valley.

Each source in the list was opened and checked against the claim it was cited for, where its link worked. A claim carries [IC] when two independent sources agree or an official record confirms it. It carries [TBV] when only one source confirms it, or when the source could not be opened. The *Sources checked* column names the sources that were read; the References at the end give the links. The key to the letters in square brackets is on [[about|About This Site]].

## The record

| Year | What happened | Damage to soil, slopes and riverbanks | Sources checked |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2003 | **Montagu, March.** A cut-off low brought 178 mm of rain in a day [TBV]. The Kogmans River rose to levels not seen in more than 70 years, and part of the Bellair Dam wall gave way [TBV]. A newspaper report the next day described flooding in Montagu, Ashton, Robertson, Knysna, Hermanus and Riversdale, with three deaths [TBV]. Losses in Montagu have been put at R238.3 million [TBV]. | Fast overland flow tore up growing vegetation and eroded the Kogmans River catchment [TBV]. | IOL, 25 March 2003; de Waal (2012), citing the Disaster Mitigation for Sustainable Livelihoods Programme (DiMP) |
| 2004 to 2005 | **Garden Route District, December 2004 to January 2005.** A cut-off low brought widespread flooding [TBV]. The December 2004 event did an estimated R57.9 million of damage in the district, then called Eden [TBV]. | Overland flow tore up vegetation and caused severe erosion, worse where farming had removed vegetation [TBV]. | de Waal (2012), citing DiMP |
| 2005 | **Overberg, April.** Rivers burst their banks and towns were cut off [TBV]. About 3,000 people were affected in Bredasdorp, one of the worst-hit towns [TBV]. Damage in the Overberg and Karoo districts has been put at R8.9 million [TBV]. | Part of a run of floods from 2003 to 2008 that damaged riverbanks and farm soil again and again [TBV]. | Mail & Guardian, 12 April 2005; de Waal (2012), citing DiMP |
| 2006 | **Winter storms, May and August.** In May, rockfalls struck the Huisrivier Pass between Calitzdorp and Ladismith and the Outeniqua Pass between Oudtshoorn and George [TBV]. In August, floods in the Southern Cape washed away the Kaaimans River bridge near George [TBV]. | Rockfalls on mountain passes [TBV]. | Mail & Guardian, 21 May 2006 and 26 November 2007 |
| 2007 | **Cape Winelands, Overberg, Garden Route and Central Karoo, 19 to 24 November.** The Western Cape Government's preliminary estimate of losses was R746 million [IC]. Its verified losses came to about R1.18 billion, three times the losses of the August 2006 floods [IC]. Two people drowned in the Garden Route, and more than 1,500 were evacuated [TBV]. | Further damage to riverbanks, and loss of topsoil [TBV]. | Western Cape Government report to Cabinet, 2007; Western Cape Government verified losses, 2008; Mail & Guardian, 26 November 2007 |
| 2008 | **Western Cape, July and November.** In July about 16,000 people were displaced, most of them on the Cape Flats [TBV]. In November floods in the Hex River and Breede River valleys did damage estimated at about R1 billion, washed away about 80 hectares of table grapes, destroyed bridges and damaged farm workers' homes [TBV]. | Extreme erosion along many foothill rivers, and heavy damage to riverbanks [TBV]. | Fruitnet, November 2008 |
| 2012 | **Western Cape, 13 to 14 July and 8 to 10 August.** On 15 July rocks fell on Sir Lowry's Pass, striking two cars and lightly injuring 12 people, and the N2 was closed for a time [TBV]. | The provincial Minister reported that many rivers burst their banks, several irrigation dams failed and many riverbanks were washed away, and that these areas needed urgent protection to prevent a large loss of farm soil [IC]. | Mail & Guardian, 16 July 2012; Pharoah (2025), quoting the Minister's 2012 report |
| 2016 | **Cape Town, June and July.** Flooding affected between 10,000 and 15,000 people [TBV]. | People in mountain areas were warned of the danger of landslides [TBV]. Slopes stripped by recent fires were at higher risk of rockfalls and mudslides [TBV]. | Chinese Consulate General, Cape Town, 27 July 2016 |
| 2018 | **Cape Town, June and July.** On 1 June about 150 homes were affected [TBV]. | Mudslides were reported in Hout Bay and Gordon's Bay, and rockfalls on Chapman's Peak Drive [TBV]. Mudslides later closed Bainskloof Pass [TBV]. | TimesLive, 1 June 2018 |
| 2021 | **Overberg and Cape Winelands, 5 to 6 May.** Very heavy rain and strong wind did serious damage, and rivers flooded [IC]. | Rill erosion washed away crops and topsoil, including in newly planted grain fields near Riviersonderend [IC]. Fields worked before planting were most exposed; residue cover could have reduced the damage [IC]. | GreenAgri SmartAgri newsletter, June 2021 |
| 2023 | **Western Cape, June and September.** A rapid assessment of the June floods by the provincial Department of Agriculture put damage to farming at about R1 billion, including R278 million in crop losses and R748 million to rehabilitate rivers, riverbanks, vineyards and orchards; two people died [IC]. Storms in late September 2023 killed at least 11 people [TBV]. | In June large volumes of sediment were washed from upstream and dumped on riverbanks, vineyards and orchards [IC]. After the September storms, Franschhoek Pass and Clarence Drive had significant landslips, and the Franschhoek Pass needed slope stabilisation [IC]. | SAnews, 14 July 2023, 5 October 2023 and 24 November 2023; Western Cape Government, 30 October 2025 |
| 2024 | **Western Cape, July and August.** Storms from 5 to 16 July damaged roads and services across the province [TBV]. On 8 August farm dams on the property known as Riverlands in the Swartland gave way, flooding homes downstream [IC]. | A preliminary national report found that three dams failed and a fourth nearly failed [IC]. None met dam safety standards, none was registered, and none had an adequate spillway [IC]. One failed through internal erosion after it filled beyond its safe level, and its failure cascaded down to the dams below [IC]. | Government of South Africa, 9 August 2024; SAnews, 22 and 29 August 2024 |
| 2026 | **Western Cape, May.** A national disaster was declared after days of extreme rain [IC]. In the Garden Route at least 45 roads were closed [TBV], and the district's head of disaster management called it the worst flooding there in 30 years [IC]. | Landslides, mudslides and slope failures, and erosion of road embankments [TBV]. | Xinhua, 9 May 2026; The Watchers, 12 May 2026 |

## Where the sources disagree

| Question | What the sources say | Where this page stands |
|---|---|---|
| Losses in November 2007 | The flood summary gives more than R631 million. The Western Cape Government's preliminary estimate was R746 million, and its verified losses about R1.18 billion. A 2012 thesis, citing DiMP, gives more than R830 million for the Garden Route, Overberg and Cape Winelands. | The government's verified figure [IC]. |
| Rain at Montagu in March 2003 | The flood summary gives 178 mm in a day. A newspaper report the next day estimated 250 to 300 mm by late afternoon. | Both given [TBV]. |
| Losses in November 2008 | Fruitnet gives about R1 billion. The 2012 thesis, citing DiMP, gives R791.2 million for the Cape Winelands and Overberg. | Both given [TBV]. |
| The Riverlands dams, August 2024 | The flood summary and a Daily Maverick headline say four dams burst. The national preliminary report says three failed and a fourth nearly failed and was partly emptied. | The national report [IC]. |
| Rockfalls in 2006 | The flood summary says rockfalls closed mountain roads. The Mail & Guardian report on the May rockfalls says the roads were not closed. | Rockfalls without closures, for May 2006 [TBV]. |
| Damage to farming in June 2023 | The flood summary gives about US$50 million. The provincial Department of Agriculture's assessment gives about R1 billion. | The Department's assessment [IC]. |
| Franschhoek Pass, 2023 | SAnews ties the landslips to the September storms. The Western Cape Government's 2025 report on the repairs ties them to rain in October. | September and October storms [IC]. |

Across the eight severe weather events from 2003 to 2008, losses have been put at about R2.5 billion, with farming bearing up to R1 billion [TBV]. A 2021 study of the province's coastal cities found a significant rise in the number of floods and in the human and economic losses they cause [TBV].

## Near the farm

The record above says little about Hessequa or the Duiwenhoks valley. The sources checked for this page add these points.

- A 2012 study of extreme rainfall in the Western Cape names Heidelberg among the places that have had intense floods, and draws on rainfall stations at Heidelberg and at Strawberry Hill in the Duiwenhoks catchment [TBV].
- In March 2003 more than 200 people in Riversdale were moved to a sports centre after their homes were damaged [TBV].
- In November 2007 the Western Cape Government verified R33.3 million of losses in Hessequa Municipality [IC]. Hessequa's council supported the call to declare the Garden Route District a disaster area [IC].
- In December 2003 visitors found that an old bridge over the Duiwenhoks River near Heidelberg had been washed away [TBV]. They did not say which flood took it [MS].
- The source list compiled for the flood summary says the Duiwenhoks Dam overtopped during the 2008 floods [TBV]. The document it links for this claim is a different one, and it does not mention the dam [MS].
- In May 2026 one of the deaths in the Western Cape was in Genadendal, in the Riviersonderend valley, from a falling tree [TBV].
- [[institutions/capenature|CapeNature]] warns hikers in [[the-land/boosmansbos-wilderness-area|Boosmansbos]] that the Duiwenhoks can flood after heavy rain [IC].

The [[institutions/grootvadersbosch-conservancy|Grootvadersbosch Conservancy]] reports stretches of the Duiwenhoks where the floodplain is severely eroded, with high, deeply cut banks [TBV]. Whether recent floods made those cuts is not recorded [MS]. [[the-land/the-lands-story|The Land's Story]] and [[the-land/cape-water-bond|The Cape Water Bond and Clearing Around the Farm]] set out why the condition of the soil and the riverbanks matters for the land.

## What is still open

- The Disaster Mitigation for Sustainable Livelihoods Programme's consolidated report on the March 2003 floods and its 2010 review of the floods of 2003 to 2008. Neither could be opened for this page, and many of the summary's figures rest on them.
- Whether the Duiwenhoks Dam spilled or overtopped in 2008 or in any other flood, and how high the river has risen at the farm.
- Which floods reached the Duiwenhoks valley and the farm, and what they did to its banks, slopes and roads. Residents' memories and Hessequa Municipality's disaster records would fill this gap.
- Which flood washed away the old Duiwenhoks bridge.
- Where the flood summary's figure of R631 million for November 2007 comes from.

The Wilderness Nature Reserve covers about 643.6 hectares on two portions of the farm Klip Hoogte No. 83, north of Heidelberg [IC]. It was declared a Provincial Nature Reserve in July 2025 [IC]. CapeNature and the landowners worked together on the declaration, and CapeNature manages the reserve [IC]. The reserve lies about 100 metres north of the farm [MS], and it forms part of the catchment of the Duiwenhoks Dam [IC].

The page names no landowners. CapeNature and the owners of the reserve land have not yet been asked to comment on this page. The key to the letters in square brackets is on [[about|About This Site]].

## What the declaration covers

The reserve was declared under the National Environmental Management: Protected Areas Act of 2003 [IC]. It is made up of Portion 7 (Klein Kamp), measuring 117.6304 hectares, and Portion 15 (Wilderness), measuring 526.0115 hectares, of the farm Klip Hoogte No. 83 [IC]. About 615 hectares of the reserve are designated a Priority Biodiversity Area [IC].

[[the-land/neighbours|Neighbours and the Conservation Land Around the Farm]] sets out where the reserve's parcels sit relative to the farm, and where the sources disagree on the declaration date and the extent of Portion 7.

## Why it matters

- **Water.** The reserve forms part of a critical catchment for the [[the-land/duiwenhoks-river-and-dam|Duiwenhoks Dam]], which supplies water to people downstream [IC].
- **A corridor.** The reserve links directly to the [[the-land/boosmansbos-wilderness-area|Boosmansbos Wilderness Area]] [IC].
- **Fish.** It holds the Breede River redfin, which CapeNature describes as Critically Endangered [IC]. The river page sets out how the redfin's status is listed.
- **Birds.** It is a breeding site for the black harrier, which CapeNature describes as the rarest bird of prey in southern Africa and lists as Endangered [IC].

Announcing the declaration, the Western Cape's Minister of Local Government, Environmental Affairs and Development Planning said the reserve benefits the people who depend on the ecosystem services it provides [IC].

## The catchment above the dam

In April 2023 volunteers working with CapeNature cleared invasive pines from the upper catchment of the Duiwenhoks Dam, some of them lowered on ropes beneath a helicopter [TBV]. The project brought together CapeNature, the [[institutions/helihack-initiative|HeliHack Initiative]], landowners, the [[institutions/grootvadersbosch-conservancy|Grootvadersbos Conservancy]], Husqvarna and volunteers [TBV]. The sources checked do not say whether the clearing took place on land that is now in the reserve [MS]. [[the-land/cape-water-bond|The Cape Water Bond and Clearing Around the Farm]] describes the clearing since then and what it could mean for the catchment.

## What is still open

- Which of CapeNature's stewardship options the declaration used, and what it asks of the landowners.
- Whether the new Portion 7 brings the reserve up to the farm's boundary.
- Which vegetation types the reserve protects. CapeNature's announcement does not list them.
- Whether the 2023 pine clearing took place on reserve land.

The Boosmansbos Wilderness Area covers 14,200 hectares of the Langeberg, about 22 km north-west of Heidelberg [IC]. The Duiwenhoks River and its tributary, the Noukrans, rise there [IC]. CapeNature manages the wilderness area as part of Grootvadersbosch Nature Reserve [IC]. It lies north of the farm, and the family's map places one of its parcels on the farm's north-western boundary [TBV].

CapeNature has not yet been asked to comment on this page. The key to the letters in square brackets is on [[about|About This Site]].

## How it is protected

| Date | Event | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| 1896 | Demarcated as a State Forest | [TBV] |
| 1978 | Proclaimed a Wilderness Area | [IC] |
| 2004 | Included in the Cape Floral Region Protected Areas World Heritage Site | [IC] |
| 2015 | Included in the Gouritz Cluster Biosphere Reserve, declared by UNESCO that year | [TBV] |

CapeNature manages the area to "maintain its natural systems and to safeguard its pristine environment" [IC]. Entry is on foot only. No more than 12 people may enter a day, hikers choose their own routes across about 64 km of unmarked paths, and fires are not permitted [IC].

BirdLife South Africa includes Boosmansbos in its Langeberg Important Bird Area [TBV]. The Centre for Environmental Rights names it among the protected areas of the [[the-land/langeberg-water-source-area|Langeberg Water Source Area]] [TBV].

## Climate and ground

The Langeberg lies where the winter-rainfall region meets the all-year rainfall region [IC]. Boosmansbos receives an average of 1,050 mm of rain a year, with drier spells from May to July and from December to January [IC]. Hot, dry berg winds blow from May to July [IC]. The highest peak is Grootberg, at 1,637 metres [IC].

Most of the soils come from Table Mountain sandstone [IC]. Isolated pockets of Bokkeveld shale carry the indigenous forests [IC].

## Plants and animals

The vegetation is mountain fynbos, with forest in the shale pockets and sheltered kloofs [IC]. The fynbos of the Langeberg is dominated by the protea, heath and restio families [IC]. Rare heaths in Boosmansbos include *Erica blenna*, *Erica barrydalensis* and *Erica langebergensis* [IC]. The rare Langeberg rambling aloe also grows there [TBV].

Mammals recorded include grey rhebuck, klipspringer, grysbok, baboon, leopard, mongoose and genet [IC]. CapeNature lists 184 bird species, among them Verreaux's (black), martial, crowned and booted eagles [IC].

## Why it matters to the river

Boosmansbos holds the source of the Duiwenhoks [IC]. The river flows from it to the [[the-land/duiwenhoks-river-and-dam|Duiwenhoks Dam]], which supplies Heidelberg, Slangrivier and Witsand [IC]. Below the dam it passes the farm [MS]. CapeNature warns hikers that the Duiwenhoks can flood after heavy rain [IC].

BirdLife South Africa names invasive alien plants, above all cluster pine, as the main threat to the Langeberg, along with fire regimes that have changed [TBV]. In April 2023 volunteers working with CapeNature cleared invasive pines from the upper catchment of the dam [TBV]. [[the-land/neighbours|Neighbours and the Conservation Land Around the Farm]] describes that work, and [[the-land/cape-water-bond|The Cape Water Bond and Clearing Around the Farm]] sets out the later clearing and how it relates to the Cape Water bond.

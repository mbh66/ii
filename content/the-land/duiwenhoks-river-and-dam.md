The Duiwenhoks River rises in the Langeberg, in the [[the-land/boosmansbos-wilderness-area|Boosmansbos Wilderness Area]], and flows south for about 83 km to the Indian Ocean [IC]. Its catchment covers about 1,340 square kilometres [IC]. The Duiwenhoks Dam, built on the river in the 1960s, holds about 6.2 million cubic metres [IC] and supplies Heidelberg, Slangrivier and Witsand [IC]. The farm lies on the river about 700 metres downstream of the dam [MS].

This page describes the river and the dam as parts of the land around the farm. What water use the river supports on the farm itself concerns the company, and this site will say nothing on it until the company has replied. CapeNature, the Department of Water and Sanitation, the Breede-Olifants Catchment Management Agency, Overberg Water and the Duiwenhoks irrigation board have not yet been asked to comment on this page. The key to the letters in square brackets is on [[about|About This Site]].

## The river

The Duiwenhoks and its tributary, the Noukrans, rise on the southern slopes of the Langeberg [IC]. The catchment receives rain throughout the year, with peaks in autumn and spring [IC]. [[institutions/capenature|CapeNature]] warns hikers that the river can flood after heavy rain [IC]. The [[the-land/wilderness-nature-reserve|Wilderness Nature Reserve]], declared in July 2025, forms part of the catchment above the dam [IC].

The Department of Water and Sanitation gauged the river at a station named "Duiwenhoks River at Broken Hill", about 2 km south-west of the farm, from 1972 to 1984 [IC].

## Fish

The river holds a population of the Breede River redfin, a small freshwater fish found only in the Breede, Duiwenhoks and Goukou river systems [IC]. The South African National Biodiversity Institute's 2018 assessment counts the Duiwenhoks system as one of at least seven locations where the fish survives, and treats the Duiwenhoks population as viable [IC]. That assessment lists the Breede River redfin as Vulnerable in South Africa [IC]. CapeNature's 2025 statement on the Wilderness Nature Reserve calls the redfin there Critically Endangered [IC]. The table under *Where the sources disagree* sets out the two listings.

The 2018 assessment names these threats to the fish [IC]:

- Non-native fish, among them smallmouth bass, black bass, rainbow trout and sharptooth catfish.
- Heavy abstraction of water, especially while the fish spawn and their eggs hatch.
- Pollution and disturbance from farming.
- Higher temperatures and lower flows as the climate changes.

## The dam

The Duiwenhoks Dam lies on the river upstream of Heidelberg [IC]. The national water department, today the Department of Water and Sanitation, built it in the early 1960s; the sources give different dates [TBV]. It holds about 6.2 million cubic metres at full supply [IC]. The dam fills quickly after rain from the south-east, according to the irrigation board's water bailiff in 2018 [TBV]. It was overflowing in early October 2026 [TBV].

The dam's water is shared two ways.

- **Towns and rural users.** Overberg Water pumps water from the river below the dam for its Duivenhoks scheme [IC]. The scheme supplies Heidelberg, Slangrivier and Witsand, and rural households, livestock and irrigation [IC]. Its registered abstraction is 1,232,000 cubic metres a year [IC]. Hessequa Municipality takes nearly 2 million litres a day from the scheme for its towns [IC]. [[institutions/overberg-water|Overberg Water]] gives the detail.
- **Irrigation.** The Duiwenhoks irrigation board shares water from the dam among grazing and vegetable farmers, through a water bailiff [TBV]. [[institutions/duiwenhoks-irrigation-board|The Duiwenhoks Irrigation Board]] gives what is known.

Overberg Water's 2021/22 annual report estimates that the old irrigation canal from the dam loses 30 to 50 percent of its flow [IC]. The same report describes a proposed gravity pipeline from the dam to Heidelberg, costed at R195 million in 2021/22 [IC]. The [[history/overview|historical overview]] gives annual allocations recorded in the family's research file [TBV].

## The estuary

The Duiwenhoks reaches the sea through a permanently open estuary between Riversdale and Heidelberg [IC]. The river passes the hamlet of Vermaaklikheid on the way [TBV]. A conservancy works to keep the estuary's character [TBV]. Volunteers have counted the waterbirds at the river mouth for the Coordinated Waterbird Counts since at least 2002 [IC].

In 2014 the Department of Water and Sanitation completed an intermediate assessment of the estuary's ecological Reserve, the water it needs to stay healthy [IC]. The report could not be opened for this page, so its findings are not given here.

## What is still open

- What the 2014 Reserve study found about the estuary's condition, and how much of the river's natural flow now reaches it.
- Whether the Reserve determinations for the Breede-Gouritz water management area, published in August 2022, cover the Duiwenhoks, and how much water the dam releases to meet them.
- Whether redfin live in the stretch of river past the farm, and which non-native fish are there.
- Whether the irrigation board has become a water user association under the National Water Act.
- What water use the river supports on the farm. This waits for the company's reply.

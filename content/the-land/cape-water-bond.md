The Cape Water Performance-Based Bond is a R2.5 billion bond issued by FirstRand Bank and listed on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange in April 2026 under the code FR31PB [IC]. It pays for clearing invasive plants from priority catchments so that more water flows into storage dams [IC]. Part of what its investors earn depends on independently verified results [IC]. Its work is in the Boland Grootwinterhoek and the other catchments that supply the Greater Cape Town region [IC]. The Duiwenhoks catchment is not among them [MS].

Clearing of the same kind is already under way above the farm. Volunteer teams working with CapeNature have cut invasive pines from the Boosmansbos Wilderness Area, on the slopes that feed the Duiwenhoks River and its dam [IC]. This page sets out how the bond works, what clearing is happening in and around the farm, and what links the two.

Rand Merchant Bank, FirstRand, The Nature Conservancy, Conservation Alpha, CapeNature, the HeliHack Initiative, and the Grootvadersbosch Conservancy have not yet been asked to comment on this page. The key to the letters in square brackets is on [[about|About This Site]].

## How the bond works

| Item | Detail | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Issuer | FirstRand Bank | [IC] |
| Arranger | Rand Merchant Bank (RMB), part of FirstRand, which structured and distributed the bond | [IC] |
| Size | R2.5 billion | [IC] |
| Listing | Johannesburg Stock Exchange, April 2026, code FR31PB | [IC] |
| Term | Five years | [IC] |
| Who does the work | [[institutions/the-nature-conservancy\|The Nature Conservancy]] in South Africa, with about R150 million for five years | [IC] |
| Who checks the work | [[institutions/conservation-alpha\|Conservation Alpha]], as independent verification agent | [IC] |
| Where | The Boland Grootwinterhoek and other catchments of the Western Cape Water Supply System, through the [[institutions/greater-cape-town-water-fund\|Greater Cape Town Water Fund]] | [IC] |

Investors receive a regular return like any bond. On top of it, they receive a success payment only when the invasive trees have been cleared and independent verifiers have confirmed the work [IC]. Most of the money for the project comes from outcomes funders, who pay the success payments [IC]. RMB says three quarters of that outcomes funding came from bodies that had never funded nature before [TBV].

The Greater Cape Town Water Fund was set up after Cape Town's drought of 2018 [IC]. By April 2026 it had cleared about 40,000 hectares [IC], toward a goal of 54,300 hectares [TBV]. The Nature Conservancy says the clearing has given back more than 36 billion litres of water, at about a tenth of the cost of desalination [TBV]. The public statements read for this page do not set out the exact measures on which the success payments depend [MS].

The Western Cape's Minister of Local Government, Environmental Affairs and Development Planning called the bond a blueprint, and the provincial Minister of Finance said she hoped it would be the first of many [IC]. RMB has described it as the first of a series meant to cover South Africa's strategic water source areas [TBV].

The BioConomy wiki explains the instrument in more depth on its page on [Performance-Based Water Bonds](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/concepts/performance-based-water-bonds). The Ward 2 Development Forum register reads the bond as a market signal in [The Laws That Shaped the Valley](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/history/laws-that-shaped-the-valley).

## Clearing in and around the farm

| Date | Clearing | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| April 2023 | Volunteers working with CapeNature, some lowered on ropes beneath a helicopter, clear invasive pines from the upper catchment of the Duiwenhoks Dam | [TBV] |
| 2024 | CapeNature formalises its commitment to the [[institutions/helihack-initiative\|HeliHack Initiative]] | [IC] |
| 19 September 2024 | The approval for additional homes on the farm requires a plan to control and remove invasive alien plants | [IC] |
| 3 to 5 May 2025 | A HeliHack operation removes 15,194 pines from 1,080 hectares on the steep western slopes above the Duiwenhoks River in Boosmansbos, with 21 volunteers and 12.8 hours of helicopter time | [IC] |
| By May 2025 | HeliHack operations have removed 26,913 pines from 3,880 hectares, about 27 percent of Boosmansbos | [IC] |
| 2025 and 2026 | The Grootvadersbosch Conservancy reports on the state of the Duiwenhoks River, with data gathered with CapeNature and the catchment management agency | [TBV] |

The Grootvadersbosch Conservancy reports significant invasive vegetation along the upper Duiwenhoks, and says the lower sections of the river are dominated by it [TBV]. It also reports stretches where the floodplain is severely eroded, with high, deeply cut banks [TBV]. It recommends clearing invasive vegetation along the river and its floodplain, stabilising the eroded banks and restoring the plants that grow beside the river [TBV]. The conservancy says it helps its members pay for clearing [TBV].

CapeNature is restoring old plantations of exotic trees at Grootvadersbosch to indigenous forest [IC]. The [[institutions/breede-olifants-catchment-management-agency|Breede-Olifants Catchment Management Agency]] names alien vegetation removal among its priorities [IC].

This site does not know whether any clearing has yet taken place on the farm itself [MS].

## Why the bond matters here

**The same service, in another water source area.** The bond pays for the work the HeliHack teams already do: clearing pines from a strategic water source area above a dam. Boosmansbos lies in the [[the-land/langeberg-water-source-area|Langeberg Water Source Area]] [IC]. The Duiwenhoks Dam supplies Heidelberg, Slangrivier, Witsand and the farms around them, about 15,000 people [IC]. In January 2026 [[institutions/hessequa-municipality|Hessequa Municipality]] asked those towns to use water sparingly because the dam was low [IC]. CapeNature estimates that catchment management and restoration can increase streamflow by 15 to 30 percent [TBV].

**What a later issue would look for.** The Ward 2 Development Forum register and the Valley of Grace BioHub list what a catchment needs before a bond like this can pay for its water: measured flows, a checking method an independent verifier accepts, signed stewardship agreements, and a trained local team [MS]. Around the farm, some of this exists. The HeliHack operations count the pines they remove and the hectares they cover [IC]. Protected land above the dam has grown, with the [[the-land/wilderness-nature-reserve|Wilderness Nature Reserve]] declared in July 2025 [IC]. Other parts are missing. The gauging station on the river near the farm recorded flows only from 1972 to 1984 [IC], no later gauging near the farm has been found for this site [MS], and no study found for this site measures how much water the clearing in Boosmansbos adds to the dam [MS]. Clearing done by volunteers could be paid for, and followed up, if a bond or water fund bought its results.

**Where the farm sits.** The bond pays for water that flows into storage dams [IC]. The farm lies about 700 metres below the Duiwenhoks Dam [MS]. Clearing above the dam, in Boosmansbos and the Wilderness Nature Reserve, fits the bond's design most directly. Clearing on the farm and along the river below the dam would add water to the stretch that Overberg Water pumps from and the irrigation farmers share, and could help the river's redfin and its eroded banks. A buyer for that water would be a downstream user or a fund that values river health, if one comes forward.

**A place to measure.** [[the-land/the-lands-story|The Land's Story]] proposes recording the land as it is and measuring how water-holding work changes it. The same records could show what clearing on and above the farm gives the river. That evidence is what a verifier would ask for.

## What is still open

- Whether RMB plans an issue for the Langeberg or any catchment outside the Western Cape Water Supply System.
- What the bond's success payments measure: hectares cleared, water added, or both, and how Conservation Alpha checks them.
- Whether the HeliHack records meet the standard a verifier would ask for, and who pays for follow-up clearing in Boosmansbos.
- How much water the clearing above the Duiwenhoks Dam has added, and whether anyone is measuring it.
- Whether any clearing has taken place on the farm. This concerns the company, and the site will say nothing more on it until the company has replied.
- What the Grootvadersbosch Conservancy's 2025 and 2026 river reports found in detail. They have not yet been read for this site.

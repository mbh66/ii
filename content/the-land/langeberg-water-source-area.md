Strategic water source areas are the roughly 10 percent of the land of South Africa, Lesotho and eSwatini that supplies about half the water in their rivers [IC]. There are 22 such areas for surface water [IC]. The Langeberg is one of them [IC]. The Duiwenhoks River, which passes the farm, is among the rivers that flow from it [TBV]. The key to the letters in square brackets is on [[about|About This Site]].

The BioConomy wiki defines the term in its entry on the [Strategic Water Source Area](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/glossary/m-s/strategic-water-source-area-swsa).

## The Langeberg in figures

| Measure | South African National Biodiversity Institute (SANBI), 2023 | Centre for Environmental Rights |
|---|---|---|
| Size | 171,527 hectares [IC] | 208,683 hectares [TBV] |
| Dominant biome | Fynbos [IC] | |
| Natural or semi-natural land | 78 percent [IC] | 73 percent [TBV] |
| Formally protected | 48 percent [IC] | About 60 percent [TBV] |
| Runoff | 343 million cubic metres a year [IC] | |

Neither source explains the difference in size. SANBI's figures are from its 2023 factsheet. The Centre for Environmental Rights does not say where its figures come from [MS].

The Centre for Environmental Rights lists the Breede, Gouritz, Doring, Duiwenhoks and Naroo rivers as flowing from the Langeberg area, and Swellendam, Barrydale and Heidelberg among the towns it supplies [TBV]. It counts one national park, three mountain catchment areas, nine forest protected areas and three provincial nature reserves inside the area [TBV]. The protected areas it names include Bontebok National Park, Grootvadersbosch Nature Reserve and the Boosmansbos Wilderness Area [TBV].

## Condition and threats

The Centre for Environmental Rights reports that up to 69 percent of the area's wetland ecosystems and 68 percent of its river ecosystems are critically endangered [TBV]. It names alien plants, climate change and fire as the main threats [TBV].

BirdLife South Africa's account of the Langeberg Important Bird Area names invasive alien plants as the main threat, above all cluster pine, with eucalyptus, hakea, acacias, bugweed and American bramble [TBV]. It adds changed fire regimes, forestry, farming that spreads up the slopes, and some pressure from tourism and off-road routes [TBV]. It records the Langeberg West and Langeberg East Mountain Catchment Areas, of 77,096 and 71,300 hectares, and notes that management responsibility for some catchment and state forest land is uncertain [TBV].

## Securing water source areas

SANBI advises against land uses that reduce streamflow or harm water quality in strategic water source areas, among them mining, timber plantations and overgrazing [IC]. It recommends protection, restoration, good management of land and water, and monitoring, carried out across sectors [IC]. Many of the areas are already under pressure, and some have very little formal protection [IC].

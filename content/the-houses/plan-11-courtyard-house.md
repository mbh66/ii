> **These plans are not agreed.** The Haupt family drew them in October 2026 for discussion among themselves. Nobody outside the family has been asked: not the company, its Community Management Panel (CMP) or its Board, not the residents and not Hessequa Municipality. They will be put to the company through the CMP once the family has agreed what to propose.

## The goal

> Wrap the two halves and their shared kitchen around a courtyard open to the north, under one roof, so the house holds a sheltered outdoor room at its centre from the first day.

## What shaped it

- The family has set a further rule: both halves must be joined under a single roof [MS].
- Each home may have up to 200 m² of floor area [IC], counted as area under roof [MS]. A courtyard open to the sky is neither, so it adds to the house without counting toward the 200 m².
- Plans 9 and 10 showed that one shared kitchenette frees enough space for two bedrooms in each half on one level. This plan keeps that.

The house is a U, 20 metres wide and 14 metres deep, open to the north. Half A is the west wing, Half B the east wing, and the shared core is the south range between them. The courtyard in the middle is 8 by 10 metres, about 80 m², closed on three sides and open to the sun and the view on the fourth.

Each wing is 84 m²:

- **The living room,** 27 m², takes the north end of the wing, with windows to the north and to the courtyard. It is larger than in Plans 9 and 10.
- **Two bedrooms** of about 16 m² each face outward, west in Half A and east in Half B, so the two households' bedrooms look away from each other.
- **A step-free bathroom** of about 11 m² sits between the bedrooms, in both halves.
- **A gallery** 1.5 metres wide runs along the courtyard side, glazed to the courtyard. It is the way through the wing, a sunny place to sit, and it leads to the half's own front door on the south side and to the kitchenette.

The south range holds the shared kitchenette and dining room of 22 m², facing north into the courtyard, with the laundry and the plant room beside it. The kitchenette has the only stove and oven in the house.

Everything is on one level, and every room in both halves is step-free.

One continuous roof covers both wings and the south range. Every slope falls inward to the courtyard, from eaves about 4 metres above the floor on the outside to about 2.7 metres on the courtyard side. Gutters run along the three courtyard edges to two downpipes at the inner corners, and from there to the tanks. All the rain on the house, about 200 litres for each millimetre that falls, gathers in one place.

The roof over the south range faces north, which suits solar panels. The roofs over the wings face east and west, where panels give less [TBV]. The low courtyard eaves let the winter sun into the courtyard, and the high outer walls give the bedrooms tall windows to the outside.

### What Phase 1 lays down for Phase 2

- Water, drainage and power, and a gas line if wanted, run under the slab to the courtyard face of the south range, and capped.
- The courtyard levelled and covered in gravel, with a movable braai, for the first two years.
- The terrace north of the courtyard left as it is, or filled while the machine is on site.

Two years after the family moves in, the courtyard is paved and the outdoor living spreads north from it:

- **The outdoor kitchen** runs along the courtyard's south wall, under the kitchenette windows, with a braai, a wood-fired oven, a counter and a sink at counter height below the sills. Food passes through the window.
- **The courtyard,** 80 m², is sheltered on three sides. Its southern half stays open to the sky, so the winter sun reaches the kitchenette windows at noon on 21 June, when it stands about 33° above the horizon [IC].
- **The pergola,** about 80 m², covers the northern part of the courtyard and the dining terrace beyond it, with a deciduous vine.
- **The dining terrace,** 48 m², extends the courtyard north past the wings, about 0.3 metres lower, with a short ramp.
- **A fire circle** of 36 m² in front of Half A's living room and **a kitchen garden** of 36 m² in front of Half B's.

A stone seat wall along the north edge holds the terrace level, about 1.1 metres high on Unit 1 [TBV]. Rain on the paving runs to the beds and to a swale below the seat wall, as the approval asks for sustainable drainage [IC]. In all there are about 164 m² of paving and 36 m² of beds. The terrace can run further north, or wrap around the outer sides of the wings, as far as the family needs.

## How each household would use it

- **House 1.** Michael and his daughter in one wing, a bedroom each. Veronica in the other, with her second bedroom as a room for visitors or for work.
- **House 2.** Robert and his wife in one wing. Their mother and her partner in the other, with every room, the kitchenette and the courtyard on one level, and a ramp to the terrace.

Each household's gallery faces the other's across 8 metres of courtyard. Planting, blinds or glass that is obscured up to eye height would give each the privacy it wants. Whether both households want to share one kitchen is theirs to decide.

## On the three sites

The site figures come from [[the-houses/layout-d-site-development-plan|Layout D]], which modelled each platform from 10-metre contours. They are [TBV] until the platforms have been walked.

- **Unit 1.** The best fit. The ground falls about 1 metre across the house's 14-metre depth [TBV], taken up by fill under the north ends of the wings.
- **Main Dwelling.** The ground falls about 1.7 metres across the house [TBV], and the terrace's seat wall would be nearly 2 metres high. A fair fit. Part B, the shared building, could close part of the courtyard's north side or stand beside one wing, joined under the same roof.
- **Unit 3.** The ground falls about 3 metres across the house [TBV]. A poor fit.

## Pros

- One roof, one kitchen and one courtyard. Of all the plans it reads most clearly as one home.
- An outdoor room sheltered from the wind on three sides, usable from the first day as gravel and fully in Phase 2, all outside the 200 m².
- The largest living rooms of the plans with two bedrooms in each half, and every room on one level.
- No height approval: the house stands about 4 metres at its highest.
- All the rain gathers in one place, at the two inner corners.
- The outer walls can be solid, with smaller windows, and most of the glass faces the courtyard, farther from the veld if a fire comes.

## Cons

- A U has more outside wall for its floor area than any of the straight plans, which costs more to build and loses more heat.
- The galleries take about 28 m² as circulation.
- The two valleys where the roofs meet, and the gutters on the courtyard side, carry all the house's rain. They need to be generous, with an overflow that keeps a storm out of the courtyard rooms.
- Half A's bedrooms face west and take the hot afternoon sun in summer. They need shade on that side.
- The courtyard is 8 metres wide. It is a sheltered room, smaller than the open terraces of Plans 9 and 10.
- The two galleries look at each other across the courtyard.

## What is still unknown

- Whether the CMP and the shareholders would approve the design and the patio, as landscaping and earthworks need the CMP's sign-off [MS] and the design of every home must be acceptable to all shareholders [MS].
- Whether the pergola needs building plans from the municipality.
- How large the gutters, downpipes and tanks must be for the heaviest rain on record.
- How much less the east and west roofs would give from solar panels.
- Whether the patio counts toward the disturbed footprint named in the approval.
- How the CMP's fire rules would treat a fire circle.

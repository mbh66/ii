Nobody outside the family has been asked: not the company, its Community Management Panel (CMP) or its Board, not the residents and not Hessequa Municipality. They will be put to the company through the CMP once the family has agreed what to propose.

## The goal

> Build the home as one simple farm shed, framed in steel and closed in quickly, with the two halves fitted out inside it and a shared hall at its heart.

## What shaped it

- Both halves must be joined under a single roof [MS]. A barn is the simplest single roof there is.
- Robert taught himself to build in relation to the land [MS], and prefers a concrete slab [MS]. A portal frame on a slab is a method a small team can raise.
- All the additional units must be built by about September 2029 [IC]. A shed shell can be closed in early, and the inside fitted out under its roof.
- The approval asks that buildings, sheds included, suit the area's tourism routes in their size, form and colour [IC]. A farm shed in a dark roof colour is a familiar form on this land.

The barn is 16 metres long and 10 metres deep, on a slab of 160 m². Four steel portal frames stand across it, 5 to 5.5 metres apart, and carry a pitched roof with its ridge running east to west, about 5.8 metres above the floor, and eaves about 3.4 metres.

The frames divide the barn into three bays:

- **Half A** takes the west bay and **Half B** the east bay, each 55 m² on the ground floor: a sitting room of about 19 m² on the north wall, the first bedroom behind it with a window in the gable end, a step-free bathroom, and an entry with the stair. Above the bedroom and bathroom, a closed room of 20 m² in the roof holds the second bedroom, with 2 to 3 metres of headroom and a window in the gable or the roof. Each half is 75 m².
- **The shared hall** takes the middle bay, 50 m²: a living and dining hall of 30 m² open to the ridge, with large glazed sliding doors to the north, the shared kitchen of 14 m² behind it, and the laundry and plant room.

The hall is the household's shared living room as well as its kitchen, which is why each half's own sitting room can be small. The second bedroom of each half is a closed room in the roof, more private than the open mezzanines of Plan 7.

The walls between the frames can be whatever suits the family: insulated panels where speed matters, and site stone, rammed earth or straw bale on the south wall and the gables where mass and warmth matter, which brings Veronica's earthship thinking into the barn. The north wall of the hall and the sitting rooms carries most of the glass.

The north slope of the roof, about 90 m², faces north and takes the solar panels. The whole roof catches about 160 litres for each millimetre of rain, and its simple form has no valleys.

### Building in order

1. The slab, with the services cast in.
2. The four portal frames and the roof, which give a dry place to work.
3. The outer walls and the glass, which close the shell.
4. The fit-out inside, bay by bay.

Whether the approval's deadline counts a unit as built when its shell is closed or only when it can be lived in has not been asked. The plan assumes the whole house is finished before about September 2029 [IC].

North of the barn doors, a terrace runs the full width of the barn and 4 metres past each end. Beyond them lies the dining terrace of 72 m² under an open pergola, with a fire circle and a kitchen garden either side. ## How each household would use it

- **House 1.** Michael in the ground-floor bedroom of one half, and his daughter in the room above. Veronica in the other half, with the room in the roof for visitors or for work.
- **House 2.** Robert and his wife in one half. Their mother and her partner in the other, with their bedroom, bathroom and sitting room on the ground floor, the hall and kitchen next door, and the room in the roof for a visitor or someone who comes to help.

The hall is where both households meet every day. Whether both households want to share one hall and one kitchen is theirs to decide.

## On the three sites

The site figures come from [[the-houses/layout-d-site-development-plan|Layout D]], which modelled each platform from 10-metre contours. They are [TBV] until the platforms have been walked.

- **Unit 1.** About 0.7 metres of fall across the barn's 10-metre depth [TBV]. With the slab fully on fill, the ridge would stand about 6.2 metres above the ground below it. A good fit.
- **Main Dwelling.** About 1.2 metres of fall [TBV]. The ridge would stand about 6.4 metres above the ground below it, close to the limit. A fair fit, and better with a shallow cut at the south edge. Part B, the shared building, could be a fourth bay of the same barn, under the same roof.
- **Unit 3.** About 2.2 metres of fall [TBV]. The ridge would stand about 6.9 metres above the ground below it, over the limit. A poor fit.

## Pros

- The fastest and simplest house to close in, with a roof that keeps the work dry from early on.
- One simple roof with no valleys, and the largest north-facing roof of the plans for solar panels.
- A shared hall open to the ridge, the most generous room in any of the plans.
- The second bedrooms are closed rooms, private and quiet.
- Walls can mix speed and mass: panels where time matters, stone or earth where warmth matters.
- A farm shed is a form the land already knows.

## Cons

- The hall counts toward the 200 m², so each half's private rooms are the smallest of the plans with two bedrooms in each half.
- Each half's second bedroom is up a stair.
- A shed is hot in summer and cold in winter unless it is insulated well. The roof needs insulation under the sheeting and vents at the ridge.
- Sound carries in a tall open hall. The walls between the hall and each half need to stop it.
- Steel does not burn, but it loses strength in a fire. The frame may need protection, and the roof sheeting needs closed edges against embers.
- The ridge stands close to the height limit on the Main Dwelling's slope.
- Whether a room in the roof counts as a double storey, and so needs every shareholder's approval [MS], has not been asked.

## What is still unknown

- Whether the shareholders would accept a barn as the design of a home, as the design, colours and materials must be acceptable to all of them [MS].
- Whether a room in the roof counts as a double storey under the Shareholders' Agreement.
- Whether the approval's deadline counts a unit as built when its shell is closed.
- What fire protection the municipality would ask for on a steel frame.
- Whether the CMP would approve the patio, as landscaping and earthworks need its sign-off [MS].

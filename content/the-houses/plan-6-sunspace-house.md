Nobody outside the family has been asked: not the company, its Community Management Panel (CMP) or its Board, not the residents and not Hessequa Municipality. They will be put to the company through the CMP once the family has agreed what to propose.

## The goal

> Carry the parts of the earthship that work on rocky ground onto a concrete slab: sun taken in through a glazed sunspace, warmth held in a heavy south wall, and water used twice.

## Why the land shapes it

An earthship is a house built around thermal mass and the sun, with heavy walls of rammed earth tyres set into or banked with earth, a glazed sunspace on the sunny side, and planters that clean the household's greywater [IC]. Veronica's first choice for her home is an earthship, and she understands the limits the rocky ground sets [MS]. The ground is sandy and very rocky, and the advice received is to avoid digging deep [MS]. A classic earthship is dug into the ground. This plan keeps what does not need digging.

The house is the line of [[the-houses/plan-1-long-house|Plan 1]], 25 by 8 metres, on a concrete slab. Along its whole north side runs a sunspace 1.6 metres deep, under sloped glass, with planters along the glass. The rooms open off the sunspace, which is also the way between them, so each room takes its winter sun through two layers of glass. The sunspace is part of the 200 m², so the rooms behind it are smaller than in Plan 1.

The south wall is heavy: site stone, or rammed earth tyres on the slab. Earth from the site is banked up against it above ground, with no digging into the rock. The roof falls from about 4.8 metres above the slab at the sunspace to about 3 metres at the south wall, where it meets the earth bank. Rainwater from the roof runs to tanks behind the south bank, or to the store and water room in Half A.

Greywater from the kitchens, basins and showers would pass through the planters in the sunspace and the shared greywater room in the core, which clean it for watering the gardens or flushing toilets. Sewage would still go to the septic tank the approval requires [IC].

## How each household would use it

- **House 1.** Veronica in Half B, with her sunspace for plants. Michael and his daughter in Half A.
- **House 2.** Robert and his wife in Half A. Their mother and her partner in Half B, with every room level and the sunspace as a warm place to sit in winter.

## On the three sites

The site figures come from [[the-houses/layout-d-site-development-plan|Layout D]], which modelled each platform from 10-metre contours. They are [TBV] until the platforms have been walked.

- **Unit 1.** About 0.6 metres of fall across the house [TBV], with the north side, where the glass stands, on the low side. A good fit.
- **Main Dwelling.** About 1 metre of fall [TBV]. A good fit, and the sunspace could run on into the shared building.
- **Unit 3.** The ground already rises behind the south wall, so less earth would need to be banked up, but the slab would still need about 1.8 metres of fill at its north edge [TBV]. A fair fit.

## Pros

- The warmest house in winter of the six, with the most stored heat in its walls.
- Water used twice before it leaves the house.
- The earth bank shelters the south wall from wind and from fire coming from that side.
- Single storey and step-free for both halves.

## Cons

- The sunspace takes about 37 m² of the 200 m², so the living rooms and bedrooms are smaller than in Plan 1.
- Sloped glass along 25 metres needs care to keep it from overheating the house in summer: shade, vents at the top and openings at the bottom.
- The largest areas of glass face the slope below, the way fire running uphill would come. That calls for toughened glass, or shutters that close.
- Rammed tyres take a great deal of work, and the municipality may ask for an engineer's design.
- Whether greywater planters need the municipality's approval has not been asked.

## What is still unknown

- Whether greywater reuse inside the house needs the municipality's approval, and how it fits the requirement for septic tanks.
- Whether the earth bank, the tanks behind it and the sunspace fit inside the Area's firebreak.
- Whether a home of two halves counts as one dwelling. [[the-houses/house-plans|House Plans]] sets out the question.

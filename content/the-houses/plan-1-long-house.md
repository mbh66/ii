> **These plans are not agreed.** The Haupt family drew them in October 2026 for discussion among themselves. Nobody outside the family has been asked: not the company, its Community Management Panel (CMP) or its Board, not the residents and not Hessequa Municipality. They will be put to the company through the CMP once the family has agreed what to propose.

## The goal

> Put both halves in one low line along the contour, with every main room facing north and only the shared core between them.

The house is a single bar 25 metres long and 8 metres deep, the length of the platform the contour model scored on each site [MS]. Half A takes the west 13 metres, Half B the east 10 metres, and a core 2 metres wide between them holds the shared laundry and the plant room.

The living rooms and all three bedrooms stand along the north wall. The entries, bathrooms, sculleries and stores form a strip along the south wall, where rooms used for a short time take the cold side of the house. Each half has its own front door on the south side, close to the parking, and its own garden to the north. The two living rooms are at opposite ends of the house.

The roof is one plane rising to the south, about 4 metres above the slab at its high side, with the solar panels on its north face and gutters along the north eave feeding the tanks.

## How each household would use it

- **House 1.** Michael and his daughter in Half A, with a bedroom each. Veronica in Half B.
- **House 2.** Robert and his wife in Half A, with the second bedroom as a study or a room for visitors. Their mother and her partner in Half B, with the step-free bathroom beside the bedroom and the front door a few steps from the parking.

## On the three sites

The site figures come from [[the-houses/layout-d-site-development-plan|Layout D]], which modelled each platform from 10-metre contours. They are [TBV] until the platforms have been walked.

- **Unit 1.** The ground falls about 0.6 metres across the house's depth [TBV]. A perimeter wall of that height at the north edge lifts the slab level. A good fit.
- **Main Dwelling.** The fall across the house is about 1 metre [TBV]. The shared building, Part B, could join the east end, beside Half B, so the step-free half is closest to it. A good fit.
- **Unit 3.** The fall across the house is about 1.8 metres, and the house needs 25 metres along the contour where only about a third of the Area is gentle ground [TBV]. A poor fit. Plans 3 and 5 suit Unit 3 better.

## Pros

- Both halves on one level, with no stair anywhere.
- The simplest slab and roof of the six plans: one rectangle and one roof plane.
- Every living room and bedroom has a north window.
- The shared core and the two sets of stores and bathrooms keep the halves apart for sound.

## Cons

- It needs the full 25 metres of a platform along the contour.
- In Half A the bedrooms lie at the end of a passage 8.5 metres long.
- The two north gardens meet beside the core, so they would need a hedge or a wall between them.
- The plant room pump and inverter sit against both halves, so the core needs sound insulation.

## What is still unknown

- Whether the platform at Unit 1 or the Main Dwelling holds 25 metres of level ground once walked.
- Whether a home of two halves counts as one dwelling. [[the-houses/house-plans|House Plans]] sets out the question.

> **These plans are not agreed.** The Haupt family drew them in October 2026 for discussion among themselves. Nobody outside the family has been asked: not the company, its Community Management Panel (CMP) or its Board, not the residents and not Hessequa Municipality. They will be put to the company through the CMP once the family has agreed what to propose.

## The goal

> Join the two halves with a shared kitchen in the shape of an H, under one roof, so the house makes two outdoor rooms: a sunny court for living on the north, and a sheltered court for arriving on the south.

## What shaped it

- Both halves must be joined under a single roof [MS].
- The approval asks that buildings suit the area's tourism routes in their size, form and colour [IC]. The H is one of the plan shapes of the old Cape farmhouses [TBV], and a house of gabled wings with a lower crossbar reads as a farmhouse on its werf.
- Plans 9 to 11 showed that one shared kitchen frees enough room for two bedrooms in each half on one level. This plan keeps that, and uses the same wings as [[the-houses/plan-11-courtyard-house|Plan 11]].

The house is 20 metres wide and 14 metres deep. Half A is the west wing and Half B the east wing, each 6 by 14 metres. The crossbar between them, 8 by 4 metres, holds the shared kitchenette and dining room of 22 m² on its north side, with the plant room and the laundry beside it. The crossbar sits in the middle of the wings' length, so it divides the space between them into two courts of 8 by 5 metres:

- **The north court** is the living court. It is open to the north, so the sun reaches it and the kitchenette's north windows in winter. The two living rooms flank it.
- **The south court** is the arrival court. Each half's front door opens off it, from the end of its gallery. The laundry opens onto it as a service yard, and the parking stands just beyond its open south side, level with both doors.

Each wing is 84 m², as in Plan 11: a living room of 27 m² at the north end, two bedrooms of about 16 m² facing outward, a step-free bathroom, and a gallery along the court side. Everything is on one level, and every room in both halves is step-free.

Each wing has a pitched roof running north to south, with a gable at each end, eaves about 2.8 metres above the floor and a ridge about 4.5 metres. The crossbar's roof runs east to west between them, with its ridge a little lower, and joins each wing in two valleys. The north gables of the wings give the living rooms tall north windows up into the roof.

The wings' roofs face east and west, and only the crossbar's north slope faces north, so solar panels would go on the east and west slopes or on a ground mount [TBV].

### What Phase 1 lays down for Phase 2

- Water, drainage and power, and a gas line if wanted, taken to the north face of the crossbar and capped.
- Both courts levelled and covered in gravel, with a movable braai in the north court.
- The parking laid as open gravel, uncovered, since a carport would count toward the 200 m².

- **The north court,** 40 m², is paved, with the outdoor kitchen along the crossbar's wall under the kitchenette windows.
- **The dining terrace,** 48 m², extends the north court past the wings under an open pergola of about 48 m², about 0.3 metres lower, with a short ramp. The pergola stands north of the court, so the winter sun still reaches the kitchenette.
- **A fire circle** of 36 m² and **a kitchen garden** of 36 m² stand in front of the two living rooms.
- **The south court,** 40 m², is paved as the arrival court, with a tree and a bench.

In all there are about 164 m² of paving and 36 m² of beds, all open to the sky and outside the 200 m². A stone seat wall along the north edge holds the terrace level, about 1.1 metres high on Unit 1 [TBV], and rain on the paving runs to the beds and a swale below it, as the approval asks for sustainable drainage [IC].

## How each household would use it

- **House 1.** Michael and his daughter in one wing, a bedroom each. Veronica in the other, with her second bedroom as a room for visitors or for work.
- **House 2.** Robert and his wife in one wing. Their mother and her partner in the other, with level parking at the south court, a short walk to their own front door, and every room on one level.

The front doors open off the south court, away from the living court, so a visitor arrives without walking through where the households sit. The galleries face each other across each court and need planting or screening for privacy. Whether both households want to share one kitchen is theirs to decide.

## On the three sites

The site figures come from [[the-houses/layout-d-site-development-plan|Layout D]], which modelled each platform from 10-metre contours. They are [TBV] until the platforms have been walked.

- **Unit 1.** The best fit. The ground falls about 1 metre across the house's 14-metre depth [TBV].
- **Main Dwelling.** The ground falls about 1.7 metres across the house [TBV], and the terrace's seat wall would be nearly 2 metres high. A fair fit. Part B, the shared building, could close the south court's open side, joined under the same roof, which would make the south court the arrival court for the whole farm's shared facility as well.
- **Unit 3.** The ground falls about 3 metres across the house [TBV]. A poor fit.

## Pros

- Two outdoor rooms: a sunny court for living and a sheltered court for arriving, both outside the 200 m².
- Level parking at the front doors, which suits the step-free half.
- A plan form that belongs to the Cape countryside, which helps with the approval's condition on fitting the tourism routes [IC].
- One roof and one kitchen, with no height approval: the ridges stand about 4.5 metres.
- The front doors and the laundry are kept away from the living court.

## Cons

- About as much outside wall as the courtyard house, more than any of the straight plans.
- Four valleys where the crossbar roof meets the wings, each a place for water to get in if badly built.
- The courts are 8 by 5 metres, intimate rather than large. The terrace north of the house gives the space for a big table.
- The wings' roofs face east and west, which suits solar panels less than a north-facing roof.
- The south court is in the crossbar's shadow in winter, which keeps it cool and damp in the cold months.

## What is still unknown

- Whether the CMP and the shareholders would approve the design and the patio, as landscaping and earthworks need the CMP's sign-off [MS] and the design of every home must be acceptable to all shareholders [MS].
- Whether the H plan is as well rooted in the farmhouses of this district as in the older Cape farms further west.
- How much less the east and west roofs would give from solar panels, and whether a ground mount would be allowed.
- Whether the pergola needs building plans from the municipality.
- Whether the patio counts toward the disturbed footprint named in the approval.

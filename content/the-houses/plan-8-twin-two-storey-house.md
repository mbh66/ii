> **These plans are not agreed.** The Haupt family drew them in October 2026 for discussion among themselves. Nobody outside the family has been asked: not the company, its Community Management Panel (CMP) or its Board, not the residents and not Hessequa Municipality. They will be put to the company through the CMP once the family has agreed what to propose.

## The goal

> Give both halves two closed bedrooms, each upstairs bedroom with its own shower room, while each half keeps a full ground floor.

The house is 17 metres by 8 metres on a slab of 136 m². Each half has 60 m² on the ground floor and 32 m² upstairs, 92 m² in all, and the shared core keeps its 16 m². The two halves are the same size, mirrored either side of the core.

On the ground floor of each half, the kitchen and living room and the first bedroom stand along the north wall. The entry, the scullery, the bathroom and the stair form a strip along the south wall. Half B's bathroom is the large step-free one.

The upper floor of each half is 4 metres wide and stands on the core side of the half. It holds the second bedroom on the north wall, a landing and a shower room. The core between them stays one storey, so the upstairs rooms of the two halves are 2 metres apart and share no wall.

Over the two-storey parts the roof rises to the south, from an eave about 5.4 metres above the slab to a ridge about 6.2 metres above it. The single-storey parts of each half and the core have lower roofs, about 4 metres high. From outside, the house reads as a low building with two taller parts at its middle.

Compared with Plans 1 to 6, both halves now have two bedrooms. Half A gives up 12 m² and Half B gains 12 m². Each living room is 22.5 m², the smallest of the eight plans.

## How each household would use it

- **House 1.** Michael's daughter upstairs in Half A, with her own shower room, and Michael below. Veronica in Half B, with her bedroom downstairs and the upstairs room for visitors or for work.
- **House 2.** Robert and his wife in Half A, on whichever floor suits them. Their mother and her partner in Half B, with their bedroom, bathroom and kitchen on the ground floor, and the upstairs room and shower room for a visitor or someone who comes to help.

## On the three sites

The site figures come from [[the-houses/layout-d-site-development-plan|Layout D]], which modelled each platform from 10-metre contours. They are [TBV] until the platforms have been walked.

- **Unit 1.** About 0.6 metres of fall across the house [TBV]. With the slab fully on fill, the north eave of each two-storey part stands about 6 metres above the ground below it. A good fit.
- **Main Dwelling.** About 1 metre of fall [TBV]. With the slab fully on fill, the north eave would stand about 6.4 metres above the ground below it, close to the limit [TBV]. A shallow cut at the south edge, or a lower upper floor, would give more room. A fair fit.
- **Unit 3.** About 1.8 metres of fall [TBV]. With the slab fully on fill, the north eave would stand about 7.2 metres above the ground below it, over the limit [TBV]. A poor fit.

## Pros

- Both halves get two closed bedrooms, and both stay the same size.
- Each upstairs bedroom has its own shower room, which suits a visitor or a carer.
- The upstairs rooms of the two halves share no wall.
- At 136 m², the slab and the main roof are smaller than in every plan except Plan 5.
- The ground floor of Half B holds everything its household needs from day to day.

## Cons

- A double storey needs the approval of every shareholder [MS].
- The living rooms are the smallest of the eight plans.
- The two-storey parts stand close to the 6.5-metre limit, and the approval asks for homes screened from neighbours, the roads and the nature reserves within view [IC].
- A roof at two heights has more joints to keep watertight than a single plane.
- The roof catches about 136 litres for each millimetre of rain.
- Each upper floor has one stair. Each upstairs bedroom needs a window large enough to leave by if the stair is blocked by fire.

## What is still unknown

- Whether every shareholder would approve a double storey.
- How the municipality measures height on a slope, which decides whether Plan 8 fits at the Main Dwelling as drawn.
- Whether a home of two halves counts as one dwelling. [[the-houses/house-plans|House Plans]] sets out the question.

> **This layout is not agreed.** The prospective stewards drew it in October 2026 from public contour data, and after only a brief weekend visit walking the plots. Nobody else has been asked: not the company, its Community Management Panel (CMP) or its Board, and not the residents. It will be put to the company through the CMP, with the other three layouts, after more detailed discussions.

## The goal

> Give each home the most room and privacy the Residential Zone allows, and bring the Main Dwelling next to the shared facilities.

## Why it matters for the land

[[the-houses/index|The Houses]] explains what was decided before any layout was drawn and how the plots were placed. In short: seven homes, each unit up to 200 m² [IC], each on an unfenced Area of about one hectare with a 10-metre firebreak [MS], on sandy, rocky ground that a construction expert has advised against digging into [MS].

## The idea

Homes stand at least 185 metres apart. A spacing of 200 metres was tried first, and six plots of one hectare do not fit in the zone at that distance. Three plots run in a line down the north-west of the zone, at about 341, 365 and 390 metres. Three sit on or beside the knoll top between about 414 and 420 metres. The Main Dwelling stands beside the Community Resources plot, about 40 metres from it and about 60 metres from the Shed, with part of its Area inside the Production Zone.

## Pros

- The most privacy. The closest two homes are about 186 metres apart [TBV].
- The Main Dwelling, with its shared half, stands next to the Community Resources and the Shed, about a minute's walk away [TBV].
- Three homes lower down are more sheltered from wind than the knoll top.

## Cons

- The weakest ground of the four layouts. C3 has about 3.7 metres of rise and fall across its platform, and C1 and C2 about 2 metres [TBV]. On rocky ground with little digging, that means fill, retaining or a stepped design.
- The longest daily trips: C1, C2 and C3 are about 22 to 28 minutes from the Shed, with climbs of about 29 to 78 metres on every return [TBV]. One trip from every home adds up to about 166 metres of climbing a day.
- The longest roads, pipes and power runs of the four.
- The homes form a line and a small group, the loosest arrangement of the four.

## The goal

> Keep the earthship ideas of Plan 6, give both halves two bedrooms on one level around one shared kitchenette, and add a large outdoor kitchen and dining terrace two years later without taking the winter sun from the glass.

## Why the patio can be larger than the house

The Shareholders' Agreement limits each home to 200 m² under roof [MS], and the approval limits floor area to 200 m² [IC]. As in [[the-houses/plan-9-shared-kitchen-and-patio|Plan 9]], paving open to the sky is neither, so it stays outside the 200 m². An open pergola of slats carrying a vine keeps it open to the sky. Roof sheeting or polycarbonate over any part of the patio would bring that part inside the 200 m².

The house is [[the-houses/plan-6-sunspace-house|Plan 6]]: 25 by 8 metres on a concrete slab, one storey, with a sunspace 1.6 metres deep under sloped glass along the whole north side and earth banked against the heavy south wall. The roof and the heights are the same, about 4.8 metres at the sunspace and 3 metres at the south wall.

What changes is the inside. The two kitchens of Plan 6 come out, and the shared core widens from 2 metres to 4. It holds one kitchenette and dining room of 16 m², with its own 6.4 m² stretch of the sunspace in front for a table in the sun, the laundry and the greywater and plant room.

With the kitchens gone, each half has room for two bedrooms on one level. Each half is 84 m²: its sunspace, two bedrooms of 13 m², a living room of 16 m² next to the core, a bathroom, a store and water room, and its own front door on the south side. Half B's bathroom is the large step-free one. The bedrooms take the outer ends of the house. One sunspace runs the full 25 metres, so each household reaches the kitchenette either along the sunspace or through its own living room, each through a door that locks.

The kitchenette has the only stove and oven in the house. For the first two years it is the whole kitchen for both households, and on cold or wet days it stays the kitchen after that.

### What Phase 1 lays down for Phase 2

- Water, drainage and power, and a gas line if wanted, taken under the slab edge to the outdoor kitchen's position and capped.
- The sun terrace's level set at the floor of the sunspace, so the doors open straight out.
- Fill for the terraces placed and compacted while the machine is on site, or left until Phase 2 if the CMP prefers less disturbance at first.
- A gravel area and a movable braai north of the kitchenette for the first two years.

### The sun terrace comes first

The sunspace works only while winter sun reaches its glass. At the farm's latitude the noon sun stands about 33° above the horizon on 21 June [IC]. A pergola 2.8 metres high against the glass would shade it all winter, vine or no vine, because the slats and beams alone cast shade. So the first 4.5 metres north of the glass are a paved sun terrace with nothing over it, at the level of the sunspace floor. The pergola starts beyond it, on terraces about 0.45 metres lower, where the winter sun passes over its top to the foot of the glass.

The sun terrace runs the full 25 metres: a terrace of about 47 m² in front of each half and a shared stretch of 18 m² in front of the kitchenette, with planters marking where one ends and the next begins.

### Under the pergola

- **The outdoor kitchen,** about 24 m², straight across the sun terrace from the kitchenette, with a braai, a wood-fired oven, a prep counter and a sink. Herb beds stand either side of it.
- **The dining terrace,** about 72 m², north of the outdoor kitchen, room for a long table for twenty.
- **The pergola** covers both, about 96 m², with a deciduous vine that shades the tables in summer and drops its leaves in winter.

Beyond the pergola are a fire circle of about 37 m² to the west and a kitchen garden of about 36 m² to the east. A ramp of 1 in 12 between the sun terrace and the lower terraces keeps the whole patio step-free. A stone seat wall along the north edge holds the terraces level, about 1.1 metres high on Unit 1 [TBV]. Rain on the paving runs to the beds and to a swale below the seat wall, and water from the outdoor sink goes to the kitchen garden through a grease trap. The approval asks for sustainable drainage that keeps stormwater clean [IC].

In all, the patio has about 246 m² of paving and about 57 m² of beds, more than the house itself, and none of it counts toward the 200 m².

## How each household would use it

- **House 1.** Michael and his daughter in one half, a bedroom each. Veronica in the other, with her second bedroom as a room for visitors or for work, and the sunspace for her plants.
- **House 2.** Robert and his wife in one half. Their mother and her partner in the other, with every room, the kitchenette, the sun terrace and, by the ramp, the whole patio on one level with no step.

Sharing a kitchen asks more of two households than sharing a laundry. Whether both households in each home want to share one is theirs to decide.

## On the three sites

The site figures come from [[the-houses/layout-d-site-development-plan|Layout D]], which modelled each platform from 10-metre contours. They are [TBV] until the platforms have been walked.

- **Unit 1.** The best fit. The ground falls about 1.6 metres from the earth bank to the north edge of the patio, about 21.5 metres away [TBV], taken up by the step of 0.45 metres and a seat wall of about 1.1 metres.
- **Main Dwelling.** The ground falls about 2.6 metres over the same distance [TBV]. The lower terraces would need a second step, or a seat wall about 2 metres high, which is a retaining wall more than a seat. A fair fit.
- **Unit 3.** The ground falls nearly 5 metres over the same distance [TBV]. A poor fit.

## Pros

- Both halves have two bedrooms, and every room in both halves is on one level. Neither household has a stair.
- No height approval is needed: the house stands about 4.8 metres at its highest.
- One kitchen in the house, which makes it read as one dwelling.
- The paved sun terrace keeps vegetation 4.5 metres back from the glass, which answers the fire risk Plan 6 raised for the north face.
- The patio is the largest of the plans and lies outside the 200 m².

## Cons

- The halves are no longer fully self-contained. Each household depends on the shared kitchenette.
- The living rooms are small, about 16 m² each, with the sunspace in front adding a strip of 16.8 m² to sit in.
- The outdoor kitchen is across the sun terrace from the kitchenette, about 5 metres, which is a longer carry than in Plan 9.
- In summer the paving in front of the glass gets hot and throws light and heat into the sunspace. The sunspace needs vents at the top and openings at the bottom, as in Plan 6, and the sun terrace may need movable shade in summer.
- A patio of this size disturbs more ground. The approval names a disturbed footprint of about 4,507 m² for the whole development [IC], and nothing may be built in highly sensitive indigenous vegetation [IC].
- An open fire on a farm in fynbos needs firm rules, and a vine on a pergola needs irrigating so it does not dry out.

## What is still unknown

- Whether the CMP and the shareholders would approve a patio of this size, as landscaping and earthworks need the CMP's sign-off [MS] and the design of every home must be acceptable to all shareholders [MS].
- Whether movable shade over the sun terrace in summer would count as area under roof.
- Whether the pergola needs building plans from the municipality.
- Whether greywater from the house and the outdoor sink may go to the garden, and how that fits the requirement for septic tanks.
- Whether the patio counts toward the disturbed footprint named in the approval.
- How the CMP's fire rules would treat a fire circle.

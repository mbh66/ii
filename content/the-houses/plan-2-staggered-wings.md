> **These plans are not agreed.** The Haupt family drew them in October 2026 for discussion among themselves. Nobody outside the family has been asked: not the company, its Community Management Panel (CMP) or its Board, not the residents and not Hessequa Municipality. They will be put to the company through the CMP once the family has agreed what to propose.

## The goal

> Give each half more privacy, and a sheltered garden, by setting one wing back behind the other.

The two halves of [[the-houses/plan-1-long-house|Plan 1]] are kept, and Half B is set 3.5 metres back to the south and mirrored, so its living room is at the far west end. The shared core joins the two wings where they overlap. The house measures 25 metres by 11.5 metres overall.

Half A's west wall, which can stand without windows, then screens Half B's north garden from Half A. The corner between that wall and Half B's north wall makes a garden sheltered from the east and the south, and open to the sun and the view to the north. The living rooms are at opposite ends of the house, and each half's front door faces south, Half B's at the far west corner.

Each wing has its own roof plane rising to the south. The step in plan gives two shorter roofs, each about 4 metres high at its south side.

## How each household would use it

- **House 1.** Michael and his daughter in Half A, Veronica in Half B, with the sheltered corner as her garden.
- **House 2.** Robert and his wife in Half A. Their mother and her partner in Half B, with the sheltered corner as a garden to sit in.

## On the three sites

The site figures come from [[the-houses/layout-d-site-development-plan|Layout D]], which modelled each platform from 10-metre contours. They are [TBV] until the platforms have been walked.

- **Unit 1.** The ground falls about 0.85 metres across the house's 11.5-metre depth [TBV]. Both wings can sit on one level, on a perimeter wall that rises to the north. A good fit.
- **Main Dwelling.** The fall is about 1.4 metres [TBV]. Half B can sit about 0.4 metres above Half A, with a short ramp in the core, which keeps Half B step-free from the parking. The shared building, Part B, could join the east end of Half A or the west end of Half B. A good fit.
- **Unit 3.** The fall is about 2.6 metres across the house [TBV]. A poor fit as drawn. [[the-houses/plan-3-stepped-house|Plan 3]] is this plan adapted to the slope.

## Pros

- More privacy between the halves than in Plan 1, with a blank wall between their gardens.
- Half B gains a garden sheltered on two sides.
- The two roofs are shorter, and the house reads as two small wings on the land.
- Both halves stay on one level.

## Cons

- It needs a deeper platform, 11.5 metres, and more of the slab stands on fill.
- More outside wall and more roof edge than Plan 1, for the same floor area.
- Half A's living room has no west window, by design.

## What is still unknown

- Which direction the strongest winds come from on each site. The corner is open to the north and west, and whether the winds come from that side has not been recorded.
- Whether a home of two halves counts as one dwelling. [[the-houses/house-plans|House Plans]] sets out the question.

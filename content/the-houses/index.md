> **These layouts are not agreed.** The prospective stewards drew them in October 2026 from public contour data, and after only a brief weekend visit walking the plots. Nobody else has been asked: not the company, its Community Management Panel (CMP) or its Board, and not the residents. They will be put to the company through the CMP, after a more detailed discussion.

This section sets out where the six homes and the Main Dwelling could stand. Layout D follows the Site Development Plan the municipality approved, and is the starting point. Layouts A, B and C are alternatives the family drew from the farm's contours before it had been able to fully interpret the plan. They differ only in how far apart the homes are, and they show what moving away from the plan would gain and what it would cost in ground, effort and shelter.

Also see [[house-plans|House Plans]] for mockups of what each house could look like.

## Layout D is the starting point

- The Shareholders' Agreement defines the Site Development Plan as the plan Hessequa Municipality approved in 2025, and attaches it as Annexures 1 and 2 [MS]. The plan marks where each of the seven dwellings stands [MS].
- Moving a dwelling away from the position the plan shows would mean asking the municipality to approve an amended plan [TBV]. The family has not yet confirmed this with the municipality.
- Changing the exclusive-use Areas needs every founding shareholder to agree, and an Area can be moved only before building on it starts [MS].
- Units 3, 4 and 6 are allocated, and the ground for Unit 6 has already been levelled [MS].
- The approval lapses if it is not used within five years, and all the additional units must be built by about September 2029 [IC]. Any request to amend the plan would add time to that schedule.

[[the-houses/layout-d-site-development-plan|Layout D: Following the Site Development Plan]] gives each home's figures.

## What the alternatives would add

Each alternative moves some homes away from the plan. In return:

- **Layout A** gives every home flatter ground. Five of its six platforms have less than a metre of rise and fall, against two of six in Layout D, and no home stands on the north slope [TBV]. All six homes stand on the knoll top, the most exposed ground in the zone.
- **Layout B** gives homes more room, with the closest two 150 metres apart against about 108 metres in Layout D, and puts two homes lower down, out of the wind [TBV]. Those two homes face a climb of 56 to 78 metres back to the Production Zone on every trip.
- **Layout C** gives the most room, with homes at least 185 metres apart, and puts three homes lower down [TBV]. It has the weakest ground and the longest walks of the four.

The clearest gain the alternatives point to is at Unit 3. Layout D places it on the north slope, at nearly 13° with about 3.4 metres of rise and fall across the platform, on ground where none of the alternatives places a home [TBV]. A change for Unit 3 alone, if its future household wants one, would be a smaller request than a different layout.

The three alternatives remain on this site as a record of what the ground offers.

## What was determined before any layout was drawn

- The farm may have seven homes: one Main Dwelling and six additional units [IC].
- Each unit may have up to 200 m² of floor area and stand up to 6.5 metres high [IC].
- The approval asks for units grouped in clusters, on ground already disturbed near the farm homestead, screened from neighbours, the R322, the N2 and the nature reserves within view [IC].
- Each home sits on its own Area of about one hectare, unfenced, with a firebreak of at least 10 metres around it [MS].
- The Main Dwelling is approved for 350 m² under roof, in two connected parts: one home and one shared facility (the community centre) [MS].
- An Area can be moved, with the consent of all shareholders concerned, only before building on it starts [MS].
- The Areas on the family's working map were placed only to show the layout. They may move, as long as they stay roughly within the Residential Zone, and the Main Dwelling may stand almost anywhere [MS].
- All the farm's shared facilities are located in the Production Zone [MS].
- The Site Development Plan attached to the Shareholders' Agreement marks where each dwelling stands, with its driveway, but does not draw the Areas [MS]. Its allocation table of 31 October 2025 shows Units 3, 4 and 6 as allocated, and the Main Dwelling and Units 1, 2 and 5 as available [MS].
- The ground for Unit 6 has already been levelled [MS].

[[documents/municipal-approval-2024|Municipal Approval for Additional Homes (2024)]] and [[documents/shareholders-agreement|Shareholders' Agreement (2025)]] provide the full conditions.

## What the family asked of a site

The family ranked what matters in choosing a site [MS]:

1. **Ground.** The ground is sandy and very rocky everywhere. The advice received is to avoid digging too deep into the ground [MS]. So the best site is a naturally flat base that requires little cutting or filling.
2. **Micro-climate.** Sun for solar panels, for warmth indoors and for growing vegetables, and shelter from wind and driving rain.
3. **Daily living.** The walk and the climb to the shared facilities in the Production Zone, which residents make every day.

Water and fire risk do not separate one site from another. Water is abundant, each home will have water piped to it and its own pump [MS]. Fire risk is the same for every home and is handled by a site-wide fire management plan [MS].

## How the layouts were drawn

1. **The ground.** The Western Cape Government's contour lines, drawn every 10 metres, were turned into a model of the ground on a 5-metre grid [IC]. The model's heights match the province's own readings at every Area on the working map.
2. **Every possible house platform was scored.** For each spot in and around the Residential Zone, the model scored a platform of 25 by 15 metres, room enough for a home of up to 200 m², on five things:

| What was scored                                    | Weight | Why                                             |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Rise and fall of the ground across the platform    | 40%    | Less cutting and filling on rocky, sandy ground |
| Hours of direct sun on 21 June, the shortest day   | 20%    | Winter warmth, solar power, winter vegetables   |
| Ground facing north                                | 10%    | Sun on the windows and the garden               |
| Hollows where cold air gathers, and exposed crests | 10%    | Frost, damp and wind                            |
| Walk and climb to the Shed in the Production Zone  | 20%    | The daily trip to the shared facilities         |

3. **Every possible plot was tried.** A plot of one hectare, either 125 by 80 metres or 100 by 100 metres, was placed every 10 metres across the zone and turned in 15-degree steps. Each plot kept its house at least 18 metres inside its own boundary, so the 10-metre firebreak stays within the Area, and kept at least 85% of its ground inside the Residential Zone.
4. **The best six were chosen together.** A search picked the six plots with the best combined score that didn't overlap and kept their homes a set distance apart. That distance is the only thing that changes between the layouts.
5. **The Main Dwelling was placed last,** on the best platform outside the six plots, inside the farm boundary.

## The four layouts

- [[the-houses/layout-d-site-development-plan|Layout D: Following the Site Development Plan]]: the starting point. Each home within about 35 metres of the dwelling on the approved plan, Unit 6 on the levelled ground, and the Main Dwelling beside the existing cabin. Layout D was drawn around the plan's positions, so steps 3 to 5 above applied only within those limits.

Alternatives drawn before the plan was seen:

- [[the-houses/layout-a-clustered|Layout A: Clustered on the Knoll]]: homes at least 100 metres apart, all six on the flat top of the knoll.
- [[the-houses/layout-b-spread|Layout B: Spread Along the Zone]]: homes at least 150 metres apart, four on the knoll and two down the north-west slope.
- [[the-houses/layout-c-widest-spread|Layout C: Widest Spread]]: homes at least 185 metres apart, the widest spacing that fits six plots in the zone, with the Main Dwelling beside the Community Resources plot.

The clustering condition in the municipal approval letter asks for units grouped in clusters [IC]. Whether a layout meets it is a question for the municipality to answer.

## What the model cannot see

- The contours are 10 metres apart. Anything smaller, a rock outcrop, a bench of flat ground, a few metres of fall, is invisible to the model. The knoll top looks flat partly because no contour falls inside the 420-metre line.
- The sun count covers the hills within about 600 metres. The Langeberg to the north shortens early and late winter sun for every home alike, and isn't counted.
- Wind is not modelled. The knoll top is the most exposed ground in the zone, which counts against Layout A in a way that hasn't been reflected in the scores.
- Vegetation is not modelled. The approval forbids building in highly sensitive indigenous vegetation [IC]. During the weekend visit, there appeared to be little evidence of sensitive vegetation. A botanical assessment was conducted in 2021, but this has not been seen by the family [MS].

## What is still undecided

- Whether the company wishes to move any Area, given that Units 3, 4 and 6 are allocated and Unit 6 has been levelled [MS].
- Whether Layouts A, B and C, which move homes away from the dwellings on the approved Site Development Plan, would need the plan amended. That is a question for the municipality.
- Whether the knoll top is as flat and buildable as the model shows.
- How each platform sits against the 2021 botanical assessment.
- Whether the community prefers homes close together or far apart. That is a choice about how people want to live, which the model cannot decide.

## What happens next

1. Share the alternative Layouts with the company through the CMP.
2. Ask the municipality whether moving a dwelling within its Area, or moving it further, needs an amended plan.
3. Walk the platforms of Layout D with a probe, a compass and a sun-path app, starting with Unit 3, and check the knoll for wind and rock.
4. Ask the company whether a drone survey of the Residential Zone would be welcome. A survey would let the same method check each platform on ground measured to a few centimetres.
5. Bring any proposed change to Layout D to the CMP for sign-off before any design work.

> **These plans are not agreed.** The Haupt family drew them in October 2026 for discussion among themselves. Nobody outside the family has been asked: not the company, its Community Management Panel (CMP) or its Board, not the residents and not Hessequa Municipality. They will be put to the company through the CMP once the family has agreed what to propose.

> [!info] What the letters in square brackets mean
> Facts on this site are followed by a tag that shows where they come from.
>
> - **[IC] Independently Corroborated.** Confirmed by at least two sources that do not depend on each other, or by an official record.
> - **[MS] Mission-Sourced.** Comes from the company's own documents or statements, or from the family writing this site, in their own words. It has not been checked against other sources.
> - **[TBV] To Be Verified.** Seems likely, but nobody has confirmed it yet.
>
> Statements without a tag are proposals. They describe what an idea could become.

> [!warning] Superseded
> This plan puts each terrace under its own roof. The family has since set a rule that both halves sit under a single roof [MS], so it no longer meets the brief. It stays on the site as a record. On a slope like Unit 3's, a single roof over a raised floor on piers would be the way to revisit it.

## The goal

> Let the house follow a slope in two steps, each half on its own level, without cutting deep into rock.

The two halves stand on two terraces. Half B takes the upper terrace, to the south and west, where the track arrives. Half A takes the lower terrace, to the north and east, about 1.4 metres below. The shared core stands between them: a link stair of eight steps, and the plant room and laundry at the upper level. The house measures 23 by 14 metres overall.

Because the two halves stand side by side as well as one above the other, each looks north past the other. Each terrace is 8 metres deep, and each has its own roof rising to the south, about 4 metres above its own floor.

## How each household would use it

- **House 1.** Veronica on the upper terrace in Half B, Michael and his daughter on the lower terrace in Half A.
- **House 2.** The siblings' mother and her partner on the upper terrace, level from the parking. Robert and his wife below, with the link stair between the two halves.

Half B stays step-free only if the track and the parking arrive at the upper level. The Site Development Plan marks a driveway for each dwelling [MS], and where Unit 3's driveway arrives has not been checked on the ground.

## On the three sites

The site figures come from [[the-houses/layout-d-site-development-plan|Layout D]], which modelled each platform from 10-metre contours. They are [TBV] until the platforms have been walked.

- **Unit 3.** The plan is drawn for this site. On a slope of about 13°, each 8-metre terrace falls about 1.8 metres [TBV]. Each slab can sit half on fill and half in a shallow cut of about 0.9 metres, or fully on fill behind a perimeter wall about 1.8 metres high at its north edge, which avoids cutting rock [TBV]. The two terraces are about 6 metres apart up the slope, which gives the step of about 1.4 metres [TBV]. Only about a third of Unit 3's Area is gentle ground [TBV], so the two terraces could be placed on separate benches if the walk over the site finds them.
- **Main Dwelling.** At about 7°, the step between the terraces would be about 0.7 metres [TBV], short enough for a ramp in the core. A fair fit, and Plan 2 gives much the same house without the step.
- **Unit 1.** At about 4°, the step would be under half a metre [TBV]. Plans 1 and 2 suit Unit 1 better.

## Pros

- Each half sits on its own level, so less of either slab stands on deep fill.
- The two halves are separated by a change of level as well as by the core, which gives both privacy.
- Each half looks north past the other.
- No height approval is needed: each terrace stays a single storey.

## Cons

- Two slabs, two roofs and the retaining edges cost more than one slab and one roof.
- Half A's residents climb eight steps to reach the laundry.
- The house is 14 metres deep up the slope, so it needs ground that is gentle enough for two terraces.
- Unit 3 is allocated [MS].

## What is still unknown

- Where the gentle benches lie on Unit 3, which the 10-metre contours cannot show.
- Where Unit 3's driveway arrives, which decides whether Half B is step-free.
- How the municipality measures height on a slope.
- Whether a home of two halves counts as one dwelling. [[the-houses/house-plans|House Plans]] sets out the question.

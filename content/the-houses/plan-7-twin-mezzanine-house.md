> **These plans are not agreed.** The Haupt family drew them in October 2026 for discussion among themselves. Nobody outside the family has been asked: not the company, its Community Management Panel (CMP) or its Board, not the residents and not Hessequa Municipality. They will be put to the company through the CMP once the family has agreed what to propose.

## The goal

> Give both halves two bedrooms inside the 200 m², by lifting each half's second bedroom onto a mezzanine.

The house is one storey, 19.5 metres by 8 metres, with a mezzanine of 22 m² in each half. Each half has 70 m² on the ground floor and 22 m² on its mezzanine, 92 m² in all, and the shared core keeps its 16 m². The two halves are the same size, mirrored either side of the core.

On the ground floor of each half, the kitchen and living room and the first bedroom stand along the north wall. The entry, the bathroom, the stair and the scullery form a strip along the south wall. Half B's bathroom is the large step-free one, with a level shower.

Each half's roof rises to the south, from an eave about 3.4 metres above the slab to a ridge about 6.2 metres above it. The mezzanine sits over the south rooms, with its floor at about 2.65 metres, which leaves ceilings of 2.4 metres in the bathroom, stair and scullery below. It opens north over the living room, which rises to the roof. Its lowest edge has about 2.15 metres of headroom, and its south side about 3.5 metres.

A bedroom on a mezzanine is open on one side to the living room below. A low wall along its edge, with a sliding screen or glass above it, would give it privacy and keep the sound down, and still let it share the light from the north windows.

Compared with Plans 1 to 6, both halves now have two bedrooms. Half A gives up 12 m² and Half B gains 12 m². Each living room is about 26 m², against 32.5 m² and 30 m² in Plan 1.

## How each household would use it

- **House 1.** Michael in the ground-floor bedroom of Half A, and his daughter on its mezzanine. Veronica in Half B, with her mezzanine as a room for visitors or for work.
- **House 2.** Robert and his wife in Half A, with the mezzanine as a study or a room for visitors. Their mother and her partner in Half B, with their bedroom, bathroom and kitchen all on the ground floor, and the mezzanine for a visitor or someone who comes to help. The mezzanine stair is the one part of Half B that is not step-free.

## On the three sites

The site figures come from [[the-houses/layout-d-site-development-plan|Layout D]], which modelled each platform from 10-metre contours. They are [TBV] until the platforms have been walked.

- **Unit 1.** About 0.6 metres of fall across the house [TBV], with the high side of the roof on the uphill side. A good fit.
- **Main Dwelling.** About 1 metre of fall [TBV]. At 19.5 metres the house is 5.5 metres shorter than Plan 1, which leaves room on the platform for the shared building, Part B, at either end. A good fit.
- **Unit 3.** About 1.8 metres of fall across the house, on 19.5 metres of contour [TBV]. A fair fit.

## Pros

- Both halves get two bedrooms, and both stay the same size.
- 44 m² less slab and roof than Plan 1.
- Each half's living room rises to the roof and shares its light with the mezzanine.
- The ground floor of Half B holds everything its household needs from day to day.
- The ridge stays about 0.3 metres under the height limit, on the uphill side.

## Cons

- A bedroom on a mezzanine is less private than a closed room, for sound and for light.
- Each half needs its own stair, which takes 4.5 m² of ground floor.
- A mezzanine under the roof can overheat in summer. It needs high windows or vents that open at the top of the roof.
- The south walls stand about 6.2 metres high. The approval asks for homes screened from neighbours, the R322, the N2 and the nature reserves within view [IC], and a taller wall is harder to screen.
- The roof catches about 156 litres for each millimetre of rain, against about 200 litres in Plan 1.
- The national building regulations set a minimum height for a room someone sleeps in, and the lowest part of the mezzanine may fall under it [TBV].

## What is still unknown

- Whether a mezzanine counts as a double storey under the Shareholders' Agreement, and so needs every shareholder's approval.
- Whether the municipality counts all of each mezzanine as floor area. The plan counts all of it.
- Whether a bedroom on an open mezzanine meets the national building regulations, and how much of it must be closed off.
- Whether a home of two halves counts as one dwelling. [[the-houses/house-plans|House Plans]] sets out the question.

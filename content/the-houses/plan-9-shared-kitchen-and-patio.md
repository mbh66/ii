## The goal

> Build a home of 200 m² that two households can live in from the first day, around one shared kitchenette, and add a large outdoor kitchen and dining terrace two years later, open to the sky so that it stays outside the 200 m².

## Why the patio can be larger than the house

The Shareholders' Agreement limits each home to 200 m² under roof [MS], and the approval limits each unit's floor area to 200 m² [IC]. Paving open to the sky is neither floor area nor area under roof. So the patio can be as large as the family needs, and this plan draws one of about 190 m² of paving, larger than the house's footprint of 156 m².

The line falls at the roof. An open pergola of timber or steel slats, carrying a vine, keeps the patio open to the sky. Roof sheeting, polycarbonate or a solid cover over any part of the patio would make that part area under roof, and it would then come out of the 200 m². Shade cloth that comes down in winter sits close to the line, and whether it counts has not been asked.

The house is [[the-houses/plan-7-twin-mezzanine-house|Plan 7]], one storey of 19.5 by 8 metres with a mezzanine in each half, and its roofs and heights are the same. What changes is the middle. The two kitchens of Plan 7 come out, and the shared core widens from 2 metres to 4 to hold one shared kitchenette and dining room of 20 m², the laundry and the plant room.

Each half keeps its own front door, a living room of about 21 m², a bedroom and a bathroom on the ground floor, and a second bedroom on its mezzanine. Each half is 84 m²: 62 m² on the ground floor and 22 m² on the mezzanine. The core is 32 m². The two living rooms sit either side of the kitchenette and open into it, each through a door that locks from either side. The bedrooms take the outer ends of the house, where they are quietest.

The kitchenette has the only stove and oven in the house. Each living room could have a counter with a kettle and a small fridge. For the first two years the kitchenette is the household's whole kitchen, so it is drawn large enough to cook for both households and to seat six at a table, with a door north to where the patio will be.

### What Phase 1 lays down for Phase 2

- Water, drainage, power and, if wanted, a gas line, taken under the slab edge to the outdoor kitchen's position and capped.
- The kitchenette's north door and the north doors of both living rooms, set at the level the terraces will meet.
- The fill for the terraces placed and compacted while the machine is on site for the house, or the ground left as it is until Phase 2 if the CMP prefers less disturbance at first.
- A gravel area and a movable braai north of the kitchenette for the first two years.

About two years after the family moves in, the patio is built north of the house, on the sunny side:

- **The outdoor kitchen,** about 32 m², directly outside the kitchenette, with a braai, a wood-fired oven, a prep counter and a sink along the house wall.
- **The dining terrace,** about 72 m², north of the outdoor kitchen, room for a long table for twenty. It sits about 0.45 metres below the house floor, which halves the fill it needs, and a ramp along its east side keeps it step-free from Half B.
- **Two private terraces** of about 23 m² each, outside each half's living room, screened from the shared terraces by planters.
- **A fire circle,** about 37 m², west of the dining terrace.
- **A kitchen garden,** about 36 m² of beds, east of the dining terrace and a few steps from the outdoor kitchen.

An open pergola of about 104 m² covers the outdoor kitchen and the dining terrace. A deciduous vine on it, such as a grape, shades the terraces in summer and lets the sun through in winter. The pergola stands free of the house, with a gap of about 0.6 metres to the eave, so that a fire in the vine cannot reach the roof directly.

A stone seat wall along the north and west edges holds the terraces level. On Unit 1 it would be about 0.9 metres high at its tallest [TBV]. Rain on the paving runs to the kitchen garden and to a swale below the seat wall. The approval asks for sustainable drainage that keeps stormwater clean [IC].

The patio can grow. Extending the terraces east and west along the house would bring the paving to about 300 m², nearly double the footprint.

## How each household would use it

- **House 1.** Michael on the ground floor of one half and his daughter on its mezzanine. Veronica in the other half, with her mezzanine as a room for visitors or for work. Three people sharing one kitchen.
- **House 2.** Robert and his wife in one half. Their mother and her partner in the other, step-free from the parking to their bed, the kitchenette and, in Phase 2, the patio by the ramp. Four adults in two couples sharing one kitchen.

Sharing a kitchen asks more of two households than sharing a laundry. The kitchenette is drawn so that two people can cook at once. Whether both households in each home want to share one is theirs to decide.

## The kitchen through the year

The outdoor kitchen is the main kitchen in good weather. On cold, wet or windy days the kitchenette is the kitchen, in Phase 2 as well as Phase 1. That is why the kitchenette is drawn as a full working kitchen of 20 m², and why the outdoor kitchen sits directly outside it, so that food and people move easily between the two.

## On the three sites

The site figures come from [[the-houses/layout-d-site-development-plan|Layout D]], which modelled each platform from 10-metre contours. They are [TBV] until the platforms have been walked.

- **Unit 1.** The best fit. The ground falls about 1.3 metres from the house's south wall to the north edge of the patio, 18 metres away [TBV], which the 0.45-metre step and a seat wall of about 0.9 metres take up.
- **Main Dwelling.** The ground falls about 2.1 metres over the same distance [TBV]. The dining terrace would need to step down about 0.9 metres, with a longer ramp, and the seat wall would be about 1.2 metres high. A fair fit. The shared building, Part B, could open onto the same patio, which would make it a meeting place for the whole farm.
- **Unit 3.** The ground falls about 4 metres over the same distance [TBV]. The patio would need two or three terraces with retaining walls. A poor fit.

## Pros

- One kitchen in the house. This plan reads most clearly as one dwelling of the nine, because the halves share their only stove and oven.
- Both halves get two bedrooms, and the house stays inside 200 m².
- The largest outdoor living area of any plan, and none of it counts toward the 200 m².
- The cost is split: the house first, the patio two years later, built when the family knows how it uses the land.
- The paving widens the firebreak on the north side of the house.

## Cons

- The halves are no longer fully self-contained. Each household depends on the shared kitchenette.
- For two years, two households cook in one kitchen of 20 m².
- On cold or wet days the patio is out of use, and the indoor living rooms are smaller than in Plan 7.
- A patio of this size disturbs more ground around the house. The approval names a disturbed footprint of about 4,507 m² for the whole development [IC], and nothing may be built in highly sensitive indigenous vegetation [IC].
- An open fire on a farm in fynbos needs firm rules. The fire circle would be used only as the farm's fire management plan allows.
- A vine on a pergola close to the house can carry fire if it dries out. It needs irrigating, and the pergola is better in steel or hardwood.

## What is still unknown

- Whether the CMP and the shareholders would approve a patio of this size, as landscaping and earthworks need the CMP's sign-off [MS], and the design of every home must be acceptable to all shareholders [MS].
- Whether shade cloth over the pergola counts as area under roof.
- Whether the pergola needs building plans from the municipality.
- Whether the patio counts toward the disturbed footprint named in the approval.
- Whether a counter with a kettle and a fridge in each living room would be read as a second kitchen.
- Whether a mezzanine counts as a double storey under the Shareholders' Agreement.
- How the CMP's fire rules would treat a fire circle.

> **This layout is not agreed.** The prospective stewards drew it in October 2026 from the approved Site Development Plan and public contour data. Nobody else has been asked: not the company, its Community Management Panel (CMP) or its Board, and not the residents. It will be put to the company through the CMP, with the other three layouts, after more detailed discussions.

## The goal

> Stay as close as the ground allows to the layout the municipality already approved, and give each home its one-hectare Area around the dwelling the plan shows.

## Why it matters for the land

- The Site Development Plan attached to the Shareholders' Agreement shows where each of the seven dwellings stands [MS]. It marks each dwelling's position and driveway. It does not draw the boundaries of the one-hectare Areas [MS].
- On the plan, the six units are numbered 1 to 6 in its own order, which differs from the numbering on the family's first working map [MS]. This page uses the plan's numbers.
- The ground for Unit 6 has already been broken and levelled [MS].
- The plan's allocation table, dated 31 October 2025, shows Units 3, 4 and 6 as allocated, and the Main Dwelling and Units 1, 2 and 5 as available [MS].
- Each additional unit may have up to 200 m² of floor area [IC], each on an unfenced Area of about one hectare with a 10-metre firebreak [MS], on sandy, rocky ground that a builder has advised against digging into [MS].

## The idea

Each home keeps the place the plan gives it. The dwelling positions were read from the plan by matching its drawing of the farm boundary to the Surveyor General boundary; the match puts the existing cabin within about 15 metres of where the family's map has it [TBV]. Around each dwelling, the same method as the other layouts searched for the best house platform within about 35 metres of the plan's position, then fitted a one-hectare Area around it. Unit 6 was held exactly on the levelled ground.

## Pros

- It follows the plan the municipality approved, so it is the layout least likely to need the plan amended [TBV].
- It keeps Unit 6 on the ground already prepared, and Units 3 and 4 where they were allocated.
- Five of the six homes sit on or beside the knoll top, and no home climbs more than about 21 metres to reach the Shed [TBV].
- The Main Dwelling stands beside the existing cabin and the Community Resources, about a minute's walk from the Shed [TBV].
- The homes form one loose cluster, with the closest two about 108 metres apart [TBV].

## Cons

- Unit 3 stands on the north slope below the knoll. Its platform has about 3.4 metres of rise and fall at a slope of nearly 13°, and only about a third of its Area is gentle ground [TBV]. On rocky ground with little digging, that means fill, retaining or a stepped design.
- Unit 4 has about 2.9 metres of rise and fall across its platform [TBV].
- Units 4 and 5 sit largely outside the Residential Zone drawn on the family's working map, because the plan places them further south-west [TBV]. The working map's zone and the approved plan don't fully agree.
- The Main Dwelling's Area takes in the existing cabin and part of the Production Zone.

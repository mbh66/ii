> **These plans are not agreed.** The Haupt family drew them in October 2026 for discussion among themselves. Nobody outside the family has been asked: not the company, its Community Management Panel (CMP) or its Board, not the residents and not Hessequa Municipality. They will be put to the company through the CMP once the family has agreed what to propose.

## The goal

> Take part of Half A into the roof, so the house stands shorter on the ground and the living room opens to the full height of the roof.

The house is the line of [[the-houses/plan-1-long-house|Plan 1]], shortened by 3.5 metres to 21.5 metres. Half A keeps one bedroom on the ground floor and gains a loft room of 28 m², reached by a stair from its south strip. Half B and the shared core are unchanged.

Half A's roof rises to the south, from an eave about 3.4 metres above the slab at the north wall to about 6.2 metres at the south. The loft sits over the south rooms, under the high side of the roof, and opens north over the living room, which rises to the roof. Its lowest edge has about 1.85 metres of headroom. The loft looks over the living room to the north windows and takes their winter sun.

## How each household would use it

- **House 1.** Michael's daughter in the loft room, Michael in the bedroom below, Veronica in Half B.
- **House 2.** Robert and his wife in the ground-floor bedroom, with the loft as a study or a room for visitors. Their mother and her partner in Half B, all on one level.

## On the three sites

The site figures come from [[the-houses/layout-d-site-development-plan|Layout D]], which modelled each platform from 10-metre contours. They are [TBV] until the platforms have been walked.

- **Unit 1.** About 0.6 metres of fall across the house [TBV], and the high side of the roof on the uphill side. A good fit.
- **Main Dwelling.** About 1 metre of fall [TBV]. The house is 3.5 metres shorter than Plan 1, which leaves room on the platform for the shared building at the east end. A good fit.
- **Unit 3.** About 1.8 metres of fall across the house, on 21.5 metres of contour [TBV]. A fair fit, better than Plan 1 and weaker than Plans 3 and 5.

## Pros

- 28 m² less slab and roof than Plans 1, 2, 3 and 6.
- A living room open to the roof, and a loft that shares its light.
- Half B stays on one level, unchanged.
- The ridge stays about 0.3 metres under the height limit, on the uphill side.

## Cons

- The south wall of Half A stands about 6.2 metres high. The approval asks for homes screened from neighbours, the R322, the N2 and the nature reserves within view [IC], and a taller wall is harder to screen.
- A loft can overheat in summer. It needs high windows or vents at the top of the roof that open.
- The roof catches less rain than in Plan 1.
- Whether a loft counts as a double storey, and so needs every shareholder's approval, has not been asked.

## What is still unknown

- Whether the loft counts as a double storey under the Shareholders' Agreement.
- Whether the municipality counts all of the loft as floor area. The plan counts all of it.
- Whether a home of two halves counts as one dwelling. [[the-houses/house-plans|House Plans]] sets out the question.

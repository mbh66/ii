> **These plans are not agreed.** The Haupt family drew them in October 2026 for discussion among themselves. Nobody outside the family has been asked: not the company, its Community Management Panel (CMP) or its Board, not the residents and not Hessequa Municipality. They will be put to the company through the CMP once the family has agreed what to propose.

This page sets out how the Haupt family's two homes could be planned inside, now that [[the-houses/index|The Houses]] has looked at where homes could stand. Each home would hold two households in two self-contained halves under one roof. Fifteen plans show different ways of arranging the rooms within the 200 m² each home is allowed, and how each plan would sit on three of the farm's sites: Unit 1, Unit 3 and the Main Dwelling.

## The goal

> Give each of two families' households its own front door, kitchen and bathroom inside one home of no more than 200 m² under roof, on rocky ground, without asking more of the approvals than the plan needs.

## What every plan had to meet

- **Floor area.** Each unit may have up to 200 m² of floor area [IC]. The Shareholders' Agreement sets the same figure as area under roof [MS]. The plans count every floor, and every room under the roof, inside the 200 m². The family has asked for no stoeps outside that figure [MS].
- **Height.** Each unit may stand up to 6.5 metres high [IC]. A double storey needs the approval of all shareholders [MS].
- **Size.** The constitution encourages homes of 120 m² and sets 200 m² as the upper limit [MS]. The family has chosen to use the full 200 m² for two households [MS].
- **One home on each Area.** Each shareholder has exclusive use of their Area and may build one home on it [MS]. The farm may have no more than seven units [IC], and the units may not be sold off separately by any means [IC].
- **Design approval.** The design of every home, its colours and materials included, must be acceptable to all shareholders and follow the Site Development Plan and the constitution [MS]. The CMP signs off the design before building plans go to the municipality [MS].
- **Finishes and services.** Walls in natural, earthy colours, and roofs in dark green, dark brown, grey or black [IC]. Rainwater tanks fed from the roofs, water and energy saving fittings, and sewage to septic tanks [IC].
- **Fire.** Each home sits on an unfenced Area of about one hectare with a firebreak of at least 10 metres around it [MS].
- **Ground.** The ground is sandy and very rocky, and the advice received is to avoid digging deep [MS]. The contour model scored a house platform of 25 by 15 metres on each site [MS].
- **Sun.** At the farm's latitude, about 34° south, the noon sun stands about 33° above the horizon on 21 June and about 79° on 21 December [IC]. North-facing windows under a short eave take the winter sun deep into a room and are shaded at midsummer.

## The two homes

The family plans two similar homes, each shared by two households [MS]:

- **House 1.** Michael and his daughter in one half, and Veronica in the other [MS].
- **House 2.** Robert and his wife in one half, and their mother and her partner in the other [MS]. Their half needs to be step-free.

Every plan uses the same two halves:

- **Half A**, about 104 m², with two bedrooms. In House 1, Michael and his daughter have a bedroom each. In House 2, the second bedroom serves Robert and his wife as a study or a room for visitors.
- **Half B**, 80 m², with one bedroom, a large step-free bathroom with a level shower, and no step between the parking, the front door and the bed. Veronica would live in it in House 1, and the family's mother and her partner in House 2.
- **A shared core** of about 16 m², with the laundry and the plant room: pump, inverter, batteries and hot water for both halves.

Keeping the halves the same across Plans 1 to 6 means those plans can be compared on their arrangement alone. Each half can still be fitted out to suit the people in it.

Plans 7 and 8 give both halves two bedrooms. Each half is then 92 m², with the shared core of 16 m², and the second bedroom of each half sits upstairs: on a mezzanine in Plan 7, and on a closed upper floor in Plan 8. Half B keeps its first bedroom, its step-free bathroom and its kitchen on the ground floor. Plan 9 builds on Plan 7 with one shared kitchenette for both halves, and adds a large patio open to the sky in a second phase. Plan 10 does the same for the Sunspace House of Plan 6, with two bedrooms in each half on one level. Plan 11 wraps the two halves and the shared kitchen around a courtyard, under one roof. Plan 12 joins them as an H, after the old Cape farmhouse, and Plan 13 puts them in a single steel-framed barn. Plan 14 tests a round house, and Plan 15 brings together what the others showed.

## Two households under one roof

Each half has its own front door, kitchen and bathroom [MS]. That raises a question the plans cannot settle. The approval allows seven units in all [IC], and the constitution speaks of up to seven households [MS]. Many zoning schemes treat a second kitchen as the mark of a second dwelling [TBV]. Whether Hessequa would treat a home with two self-contained halves as one dwelling or as two has not been asked. Plans 9 to 15, with one shared kitchen each, avoid the question.

The plans are drawn to read as one home:

- One building under one roof, on one slab, in one Area. The family has since made a single roof over both halves a rule for every plan [MS].
- One shared core that both halves open into, with a door from each half.
- One water connection, one septic system, one electricity supply and one set of tanks, all run from the shared plant room.

Whether that is enough is a question for the municipality. Whether two households may share one home is a question for the CMP and the Board.

## How the plans were drawn

- Every area is measured to the outside of the walls, and every plan adds up to 200 m² exactly.
- North is at the top of every plan. The living room and the bedrooms take the north wall wherever the plan allows, and the bathrooms, scullery, stores and passages take the south.
- Roofs rise to the south, so the roof faces north for solar panels and the high side stands on the uphill side of each site. Height is measured from the ground, and every site falls to the north [TBV]. A roof that rose to the north would stand highest over the lowest ground.
- The plans are schematic. They show rooms, doors to the outside and the north windows, and leave out internal doors, windows on other walls and furniture.
- An eave of about 600 millimetres would shade the north windows in summer. Whether eaves count toward the 200 m² under roof is still open.

The Main Dwelling is approved for 350 m² under roof in two connected parts: Part A, a 200 m² home allocated to one shareholder, and Part B, a 150 m² shared facility [MS]. A family home at the Main Dwelling would be Part A, joined to the shared building. [[proposals/homes-in-a-water-retention-landscape|Homes in a Water Retention Landscape]] proposes moving the Main Dwelling to the shelf beside a new pond, on flatter ground about 430 metres from the Shed [TBV]. The plans that fit the Main Dwelling would fit there too.

Unit 3 is allocated [MS]. It appears here because the family asked to see what its slope would mean for a house.

## Which home could go where

House 2 has the stronger reason to sit at the Main Dwelling. Its step-free half would be about a minute's walk from the Shed and beside the shared building [TBV], where the daily walk matters most. House 1 would then sit at Unit 1, the flatter of the two units, about ten minutes from the Shed [TBV]. Plans 1, 2, 4 and 6 suit both sites, so the two homes could use the same plan, as the family intends, or different ones. Plan 7 also suits both sites, for a home where both households want two bedrooms. Plan 8 suits Unit 1, and fits the Main Dwelling only if the height can be kept under the limit on its slope.

## Building on rock

Robert prefers a concrete slab and is open to any building method that suits the ground [MS]. Veronica's first choice is an earthship, and she understands the limits the rocky ground sets [MS].

Every plan here assumes a slab built up from the ground: a low perimeter wall of site stone or concrete blocks bedded on the rock, filled with compacted rubble from the site, and a concrete slab cast on top. The slab then follows the ground's highest point with fill, and avoids cutting into rock. On Unit 1 that wall would be about 0.6 metres high at its north edge, and on Unit 3 about 1.8 metres [TBV]. Above the slab, the walls could be site stone, stabilised earth blocks, rammed earth, or a timber frame with straw bale infill. The heavy materials suit the south walls, and the light ones the north walls, which carry most of the glass. [[the-houses/plan-6-sunspace-house|Plan 6]] carries the most of the earthship into a slab-built house.

## Who would decide

- **The design** must be acceptable to all shareholders, and the CMP signs it off against the community's guidelines and the Site Development Plan [MS]. A double storey needs every shareholder's approval [MS].
- **Two households in one home** is a community matter for the CMP, which advises the Board [MS].
- **Which home goes on which Area** turns on the allocation table, and changing the Areas needs every founding shareholder to agree [MS].
- **Hessequa Municipality** would decide whether a home of two halves is one dwelling, how height is measured on a slope, and whether the loft counts as floor area, when it approves the building plans.

## What is still unknown

- Whether Hessequa would treat a home with two self-contained halves as one dwelling.
- Whether the CMP and the Board would accept two households sharing one home.
- Whether eaves count toward the 200 m² under roof, and whether the 200 m² counts every floor.
- Whether a loft or a mezzanine counts as a double storey under the Shareholders' Agreement.
- Whether a bedroom on an open mezzanine meets the national building regulations.
- Whether shade cloth over an open pergola counts as area under roof, and whether a large patio needs the CMP's sign-off as landscaping.
- How the municipality measures height on sloping ground.
- Which Areas the family could be allocated. The Main Dwelling and Unit 1 are available on the allocation table of 31 October 2025, and Unit 3 is allocated [MS].
- How firm the rock is on each platform, and how deep the sand lies over it.
- Whether each household's needs have been read correctly. Each steward reads and approves the plans before they go further.

## What happens next

1. The family chooses two or three plans to take forward and agrees which home goes with which site.
2. The conversation with the company, through the CMP, about two households sharing one home, and about the Areas the family would ask for.
3. An informal question to Hessequa Municipality, through a registered architect or draughtsperson, on the one-dwelling question, height on a slope and the loft.
4. A walk over the chosen platforms with a probe and a level, to find the rock and set the slab heights.
5. Design drawings for the chosen plan, for the CMP's sign-off.

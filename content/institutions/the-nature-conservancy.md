The Nature Conservancy is a non-profit conservation organisation, founded in the United States in 1951 and based in Arlington, Virginia [IC]. In South Africa it set up the [[institutions/greater-cape-town-water-fund|Greater Cape Town Water Fund]] after the drought of 2018, to clear invasive trees from the catchments that fill Cape Town's dams [IC]. It is the implementation agent for the [[the-land/cape-water-bond|Cape Water Performance-Based Bond]], listed in April 2026 [IC]. The sources read for this site record no work by The Nature Conservancy in the Duiwenhoks catchment [MS].

The Nature Conservancy has not yet been asked to comment on this page. The key to the letters in square brackets is on [[about|About This Site]].

## What The Nature Conservancy does

The organisation was incorporated as a non-profit on 22 October 1951 [IC]. It grew out of a committee of the Ecological Society of America, and from 1970 it built its work on scientific inventories of natural diversity [TBV]. It works in 79 countries and territories, and says it has protected more than 48 million hectares over its history [TBV].

## The Greater Cape Town Water Fund

The Fund has its own page, [[institutions/greater-cape-town-water-fund|Greater Cape Town Water Fund]]. This section covers The Nature Conservancy's part in it.

| Date | Event | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| 2018 | Cape Town's dams come close to "Day Zero"; The Nature Conservancy sets up the Greater Cape Town Water Fund with the City of Cape Town and other partners | [IC] |
| 2018 | The Fund's business case projects that investing US$25.5 million in critical sub-catchments would add about 100 billion litres of water a year within 30 years, half of it within five | [TBV] |
| April 2023 | The Fund is registered as a separate legal entity | [TBV] |
| 2025 and 2026 | The Fund works with CapeNature, fire services and others to bring controlled burns into catchment restoration | [TBV] |
| April 2026 | The Cape Water Performance-Based Bond is listed, with The Nature Conservancy as implementation agent and about R150 million for clearing over five years | [IC] |

The Fund clears invasive pines and eucalyptus by hand, using local crews, some of whom work on ropes on steep slopes, and returns to cleared land to stop regrowth [TBV]. By April 2026 it had cleared about 40,000 hectares [IC]. The Nature Conservancy says the clearing has restored more than 36 billion litres of water a year to the streams that feed the region's dams, at about a tenth of the unit cost of desalination or new groundwater [TBV].

The Nature Conservancy describes the bond as a blueprint for restoring priority catchments across South Africa's strategic water source areas [IC].

## In this district

No work by The Nature Conservancy or the Greater Cape Town Water Fund in the Duiwenhoks catchment or the eastern Langeberg has been found in the sources read for this site [MS]. The Duiwenhoks catchment lies outside the Western Cape Water Supply System, which the Fund serves [MS]. [[the-land/cape-water-bond|The Cape Water Bond and Clearing Around the Farm]] sets out what links the Fund's work to the clearing in [[the-land/boosmansbos-wilderness-area|Boosmansbos]].

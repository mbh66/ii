Overberg Water is a water board: a state-owned body that supplies bulk water [IC]. It serves an area of more than 6,000 square kilometres across the Overberg and Garden Route districts, from the south-western Cape to the Heidelberg and Riversdale districts [IC]. Its Duivenhoks scheme takes water from the Duiwenhoks River below the [[the-land/duiwenhoks-river-and-dam|Duiwenhoks Dam]] [IC]. The municipality's 2024 approval for additional homes on the farm requires Overberg Water's comment on the farm's water-sharing arrangement [IC].

Overberg Water has not yet been asked to comment on this page. The key to the letters in square brackets is on [[about|About This Site]].

## What the board does

Overberg Water was formed in 1993 by merging the Duivenhoks and Ruensveld water boards [IC]. Its mandate comes from the Water Services Act of 1997 and the Public Finance Management Act of 1999 [IC]. It is wholly owned by the state, and the national Minister of Water and Sanitation is its executive authority [IC]. Its offices are in Heidelberg [TBV].

Its pipe network runs to about 1,450 km, and it supplies about 800 farmers across its area [IC].

In November 2022 the Minister gave notice of an intention to extend Overberg Water's service area to the whole Western Cape [IC]. A presentation to Parliament in October 2023 said the board would keep its name and take responsibility for the whole province [IC]. This page has not found whether the extension has been completed [MS].

## The Duivenhoks scheme

| Item | Detail | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Source | The Duiwenhoks Dam, built by the national water department between 1962 and 1965 | [TBV] |
| Dam capacity | 6.2 million cubic metres | [IC] |
| How water is taken | By pump station from the river below the dam | [IC] |
| Registered abstraction | 1,232,000 cubic metres a year | [IC] |
| Future need | 2,086,920 cubic metres a year | [IC] |
| Who it supplies | Heidelberg, Slangrivier and Witsand, and rural households, livestock and irrigation | [IC] |
| Hessequa Municipality's offtake | Nearly 2 million litres a day for its towns | [IC] |

In 2018 a local newspaper reported that Overberg Water allowed Heidelberg, Slangrivier and Witsand 2,200 cubic metres a day between them [TBV]. Overberg Water's 2021/22 annual report estimates that the old irrigation canal from the dam loses 30 to 50 percent of its flow [IC]. It describes a proposed gravity pipeline from the dam to Heidelberg, costed at R195 million, and says the board means to help fund it [IC].

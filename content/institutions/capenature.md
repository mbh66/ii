CapeNature is the name under which the Western Cape Nature Conservation Board works [IC]. It is the provincial public entity responsible for conserving biodiversity in the Western Cape [IC]. Near the farm it manages the [[the-land/boosmansbos-wilderness-area|Boosmansbos Wilderness Area]], [[the-land/grootvadersbosch-nature-reserve|Grootvadersbosch Nature Reserve]] and the [[the-land/wilderness-nature-reserve|Wilderness Nature Reserve]] [IC]. The municipality's 2024 approval for additional homes on the farm links the farm's environmental management plan to a stewardship agreement with CapeNature [IC].

CapeNature has not yet been asked to comment on this page. The key to the letters in square brackets is on [[about|About This Site]].

## What CapeNature does

The Board was set up under the Western Cape Nature Conservation Board Act 15 of 1998 [IC]. Wikipedia gives its formation date as 15 January 1999 [TBV]. It is now constituted under the Western Cape Biodiversity Act 6 of 2021 [IC]. It is a Schedule 3C public entity, and the Provincial Minister responsible for environmental affairs appoints its Board [IC]. Its mandate is to conserve biodiversity in the province, to provide research and training services and facilities, and to generate income [IC].

CapeNature began its Biodiversity Stewardship programme in 2003 [IC]. The programme makes agreements with private landowners, on whose land most of the province's biodiversity lies [IC]. It offers four options [IC]:

- **Contract nature reserves.** Legally recognised contracts or servitudes that protect biodiversity on private land over the long term.
- **Biodiversity agreements.** Negotiated legal agreements to conserve biodiversity over the medium term.
- **Protected environments.** Formal protected areas, usually set up by groups of landowners, often to buffer existing reserves.
- **Conservation areas.** Flexible arrangements with no fixed commitment period, conservancies among them.

CapeNature says landowners in the programme receive advice, management plans, help with planning alien plant clearing and fire management, and access to its staff [IC].

The 2024 approval requires the owner to compile an environmental conservation management plan before building plans are approved [IC]. The plan must include fire management and the control of invasive alien plants, address a botanical assessment of the farm dated 8 November 2021, and be linked to a stewardship agreement with CapeNature [IC]. The Tribunal required the stewardship agreement because part of the planned footprint lies in an area of medium-high botanical sensitivity [IC]. [[documents/municipal-approval-2024|Municipal Approval for Additional Homes (2024)]] summarises the decision. This site does not know whether the plan or the agreement is in place [MS].

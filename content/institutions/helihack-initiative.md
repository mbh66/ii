The HeliHack Initiative clears invasive trees from steep, remote mountain catchments in the Western Cape [IC]. A helicopter lowers volunteer climbers, who also work as chainsaw operators, and their equipment on a strop beneath it to places that cannot be reached on foot [IC]. Near the farm, HeliHack teams working with CapeNature have felled invasive pines in the [[the-land/boosmansbos-wilderness-area|Boosmansbos Wilderness Area]], on the slopes above the [[the-land/duiwenhoks-river-and-dam|Duiwenhoks River]] and its dam [IC].

The HeliHack Initiative and the Mountain Club of South Africa have not yet been asked to comment on this page. The page names none of the volunteers. The key to the letters in square brackets is on [[about|About This Site]].

## What HeliHack does

The project began in 2014 [TBV]. For its first two and a half years it shot biocontrol pellets, carrying the spores of a fungus that attacks silky hakea, into stands of that invasive shrub [TBV]. In 2017 it turned to felling cluster pines with teams of volunteer climbers and chainsaws, set down by helicopter [TBV]. Its first sites were in the Boland and Groot Winterhoek mountains, including CapeNature's Ceres Mountain Fynbos Reserve [TBV].

An operation runs over a weekend. Teams and equipment are flown in on the Friday, work through Saturday and are lifted out on the Sunday [TBV]. The volunteers are rock climbers and chainsaw operators, many of them also in mountain rescue, and they pay their own way [TBV]. The pilots are paid [TBV]. A 2021 account put the cost of a weekend at about R350,000, and donors had given close to R5 million by 2023 [TBV].

The Mountain Club of South Africa presented the project as its own when it nominated it for the 2021 Mountain Protection Award of the International Climbing and Mountaineering Federation [TBV]. The club bought the project's chainsaws [TBV]. CapeNature formalised its commitment to the HeliHack Initiative in 2024 [IC], and in 2025 said the initiative was extending its work to four more provincial nature reserves [IC].

## In this district

| Date | Event | Tag |

| April 2023 | HeliHack teams, CapeNature, the Grootvadersbosch Conservancy, landowners and a chainsaw maker clear invasive pines from the upper catchment of the Duiwenhoks Dam | [TBV] |
| 2024 | CapeNature formalises its commitment to HeliHack | [IC] |
| 3 to 5 May 2025 | 21 volunteers remove 15,194 pines from 1,080 hectares on the steep western slopes above the Duiwenhoks River, with 12.8 hours of helicopter time | [IC] |
| By May 2025 | HeliHack operations in Boosmansbos have removed 26,913 pines from 3,880 hectares, about 27 percent of the wilderness area | [IC] |

CapeNature described the May 2025 operation as the most successful pine removal in the initiative's history [IC]. The same report recorded the rediscovery in Boosmansbos of a long-tailed forest shrew, a Critically Endangered species not seen there for 46 years [TBV].

[[the-land/cape-water-bond|The Cape Water Bond and Clearing Around the Farm]] sets out how this clearing relates to the Cape Water Performance-Based Bond, which pays for clearing of the same kind in the catchments that supply Cape Town.

## What is still open

- Whether HeliHack is now a separate organisation or remains a project of the Mountain Club of South Africa.
- Who pays for the operations in Boosmansbos, and who pays for follow-up clearing of seedlings after the felling.
- Whether HeliHack's counts of pines and hectares are checked by anyone outside the project.
- Whether HeliHack plans further operations above the Duiwenhoks Dam, or in the [[the-land/wilderness-nature-reserve|Wilderness Nature Reserve]].

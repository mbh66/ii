The Greater Cape Town Water Fund is a partnership that clears invasive trees from the mountain catchments that supply the Western Cape Water Supply System, the network of dams serving Cape Town and the towns and farms around it [IC]. [[institutions/the-nature-conservancy|The Nature Conservancy]] set it up in 2018 with the City of Cape Town and other partners, during the drought that brought the city close to "Day Zero" [IC]. Its work is now the clearing paid for by the [[the-land/cape-water-bond|Cape Water Performance-Based Bond]] [IC]. It has no work recorded in the Duiwenhoks catchment [MS].

The Fund has not yet been asked to comment on this page. The page names none of its staff. The key to the letters in square brackets is on [[about|About This Site]].

## The business case

The Fund began from a business case published by The Nature Conservancy in 2018 [IC]. Its main findings were these.

| Finding | Detail | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Where to work | Seven priority sub-catchments, chosen from 25, covering 54,300 hectares and feeding the Wemmershoek, Theewaterskloof and Berg River dams | [IC] |
| How invaded | Invasive plants covered 69 percent of the 25 sub-catchments; about 14,400 hectares were densely invaded | [TBV] |
| Water lost | About 55 billion litres a year, roughly two months of Cape Town's supply, with losses set to more than double within 30 years without clearing | [IC] |
| Cost | R372 million in present value, about US$25.5 million, over 30 years | [IC] |
| Water gained | 55 billion litres a year within six years, rising to about 100 billion litres a year by 2045 | [IC] |
| Unit cost | About a tenth of the cost per kilolitre of desalination, water reuse, groundwater or a larger Voëlvlei Dam | [IC] |
| Jobs | About 350 in the first six years | [TBV] |

The business case also counted the Atlantis Aquifer north of Cape Town, where invasive plants were estimated to cost about 1.8 billion litres a year [TBV]. It warned that cleared land must be kept clear for the water gains to last, and that burned land needs prompt follow-up so that regrowth does not come back denser [IC].

## How it is run and measured

At first a steering committee guided the Fund [IC]. Its members included the national departments of water and environment, the Western Cape Government, CapeNature, the City of Cape Town, the South African National Biodiversity Institute, WWF South Africa and companies that use the water, among them Coca-Cola Peninsula Beverages, Nedbank and Remgro [IC]. In April 2023 the Fund was registered as an independent entity, to work across the Western Cape Water Supply System beyond Cape Town's own catchments [TBV].

Partners report their field work each month into an online decision support system that covers 87 hydrological management units [TBV]. Data loggers at six remote stream sites record flow for paired-catchment comparisons [TBV]. The Nature Conservancy has said early stream-flow results are encouraging without publishing figures in the sources read for this page [MS].

Clearing teams work by hand, some on ropes on steep slopes [TBV]. The Fund has trained and supported local crews and small businesses, including several that provide high-altitude rope access [TBV].

## What it reports

| Date | Report | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| 2019 | Clearing begins | [TBV] |
| August 2020 | 10,000 hectares cleared across the seven catchments and the Atlantis Aquifer; 100 jobs, including 40 rope technicians; a sustained gain of 4.8 billion litres a year | [TBV] |
| July 2021 | The City of Cape Town commits R125 million to clear the catchments of other city dams | [TBV] |
| 2023 | More than 46,000 hectares cleared | [TBV] |
| October 2024 | More than 17 billion litres a year recovered; over 1,000 jobs created, nearly half held by women | [TBV] |
| April 2026 | About 40,000 hectares cleared; more than 36 billion litres a year restored to the streams that feed the dams | [TBV] |
| April 2026 | The Cape Water Performance-Based Bond is listed, with The Nature Conservancy as implementation agent and the Fund coordinating the work | [IC] |

The figures for hectares cleared do not rise steadily between reports, and the sources do not say whether they count first clearing, follow-up clearing or both [MS]. The water figures come from The Nature Conservancy and the Fund, and the sources read for this page do not say whether they are measured or modelled [MS].

## In this district

The Fund works in the catchments of the Western Cape Water Supply System [IC]. The Duiwenhoks catchment lies outside that system [MS], and no work by the Fund near the farm has been found [MS].

Its model has parts that exist in some form around the Duiwenhoks. The Duiwenhoks Dam's users are known: [[institutions/overberg-water|Overberg Water]], [[institutions/hessequa-municipality|Hessequa Municipality]] and the [[institutions/duiwenhoks-irrigation-board|irrigation board]] [IC]. [[institutions/capenature|CapeNature]], the [[institutions/helihack-initiative|HeliHack Initiative]] and the [[institutions/grootvadersbosch-conservancy|Grootvadersbosch Conservancy]] already clear pines above the dam and keep counts of what they remove [IC]. A business case for the Duiwenhoks, comparing the cost of clearing with the cost of other ways to supply Heidelberg, Slangrivier and Witsand, has not been found [MS]. Nor has a shared record of clearing or a stream gauge above the farm [MS]. Those are the pieces a fund of this kind for the Langeberg could start from. [[the-land/cape-water-bond|The Cape Water Bond and Clearing Around the Farm]] sets out the same gap from the side of the bond.

| When gains arrive | The business case gives about 100 billion litres a year by 2045 in one summary and by 2050 in another. | By 2045, the date in the full business case summary [IC]. |

## What is still open

- Whether the Fund's water figures are measured at stream gauges or modelled from cleared hectares.
- What its legal form is today, and who sits on its board.
- Whether it plans work in any catchment outside the Western Cape Water Supply System.
- Whether its decision support system or methods are open to other catchments.
